# Threat model

This document lists what Noised protects, who attacks it, how, what stops each attack, and what remains. It is written for auditors and engineers. Notation in attack trees:

- **[M]** mitigation, with the code or instruction that enforces it
- **[R]** residual risk that remains after mitigation
- **[X]** attack is impossible by construction

Instruction names refer to [SPEC.md](SPEC.md). Guarantee IDs (T1, L1, A1, M1, …) refer to [SECURITY_GUARANTEES.md](SECURITY_GUARANTEES.md). Sourced facts are cited as "FACTS #n" or by follow-up section ([research/FACTS.md](research/FACTS.md)). State of third-party programs is as of 2026-10-08. This revision describes the final programs (2026-10-09): audit round 1 (vault v4, snipe shield by instruction introspection, pro-rata batch oversubscription, claim ATA validation), round 1b (zero-amount buys refused during the shield, `execute_batch` only as the pool's first swap, deterministic HMAC randomizer), PQ v2 with the round-2 fixes (post-quantum launch attestation, permissionless launch inside a signed window, creator-seeded `Launch` PDA, payout owners and payout vaults, the vault treasury, minute-long commit windows, the 10-minute execution window ended by the batch, batch-cap floors, the vault's key-index binding) and on-chain metadata (`TokenMeta`, message v2, `launch()` without arguments); see [AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md). Audit round 3 (2026-10-10, AUDIT.md §14) added 5.1.12, 5.2.11 and 5.2.12, R28 to R32, and corrected 5.2.5 and 5.2.10; its two program fixes, F1 (launchpad) and F2 (hook), are merged and described here as the code behaves. P1c, programmable launches, and review 4 (2026-10-11, AUDIT.md §15) added creator launch rules (PXR1, evaluated by the hook, signed in attestation v3, bound by certificate v4 and `RULES_BOUND`), `pq_authorize`, the `noised_autopilot` program, and review 4's fixes (R1 holders' exits skip creator rules, R3 the policy-head fast path, Ra, Rb, R5, R6a, R6b; fix IDs of AUDIT.md, not the register IDs of section 6). They added A11, A12, two actors, 5.15, 5.16 and R33 to R41, extended R21, and updated 5.1.3, 5.1.5, 5.1.7, 5.2.5, 5.5, 5.6, 5.7, 5.8 and 5.13. Audit round 4 (2026-10-11, AUDIT.md §16) found no exploitable issue in the launch path, the vault, the Shield or `noised-lmots`; its merged autopilot fixes (partial keeper buybacks, revocations that name their activation, no tips for burns before graduation, distributions that skip recipients without an account) and its client findings (a dishonest RPC steering a second device onto a withheld signature's leaf, RPC-clock-signed times, the pending-signature race; client fixes done in SDK 0.9.0 and the app) updated 5.13, 5.16, R24, R32, R36 and R39 and added R42 and R43.

## 1. Scope and assumptions

**In scope:**
- `noised_launchpad`, `noised_hook`, `noised_vault`, `noised_shield`, `noised_autopilot`
- `crates/noised-lmots` (LM-OTS and LMS)
- `crates/noised-rules` (PXR1 launch rules, PXG1 autopilot graphs, the condition VM)
- the SDK's transaction builders, verifier, PQ key derivation, identity and vault ledgers, rule and graph builders, keepers
- the frontend
- the deployment and finalization process

**Assumptions** (if one fails, the analysis below fails with it):
1. SHA-256 behaves as a random function (preimage and second-preimage resistance), so LM-OTS and LMS are existentially unforgeable as long as each one-time key signs at most one message. Ed25519 is secure against classical attackers.
2. The Solana runtime executes programs as specified. In particular, PDAs cannot be signed for except by their program, Token-2022 strips signer privileges from hook accounts (FACTS #51), indirect reentrancy is rejected (FACTS #4), the instructions sysvar lists exactly the transaction's top-level instructions, with the current index pointing at the top-level instruction being executed (FACTS, audit round 1 facts), and blocks are packed under Agave's cost model (FACTS, PQ v2 facts).
3. Meteora DBC 0.2.1, DAMM v2 0.2.4 and Token-2022 v11.0.0 behave as their audited source does. Where they can be upgraded, that is listed as a residual risk, not assumed away.
4. Our programs are finalized after audit. Before that, the upgrade authority is a trusted party (section 5.9).

## 3. Actors

| Actor | Capabilities assumed |
|---|---|
| **Creator** | Chooses `LaunchParams`, the metadata record and, optionally, launch rules and autopilot graphs. Pays for and signs `create_launch` with its wallet; signs the attestation and autopilot authorizations with its post-quantum identity. Can trade, commit, create other pools, run bots and fund many wallets. Knows the launch window in advance and can put other instructions in its own `launch` transaction, like anyone else. |
| **Insiders, snipers, bundlers** | Many wallets, fast infrastructure, Jito bundles, simulation. They can buy in any transaction that can legally exist, and buy priority to fill an account's per-block write budget. |
| **MEV searchers, block producers, ordering gateways** | Reorder, insert or delay transactions within a block. Censor transactions for a short time. A leader controls the transactions in its own slots. Third-party TPU gateways (BAM, Harmonic) front about 61.7% of leader slots and see transactions before they land (FACTS, follow-up on V1 wallets and TPU). |
| **Relayers** | Anyone who sees a signed attestation, autopilot authorization (`pq_authorize`), withdrawal or commit before it lands (RPC providers, leaders, anyone reading failed transactions): can land it, hold it back or copy its bytes into another transaction with other accounts. |
| **Autopilot operator** (for example an AI agent) | An Ed25519 key named in the active graph. Alone runs the graph's operator-gated nodes, choosing only their timing (within the trigger) and amount (1 up to the node's limit). Cannot widen any bound. Can stop running. The launch's identity can revoke it, even on a frozen graph. |
| **Keepers** | Anyone calling the autopilot's permissionless instructions (`run` for non-gated nodes, `update_price`, `apply_pending`, and `activate`, `freeze` or `revoke_operator` with a landed authorization) for a bounded tip. Choose when to call and, for a buyback, an amount between the smaller of 1% of the pool's wSOL and the limit, and the limit. Can trade around their own run, or not show up. |
| **Quantum attacker (CRQC)** | Derives any Ed25519 private key from its public key, which is every wallet address (FACTS #58). Forges any Ed25519 signature, the creator's `create_launch` and fee-payer signatures included. Decrypts recorded ElGamal ciphertexts and classical channels recorded today. Gets at most a square-root (Grover) speed-up against hash functions. |
| **Meteora operators and upgrade multisig** | Upgrade DBC and DAMM v2 (4-of-7, no timelock). DAMM v2 operators disable pools, change fees and close configs (FACTS #19, #36). A future DBC release could add an operator hook revoke (draft PR #214). |
| **Token-2022 maintainers, validators** | Upgrade Token-2022 (4-of-6). Activate feature gates that change the runtime (FACTS #2, #47, #53). Run consensus with classical keys. |
| **Frontend and supply-chain attacker** | Controls a dependency, the build machine, DNS, the hosting or an RPC endpoint (FACTS #67). |
| **Noised team (us)** | Holds the upgrade authority until finalization. Sets the treasury (a wSOL PQ vault) and platform fee once. Ships the frontend and SDK. Runs keepers. |
| **Any user / griefer** | Calls every permissionless instruction with arbitrary accounts and amounts. Publishes metadata records, rules records and autopilot graphs under any launch. Donates tokens to any account. Front-runs public transactions. Controls the state of its own token accounts (owner field, memo requirement, confidential-credit settings, closing). |

## 4. Trust boundaries

```
 [user device: wallet, PQ phrase / passkey, SDK, leaf and vault-key ledgers]  --tx-->  [RPC / gateway]  -->  [leader / validators]
                                                                               |
          +--------------------------------------------------------------------+-------------------------+
          | our programs (immutable after finalization)                        | third-party programs    |
          |  launchpad -> hook (policy, end of shield)                         |  Meteora DBC, DAMM v2   |
          |  launchpad: LMS verification (noised-lmots, sol_sha256)           |                         |
          |  launchpad/vault/shield -> token programs ------------------------>|  Token-2022, SPL Token  |
          |  vault -> SPL Memo, ATA program                                    |  SPL Memo, ATA program  |

          |  autopilot: reads launchpad accounts, never writes them            |                         |
          +--------------------------------------------------------------------+-------------------------+
```

Every arrow into a third-party program crosses a trust boundary we cannot harden. Each crossing is listed in section 5.7.

## 5. Attack trees

### 5.1 Creator extracts value from holders ("rug")

1. **Mint new supply and sell it**
   - [X] The mint authority is None and Token-2022 cannot restore it (T1). Hook configs that hand out mint authority (`token_update_authority` 3/4, FACTS #24) are impossible: the value is fixed to 1 by `dbc_params.rs` and re-checked by `checks.rs::inspect_config`.
2. **Remove liquidity**
   1. Withdraw LP after graduation.
      - [X] 100% of LP is permanently locked and owned by the launch-authority PDA, and no instruction removes it (P1, P3).
   2. Keep part of the raise out of the pool through the migration fee.
      - [X] The creator and partner migration fees are 0/0 (P2, `inspect_config`). [R] Meteora's own fixed 0.2% protocol migration fee and its 20% of any surplus apply to every DBC pool.
   3. Seed one-sided pools elsewhere with supply (the LIBRA pattern, FACTS #65).
      - [M] The creator holds only tokens it bought, in the batch or later on the curve, like everyone else. There is no allocation, and unsold supply is burned at graduation (L5, T6).
      - [R] Like anyone, the creator can open other pools with tokens it bought. These pools cannot touch the canonical pool's locked liquidity, but traders who use them take that venue's risk.
   4. Drain DBC vaults.
      - [X] Not possible in DBC 0.2.1. The vaults belong to DBC's pool authority.
      - [R] A DBC upgrade could change that (section 5.7).
3. **Honeypot: stop holders from selling**
   1. Make the hook refuse sells.
      - [X] The first rule allows every transfer into the DBC base vault from any other account (L7, `rules::evaluate`, 200k-case property test). The policy is written once by the launchpad and the creator cannot touch it.
      - [X] Since review 4 (fix R3), sells and everything after the guard are decided from the policy's fixed head (discriminator, `mint`, `base_vault`, `guard_end_ts`) before the rest is decoded, so nothing stored after the head, the creator rules included, and no later policy layout can block a sell (CR2; `sells_and_the_guard_end_never_decode_the_whole_policy`).
   2. Freeze, pause, default-frozen accounts or a permanent delegate.
      - [X] None of these exists on a certified mint (T2, T4).
   3. A prohibitive trading fee.
      - [M] The fee schedule must end before the batch executes (`FeeScheduleTooLong`). Afterwards, the floor fee plus the maximum volatility fee is at most 10% (`policy.rs::within_fee_ceiling`, F5).
   4. A permanent wallet-to-wallet lock.
      - [X] The lock ends at `guard_end_ts`, at most 72 h after launch (L6), and it never applies to sells.
   5. Repoint or replace the hook.
      - [X] The hook authority is DBC's pool authority, checked in `inspect_mint` (T5). The creator has no authority over it.
   6. Write creator launch rules that refuse sells.
      - [X] Creator rules run after every built-in allow and can only refuse, only before `guard_end_ts` (CR1). They never see a sell, a claim, the batch buy, a zero-amount or exempt transfer, or anything after the guard (CR2; `a_rule_that_refuses_everything_spares_sells_claims_and_the_batch`, `rules_never_touch_sells_claims_the_batch_or_after_the_guard`). See 5.15.
4. **Rename or re-image the token to impersonate another project**
   - [X] The metadata and pointer authorities are None (T3).
   - [X] The name, symbol and document (with its icon) are an on-chain record at the address its content hashes to, never modified or closed. The creator's post-quantum identity signs its hash, and `launch` certifies that the mint holds exactly the record's name and symbol and the uri derived from it (T3, M1, M2, certificate flag 2048). A record published under the launch by anyone else is never used, and whoever relays the creator's attestation cannot name another record (A5).
   - [R] Only with the opt-in external uri: the content behind the link can change. The on-chain record stays the canonical metadata.
5. **Insider allocation or self-sniping**
   - [X] There is no allocation or vesting (L5). Nobody can buy before the batch (L1): until the batch ends the shield, the hook refuses every buy that is not made inside a top-level `execute_batch`, of any amount, and exempt owners do not open the shield. The batch is pro-rata at one price (L2).
   - [X] Filling the batch first, even with a commit inside the launch transaction, gains nothing: the total cap never refuses a commit, and an oversubscribed batch swaps at most the cap and refunds everyone the same fraction (L2). Before audit round 1 the cap was first-come-first-served, so the creator could fill it atomically with `launch`.
   - [R] The creator can commit like anyone, with as much capital and as many wallets as it likes, and gets a share proportional to what it commits (`l1_creator_overcommitting_in_the_launch_transaction_gets_exactly_its_pro_rata_share`: 80% of the commits buy 80% of the batch, at the batch price).
   - [R] Exclusion instead of speed: a creator (or anyone) that fills the `Launch` account's per-block write budget keeps other commits out while it pays (5.2.9).
   - [R] R33: the creator's launch rules can refuse every public buy except into listed wallets, from the batch until `guard_end_ts` (at most 72 h). Disclosed, not prevented (5.15).
6. **Redirect fees**
   - [X] Recipients and their bps are fixed at `create_launch` (F1). Only the launch-authority PDA can claim DBC fees. The platform share goes to the treasury, a wSOL PQ vault (F2).
   - [M] `claim_lp_fees` accepts only a position owned by the launch authority in the DAMM v2 pool of (base mint, wSOL).
7. **Change the rules after launch**
   - [X] No instruction changes a launch's recipients, bps, caps or timing parameters, its hook policy or its DBC config after creation (G1). The one later change is `execute_batch` ending the shield early, which only moves it earlier (G4). Later instructions only advance the timeline and batch totals. DBC configs are immutable (FACTS #21).
   - [X] The creator's launch rules are the bytes its identity signed: their hash is in the attestation message (v3), `launch` installs the record's bytes byte-exact in the policy (re-hashed, then re-read from the written policy) and the certificate records the hash (CR4). Nothing changes them afterwards.
   - [X] An autopilot graph changes only through an LMS authorization by the launch's identity; a replacement takes effect at least 72 h after it becomes visible, and a frozen graph never changes (AP3, 5.16).
8. **Abandon a configured launch** (`create_launch` without an attestation or `launch`)
   - [M] Harmless: no pool, no token and no user funds exist yet. Its metadata records, if any, stay on-chain unused.
9. **Inflate the fee the batch pays** (fees go to the creator's recipients)
   - [M] The base-fee schedule must end before the batch (`FeeScheduleTooLong`), so the batch pays the floor base fee.
   - [X] No buy can precede the batch, so DBC's volatility fee has nothing to accumulate from: until the shield ends the hook refuses every transfer out of the base vault that is not inside a top-level `execute_batch`, whatever its amount (L1). Two earlier versions let a buy through. Before audit round 1, a small buy into an exempt account during the shield saturated the volatility fee. Round 1 still let zero-amount transfers pass before the shield check, and DBC 0.2.1 executes a buy whose output rounds to zero; it still moves the price and the accumulator. On a creator curve whose first segment held dust liquidity, a 2-lamport buy made a 4 SOL batch pay 395,666,224 lamports of fees instead of 40,000,000 (the round-1b review). `l2b_zero_output_buys_cannot_cross_the_snipe_shield` replays that buy against mainnet DBC: it now fails with `SnipeShield`, and the batch pays exactly the floor fee.
   - [M] Second layer: `execute_batch` requires the pool's `has_swap == 0` (`PoolAlreadyTraded`, `l2c_batch_must_be_the_first_swap`). A buy that ever got past the hook would make the batch refund in full; it could never make the batch pay a moved price or fee.
   - [R] R16: the hook layer depends on DBC making, and Token-2022 hooking, a transfer for every buy.
10. **Neuter the fair open with tiny caps**
    - [X] A non-zero `batch_total_cap` is at least a tenth of the threshold, and a non-zero `batch_wallet_cap` at least the resolved total cap / 200 (`policy.rs::validate_batch_caps`, round 2 finding C0; `r2_batch_cap_floors_and_minute_windows_hold_at_create_launch`).
    - [M] A batch too small to buy anything still cannot execute and refunds in full (`l1_a_batch_too_small_to_buy_anything_refunds_in_full`).
11. **Open the launch at a moment the creator chooses** (for example when only its own bots are ready)
    - [X] `launch` runs only inside the window its identity signed, 60 s to 1 h long, and inside it anyone can open it (A4). The creator chooses the window in advance, in public, when it signs.
    - [R] Inside the window, the first `launch` to land wins (R21).
12. **Brick someone's launch by keeping `launch` out of its attested window** (audit round 3, rated medium by its finder; the two skeptics and the proof of concept rated it low)
    - Every `launch` write-locks the `Launch` account, the only writable account of `launch` that does not derive from the fresh mint, so anyone can fill its 24M-CU per-block write budget for the whole window ahead of keepers that bid less (5.2.9 describes the cost model). Congestion or a keeper outage has the same effect, and so does a relayer that holds the creator's attestation back and lands it at `now == not_after`.
    - Before F1 the attestation was written once, so the launch stayed `Configured` forever and every later signature only burned a leaf (proof of concept against the SBPF v3 builds: four re-signed windows burned four leaves, and 35,308,080 lamports of rent stayed locked with a small record at LiteSVM's rent rate, which is higher than mainnet's; the finding estimated about 0.06 SOL with the largest record). No user funds exist before `launch`.
    - [M] F1 (merged): an attestation is recorded only with at least 60 s of its window left (`max(now, not_before) + 60 <= not_after`), so a late or held attestation only burns its leaf; and once a window has passed while the launch is `Configured`, the next valid attestation of the launch's identity replaces the old one, every field overwritten, so the creator signs a new window at its next leaf. An unexpired attestation and a live launch's attestation are never replaced. Tests: `audit_round3.rs` (`f1_…`, six tests), `metadata_record.rs` (four F1 tests with real LMS signatures), host `launch_window_leaves_a_minute_at_every_landing`; mutants L75 to L89 and 15 LiteSVM ones (AUDIT.md §13, §14).
    - [R] R28: contention can still keep `launch` out of each window while the attacker pays; each retry costs the creator one leaf. Creators choose long windows (up to 1 h) and keepers pay priority fees, which raise the attacker's cost linearly.
    - [R] R32: once a window has passed, an older signature of the identity that was sent but never landed can take the freed slot if its own window still has a minute left; whoever holds its bytes picks among windows and records the creator signed, delaying the launch by up to 7 days per held signature. Clients land or retire every signature they reveal before signing a new window (SDK 0.5.0, unreleased: `PendingAttestationError`, `landOrBurnPending`; 5.13).

### 5.2 Insiders, snipers and bundlers capture the launch

1. **Buy in the token-creation transaction** (create and buy, FACTS #65)
   - [X] The hook refuses buys until the shield ends unless they are the batch buy (L1). The pool is created by CPI inside `launch`, and DBC's first-swap exemption is disabled and unreachable through CPI (L5).
2. **Buy directly from DBC during the commit or execution window**
   - [X] `SnipeShield` refusal for every buy outside a top-level `execute_batch`, whoever receives the tokens and whatever the amount, a buy whose output rounds to zero included (`l2b_zero_output_buys_cannot_cross_the_snipe_shield`).
3. **Buy into an exempt account** (the batch escrow or a launch-authority ATA)
   - [X] During the shield, refused like any other buy: the shield is checked before exemptions (`exemptions_never_open_the_snipe_shield`).
   - [M] After the shield such a buy is only a donation. The tokens are never paid out to the buyer: `tokens_out` is measured as a balance difference inside `execute_batch`, and `distribute` burns the launch authority's tokens.
4. **Take most of the batch**
   - [M] Pro-rata: every lamport buys at the same average price, and splitting wallets or committing first gains nothing. When commits exceed `batch_total_cap`, the batch swaps at most the cap and refunds the rest pro rata, so the cap cannot be filled first-come-first-served. The creator may set `batch_wallet_cap`, at least the resolved total cap / 200.
   - [R] A participant with more capital gets a proportionally larger share. That is intended. `batch_wallet_cap` is per wallet, so many wallets get around it; it only limits naive concentration.
5. **Be first at the public open**
   - [M] Max-buy and max-wallet during the guard. The optional wallet-to-wallet lock stops token transfers between holders' accounts during the guard, so Sybil wallets cannot consolidate their tokens by transferring them (L8).
   - [M] Optional creator launch rules add anti-bot limits, for example time-windowed caps or a limit on the number of top-level instructions in a buying transaction (5.15). Rules on wallet-to-wallet transfers can be routed around through the vault or the shield (R35).
   - [R] Caps are per token account and per transfer, so many wallets get around them, and fast bots still lead. Ordering gateways and leaders decide who lands first.
   - [R] The lock does not stop control changing without a transfer: one party controlling several wallets (a multisig, a smart wallet, keys it holds), a PDA or smart-wallet owner, a PQ vault's key rotated to another party, or a delegate approval (5.2.11). A change of a token account's owner, which moves no tokens either, is refused since F2: during the guard tokens only land where the owner can never change (5.2.11).
6. **Front-run `execute_batch`**
   - [X] No one else can buy a token before the batch: the only buy the hook passes before then, of any amount, is the one inside `execute_batch`, and nobody holds tokens to sell before the batch.
   - [M] `execute_batch` runs only as the pool's first swap (`PoolAlreadyTraded`), so even a buy that got past the hook could not make the batch buy at a moved price; the batch would expire and refund in full.
   - [R] R16.
7. **Call `execute_batch` through another program to sneak a buy in**
   - [X] The hook accepts a buy during the shield only when the transaction's current top-level instruction is the launchpad's `execute_batch`. A CPI'd `execute_batch` has its buy refused, and any other program's instruction is recorded in the instructions sysvar under its own program ID (HOOKS.md §2). `end_snipe_shield` refuses the same way (`NotInBatch`).
8. **Disguise a buy as a sell** (a buy whose output account is the DBC base vault, so the hook would see the vault as the destination)
   - [X] DBC refuses it before any transfer: `output_token_account` and `base_vault` would be the same mutable account (`ConstraintDuplicateMutableAccount`, error 2040). `l2d_dbc_refuses_a_buy_whose_output_is_the_base_vault` pins this on mainnet DBC, during and after the shield.
   - [M] Token-2022 never calls a hook for a transfer from an account to itself, so the hook would not see it anyway. The hook still treats every transfer out of the base vault as a buy (defense in depth, tested by calling `execute` directly), and the first-swap check backs both up.
   - [R] R16.
9. **Keep every other commit out of the commit window** (audit round 2, finding C7, rated high)
   - Every commit write-locks the `Launch` account and the batch escrow. Agave admits at most 24M CU per block of transactions writing one account and orders them by fee reward per unit of requested compute (FACTS, PQ v2 facts). A party that sends `[launch, commit(cap)]` and then fills the `Launch` account's budget each slot with high-priority busy-loop transactions keeps commits that bid less out for as long as it pays, and takes the whole batch at the opening price.
   - [M] The commit window is at least 60 s (`MIN_OPEN_DELAY`, was 10 s), so the cost grows linearly with it (the round-2 estimate: about 0.0035 SOL per 10 s against commits with no priority fee, so about 0.02 SOL per minute). The SDK sets a measured compute limit on `commit` (130,000 CU by default) and accepts a priority fee (`computeUnitPrice`), which raise the attacker's cost per slot; a creator who wants a fair open chooses a long window.
   - [R] R23: not removed. A well-funded party can still exclude commits that bid less than it does.
10. **Front-run another committer's payout destination**
    - [X] Payout owners are fixed at the first commit, and only the committer or a PQ vault of the paid mint can be named (A7). `claim` is permissionless but pays only those owners.
    - [M] A vault named by several committers receives at most one wallet's cap in total (`PayoutVault`, `review_a_payout_vault_receives_at_most_one_wallet_cap`), so hook-exempt claims cannot add up past `max_wallet` in one account.
    - [R] R29, payout-vault quota griefing (audit round 3, low; documented, not fixed): anyone can use up a vault's quota by committing with that vault as token owner, after which the vault's holder cannot commit naming it (`CapExceeded`) and either commits into its Ed25519 wallet or opens another vault. Only by donating: the tokens that commit buys are paid to the vault, while its unspent wSOL is refunded to the attacker's own refund owner, so the attack costs the part of the commit the batch spent (proof of concept: 0.143 SOL of a 0.5 SOL cap in an oversubscribed batch, all of it turned into tokens in the victim's vault). The base mint, and so any vault of it, exists only after `launch`; a client that opens a fresh vault and commits to it in one transaction gives no ordinary user time to see the address. A leader or MEV searcher that reorders transactions could still copy the public key, open the vault and fill it first (not tested); the victim then retries with a new key. Binding the record to its first committer would let a front-runner lock a vault out for one lamport, and keying the cap on (launch, committer, vault) would bring back the aggregation `PayoutVault` stops, so neither was adopted.
11. **Pass positions to another owner during a `p2p_locked` guard by reassigning token accounts** (audit round 3, low)
    - A buyer creates a token account for the launch mint that is not an ATA and has no `ImmutableOwner` extension, buys into it through DBC (an ordinary buy within the caps), then signs Token-2022 `SetAuthority(AccountOwner)` to hand the account to another wallet. No tokens move, so the hook never runs; the proof of concept sold three Sybil accounts to one wallet atomically against a SOL payment, left that wallet holding more than `max_wallet` across them, and sold from one during the guard. A direct transfer between the same accounts fails with `TransfersLocked`.
    - [X] Batch claims were never affected: they are paid into Token-2022 ATAs, which always carry `ImmutableOwner` (`SetAuthority` on them fails with Token-2022 error 34).
    - [X] F2 (merged): while the guard is on, the hook refuses every transfer that is not a sell into a token account without `ImmutableOwner` (`MutableOwner`, 6010), unless an exempt launchpad PDA owns it (only the launchpad could sign that account's owner change, and it never does). The rule runs after the zero-amount and exempt-destination passes and before the exempt-source pass, the lock and the caps (HOOKS.md §2). So no token account a buy, a claim or any other guarded transfer reaches can be reassigned; the proof of concept now fails at the buy (`f2_buys_and_transfers_need_an_immutable_owner_while_the_guard_is_on`, `receiving_accounts_need_an_immutable_owner_during_the_guard`). SDK 0.4.0's `buildSwapTx` and `buildReleaseToTx` refuse such accounts first, in plain words.
    - [R] R30: control of a position can still change with no transfer and no owner change, so no hook sees it (no funds are stolen, sells stay open, and the lock ends at `guard_end_ts` anyway):
      - a wallet several people control: a multisig, a smart wallet with several members, a key several people hold;
      - a token account owned by a PDA or a smart wallet, whose own program can hand control to someone else (new members, a new signer);
      - a PQ vault: tokens bought or claimed into a vault's ATA during the guard stay there, but the holder can sign `withdraw` with `max_amount = 0` (a pure key rotation, which reserves and moves nothing) and `next_key` set to another party's LM-OTS key; from then on only that party can withdraw. Delivery out of the vault still waits for the guard under the lock, but the position changed hands off-market;
      - a delegate approval (`Approve`): the delegate gets revocable control shared with the owner, and can sell during the guard, because sells always pass.
12. **Fill a shared account to max-wallet** (audit round 3, info; documented, not fixed)
    - Max-wallet applies to every receiving account during the guard, including accounts many people pay into. With `max_wallet` set and the lock off, a holder of about `max_wallet` tokens can wrap them into the Shield's escrow for the mint (one per mint, owned by the shield authority, which is not exempt), after which every further wrap of that token fails with `MaxWallet` until the guard ends. With plain transfers it can top up a victim's canonical ATA to `max_wallet`, after which DBC buys into that account and PQ-vault `release` payouts into it fail until then; `release_to` into another account the victim owns, one with an immutable owner, still works.
    - [R] R31: griefing only, at most 72 h, nothing lost; the attacker keeps its zTokens 1:1 and gets its tokens back by unwrapping. Making the shield authority exempt would let `p2p_locked` holders move tokens through wrap and unwrap, so it was not done. Documented in HOOKS.md §9 and PRIVACY.md §4.

### 5.3 MEV searchers, block producers and ordering gateways

1. **Sandwich the batch buy**
   - [X] See 5.2.6. This is why `execute_batch` can safely use minimum-out 0.
   - [R] It holds only while the hook is active. If a DBC upgrade, or a future operator revoke (PR #214, not deployed), removed the hook before the batch, DBC 0.2.1's logic would make `execute_batch` fail, because it always passes hook accounts (`NoTransferHookProgram`), and the batch would refund. A release that also dropped that check would still not make the batch sandwichable: the front-running buy sets the pool's `has_swap` flag, and `execute_batch` then refuses to run (`PoolAlreadyTraded`), so the batch refunds (section 5.7).
2. **Keep `execute_batch` out of its window, then snipe the public open** (audit round 2, finding C8)
   - Anyone, not only leaders and gateways, can do this by filling the per-block write budget of the `Launch` account or the pool ahead of the keepers: round 2 measured about 0.012 SOL for the old 30-second window against keepers that bid nothing.
   - [M] The batch expires and everyone gets a full refund (L4). The window is now 600 s, so the attack costs linearly more (about 20 times, an estimate), and the batch ends the shield itself, so the long window never delays trading. Execution is permissionless: any participant, the app's crank and independent keepers can submit it, with tight compute limits and escalating priority fees (OPERATIONS.md, Keepers).
   - [R] R7: the launch opens without a batch buy. Leaders and gateways can censor it without paying.
3. **Sandwich public trades**
   - [M] DBC enforces the trader's minimum-out. The app sets slippage limits and may route through MEV-protected paths.
   - [R] Standard AMM exposure.
4. **Timestamp skew**
   - [R] `Clock::unix_timestamp` is a stake-weighted estimate and can drift by seconds. Windows are half-open intervals of 60 s or more (the launch window is closed, 60 s to 1 h), so drift can shift a window slightly but never open two windows at once.
5. **Hold back or reorder a vault withdrawal or an attestation**
   - [M] A withdrawal has no expiry and executes whenever it lands; an attestation lands as long as its window can still open. The client re-broadcasts the same signature. Seeing one signature does not help forge another, and a relayer cannot change what it does (A5).
   - [R] Delay; an attestation held back until less than 60 s of its window remain only burns its leaf, and the creator signs a new window. Before F1, one landed in its window's last seconds was recorded with no time left to use it, which left the launch unable to go live (5.1.12). A held signature can also be landed after a window passed, to take the freed slot (R32).
6. **Win the launch window**
   - [R] R21: inside the attested window the first `launch` to land wins, and whoever lands it may bundle its own commit. The batch is pro rata, so this buys no price advantage, only a place in the commit window.

### 5.4 Any user attacks launch accounting

| Attack | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Claim another participant's commit to yourself | [X] `claim(owner)` pays only the commit's recorded payout owners' canonical ATAs. Cranking it helps the owner. |
| Change a commit's payout owners | [X] They are fixed by the first commit; a later commit with other owners fails `InvalidPayoutOwner` (`a7_payouts_go_to_the_owners_fixed_at_the_first_commit`). |
| Block someone's claim | [X] `claim` validates the payout owners' ATAs with `AtaCreator::ensure` (canonical address, created if missing, owner field not compared), so only the owner can change its own accounts (L3). |
| Redirect someone's refund | [R] Only by getting a committer that is its own refund owner to reassign its own SPL Token wSOL ATA, for example with an owner-change drainer. `claim`, which anyone can crank, then pays the refund into that account, which the new owner controls; the base tokens still reach the committer's Token-2022 ATA, whose owner cannot change (L3). A vault refund owner is immune. |
| Claim twice | [X] `Commit.claimed`, plus the running totals `tokens_claimed` and `quote_refunded` against `tokens_out` and `total - quote_spent` (L3) |
| Inflate `tokens_out` or the refund pool with donations | [X] Both are balance differences measured inside `execute_batch`. Donations stay in the escrow as dust. |
| Exploit rounding with many tiny commits | [M] Every division rounds down (`math.rs::pro_rata`). Dust is bounded by the participant count, and the sum of payouts never exceeds the escrow. |
| Commit outside the window; execute twice | [X] Half-open window checks; `executed` flag |
| Exceed caps | [X] `batch_wallet_cap` per owner and per payout vault. The total cap does not refuse commits; `execute_batch` swaps at most `min(total_committed, batch_total_cap)` and requires `quote_spent` to stay within it, and `batch_total_cap <= migration_quote_threshold` (`policy.rs`). |
| Supply fake recipient ATAs or a foreign LP position | [X] Fee and claim accounts must be the canonical ATAs of the stored wallets or payout owners (`token_util.rs::AtaCreator`). Positions must be owned by the launch authority in this launch's pool. |
| Substitute programs or mints (fake DBC, fake wSOL, fake hook) | [X] Address constraints on `dbc_program`, `dbc_event_authority`, `quote_mint`, `hook_program`. CPIs use declared program-ID constants. The hook accounts of `execute_batch` and `claim` must be exactly `[meta list, policy, instructions sysvar, hook program]` (`hook_accounts.rs`). |
| Make a non-zero cap round to zero | [X] `policy.rs::cap_from_bps` rejects it. |
| Overflow | [M] `overflow-checks = true` in the release profile, checked math, u128 intermediates |
| Duplicate mutable accounts | [M] Anchor 1.x rejects them by default (FACTS #14). |
| Front-run `create_launch` with the same `launch_seed` | [X] The `Launch` PDA is `["launch", creator, launch_seed]`: nobody can take another creator's address (`a_launch_address_names_its_creator`; PQ v2 review finding 6). |
| Name a fake treasury | [X] `initialize_protocol` accepts only an existing wSOL PQ vault, and only from the upgrade authority (F2, G3). |

### 5.5 Honeypot or denial of service through the hook

1. **A hook bug refuses sells**
   - [M] The sell-first rule, pure `evaluate`, property tests over 200,000 random transfers, and the integration tests in SPEC §6.
   - [R] Audit.
2. **A malicious or extreme policy** (for example an endless snipe shield)
   - [X] Policies come only from `launch`, signed by the launchpad PDA (G4). The hook re-enforces its own hard bounds: shield at most 1 h, guard at most 72 h, max-wallet at least 0.1% of supply, max-buy at least 0.01% of supply, creator rules that verify for the policy's guard (`create_policy`, `InvalidRules`). `end_snipe_shield` only ever moves the end earlier. Creator rules can only refuse buys and wallet transfers before `guard_end_ts` (5.15).
3. **Transfers fail because hook accounts are missing**
   - [M] The meta list holds only the mint-derived policy PDA and the fixed address of the instructions sysvar. Every client, including Meteora's SDKs, which resolve with default keys (FACTS #41), resolves them the same way. Token-2022 re-derives and checks the accounts. A missing validation account makes Anchor fail the call: fail closed.
   - [R] A client that passes no hook accounts gets a failed transaction, not a loss.
4. **The hook exhausts the compute budget or CPI depth**
   - [M] The hook's `execute` is stateless and allocation-free, reads two token accounts (the destination's extension entries included) and the policy (plus the instructions sysvar, for buys during the shield and for transfers that reach creator rules) and makes no CPI. The batch path uses 4 of 5 depth levels (ARCHITECTURE.md §6). Measured in LiteSVM on the round-1b code (HOOKS.md §7): a hooked `transfer_checked` costs about 14,900 to 23,400 CU in total, depending on the mint's PDA bumps, of which the hook's own `execute` is 2,262 to 3,228, and 4,404 for the batch buy, which reads the instructions sysvar; audit round 3's F2 added 42 CU when every rule runs and 13 for the batch buy. `execute_batch` as a whole, including the 13.65k-CU `end_snipe_shield` CPI, took 114k to 174k CU on the PQ v2 build. On the review-4 build (2026-10-11) a sell costs 2,425 in the hook (decided from the policy's head), and a buy 3,295 and a wallet transfer 3,318 without creator rules, 9,126 and 8,194 at the largest rule set; only transfers that reach the rules read the sysvar and the supply (review-4 fix Rb).
5. **The hook is called directly with spoofed accounts**
   - [X] `execute` writes nothing. A direct call can only return Ok or an error, so there is nothing to corrupt (HOOKS.md). A spoofed instructions sysvar is refused (`UnsupportedSysvar`). `end_snipe_shield`, the hook's one writing instruction after policy creation, needs the launchpad's hook-authority PDA signature and the launchpad's `execute_batch` as the top-level instruction.
6. **The hook program is upgraded**
   - [M] Finalize it.
   - [R] Until then, the upgrade authority could ship a honeypot. This is the single most important reason to finalize.
7. **DBC does not revoke the hook at completion**
   - [M] The rules expire at `guard_end_ts` regardless.
   - [R] DAMM v2 cannot migrate a mint with an active hook (FACTS #35). A DBC regression would stall graduation.
8. **The batch is executed through a router**
   - [M] Keepers and the app submit `execute_batch` top-level. A CPI'd call fails without spending anything.
   - [R] If nobody submits it top-level within the 10-minute window, the batch expires and refunds (L4).

### 5.6 Quantum attacker

| Target | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Ordinary wallets (every address is an Ed25519 public key) | [R] Fully exposed. No application can fix this. Use the PQ vault for long-term custody (POST_QUANTUM.md). |
| PQ vault funds | [M] An LM-OTS forgery needs a SHA-256 preimage-type search: about 2^128 quantum work for n = 32. The signed message binds program, vault, mint, sequence, recipient wallet, maximum amount and next key, and the next key's index is bound to its position. |
| Launch authorization | [M] Forging the creator's wallet does not make any launch go live: `launch` requires an LMS attestation by the launch's identity (A1), which needs a SHA-256 preimage-type search to forge. [X] It cannot attest the creator's configuration under other metadata, other rules or another window: all three are in the signed message. [X] It cannot choose when the launch opens: the window is signed and `launch` is permissionless inside it (A4). [R] It can create launches that *name* the victim's identity; they never go live (`a5_a_launch_naming_another_creators_identity_never_goes_live`). |
| Token metadata | [X] It cannot rename a launch: the record is at the address of its hash and signed (M1, M2). |
| Creator launch rules | [X] It cannot change or swap them: the identity signs their hash in the attestation (v3), the record is at the address of that hash, and `launch` installs exactly its bytes (CR4). Forging the creator's wallet gives no power over them. |
| Autopilot graphs and the treasury | [M] Only an `Authorization` written after verifying the launch identity's LMS signature activates, freezes or revokes (`pq_authorize`, the same leaves as attestations); no Ed25519 key can change a graph (AP3). The treasury is a PDA with no key. [R] The operator, keepers and recipients that are ordinary wallets are Ed25519: a CRQC can act as the operator, within its bounds until the identity revokes it (R40), run nodes as a keeper, or take an ordinary recipient's payouts. |
| A creator identity's secret | [M] It comes from a dedicated phrase or a passkey's PRF output, never from the wallet; the derivation and the HMAC are symmetric (POST_QUANTUM.md §3.3). [R] Passkey channels to roaming authenticators, the hybrid transport and synced-passkey providers use classical public-key cryptography; a recording of them could be decrypted later. |
| Launchpad PDAs (launch authority, batch authority, escrows) | [M] A PDA has no private key, so there is nothing to forge, provided the owning program is immutable. |
| Our upgrade authority | [M] After finalization there is no key. [R] Before it, a forged upgrade-authority signature could replace our programs. |
| Meteora, Token-2022 and ATA program authorities (4 of 7, 4 of 6 Ed25519 keys; feature gates) | [R] Forged member signatures could upgrade DBC, DAMM v2 or Token-2022, which affects every token. This is outside our control. |
| Validator and vote keys | [R] Chain-level attack, out of scope. |
| Treasury and fee recipients | [M] The treasury is a wSOL PQ vault (F2). Recipients and payout owners may be PQ vaults (F1, A7). [R] Recipients that are ordinary wallets are Ed25519. |
| Shield history | [R] ElGamal ciphertexts recorded today can be decrypted later ("harvest now, decrypt later"). |
| Throwaway fee payers (vault withdrawals, metadata, rules, attestations, authorizations) | [R] The attacker can steal their SOL or grief them. They have no authority over vault funds or over what an attestation or authorization does, so use throwaway payers with only what one transaction needs. |
| Mint authorities of vaulted non-Noised mints | [R] Ed25519 keys. Forged, a freeze, pause or hook authority can delay payouts; a mint authority can dilute. Mints whose authorities could take deposits are refused (V6). |

[M] Disclosure; `verifyLaunch` reports the current upgrade slots; we monitor ProgramData (OPERATIONS.md). [M] If that check changed too, the batch's minimum-out-0 swap still could not be front-run: any earlier buy sets the pool's `has_swap` flag and `execute_batch` refuses to run (`PoolAlreadyTraded`), so the batch refunds. |

| DBC release lowers the heap it gives a pool's metadata | [R] A launch whose derived uri no longer fits would fail at `launch` (refused atomically; commits never start). Today DBC takes uris up to about 8,685 bytes, 2.1 times the launchpad's largest (FACTS, on-chain metadata facts). |
| DBC release alters the metadata strings it writes | [M] `launch` re-reads the mint and refuses anything but the attested record (`MetadataMismatch`, M2). |
| Disable a graduated Noised pool | [R] Trading and adds stop. The permanently locked liquidity is stuck forever and stops earning (FACTS #37). Fee claims still work. |
| Raise fees on a graduated pool | [R] Base fee up to 10% after the schedule; dynamic fee changes within the 99% total cap (FACTS #36). Every graduated pool already carries its migration config's default dynamic fee, up to about 20% of the tier (F5; audit round 2, finding C13). |
| Close DBC migration configs | [R] Completed curves cannot migrate until a config exists again. Funds stay in DBC vaults, and swaps revert after completion. |
| A DAMM v2 release lets others create pools under a config, or DBC signs as a config's pool creator outside migration | [R] R41: the autopilot's `bind_pool` could bind a pool someone else created for the launch's pair; buybacks would trade there, still within the price bound (5.16). |
| DAMM v2 0.2.4 reward-accumulator overflow (FACTS #38) | [M] Noised never initializes or funds rewards. [R] Reward index 1 can be initialized only by an operator. |
| Token-2022 upgrade or ZK proof kill switch | [R] Affects every Token-2022 token and zToken, and Token-2022 vaults. Confidential balances were frozen for about 12 months in 2025-26 (FACTS #53). |
| Feature gate SIMD-0500 activates | [M] Build SBPF v3 so finalization stays possible (OPERATIONS.md). |

A fail-closed guard was considered and **not adopted**. It would have the launchpad refuse to CPI when DBC's or DAMM v2's ProgramData changes. Accepting a new Meteora version would then need an admin key, which reintroduces exactly the trust this design removes. Batch refunds and fee claims would also stop after every routine Meteora upgrade.

### 5.12 PQ vault (non-quantum attacks)

| Attack | Mitigation / residual |
|---|---|
| The recipient makes a signed withdrawal fail forever: reassign its SPL Token ATA's owner, require memos, refuse non-confidential credits, or close its ATA | [X] `withdraw` reads and writes no recipient account (V4). `release` pays the canonical ATA whatever its owner field says, recreates it if closed, and sends a memo first when one is required; `release_to` lets the recipient pick another account. [R] The recipient can delay its own payout; the amount stays reserved. Before audit round 1 this attack froze the whole vault and pushed the key holder towards reusing a one-time key. |

| Squat a payout address | [X] Payouts are PDAs of the vault program, created only by `withdraw`, one per sequence. |
| Release a payout to the wrong account | [X] `release` checks the canonical ATA address of the signed recipient; `release_to` needs the recipient's signature (V1). |
| Spend one payout's tokens for another | [X] `withdraw` reserves only `balance - reserved` (invariant 11). |
| Send SOL or another mint to the vault PDA | [R] Unrecoverable, a documented limitation (SPEC §3.8). The app shows the vault's token account as the deposit address. |
| A mint authority freezes, pauses or hooks payouts | [R] Delays delivery; never blocks `withdraw` or takes funds (V4, V6). |
| Recipient is a token account, a mint or a PDA nobody can sign for | [M] `checkRecipient` refuses or warns before signing, and the signer requires the user's confirmation of that check (SPEC §4.1). [R] The program cannot tell; a client that skips the check can lose that payout. |
| Hide a revealed withdrawal from `findPendingWithdrawal` with cheap history spam, or poison it with an unauthenticated next key (round 2, findings C1, C2, C10) | [M] The search authenticates what it finds, reads spam last, and refuses to sign after an incomplete search; the durable vault-key ledger is the primary record. [R] A user without its ledger record, on a flooded vault, cannot sign until the search completes or the record is restored. |

### 5.13 Post-quantum launch attestation

| Attack | Mitigation / residual |
|---|---|
| Forge an attestation | [X] Needs an LMS signature by the launch's identity over the exact 221-byte message v3 (189 bytes in v2, before P1c): a SHA-256 preimage-type search (A1). |
| Replay an attestation on another launch, program, configuration, metadata, rules or window, or on another cluster | [X] The message names the program, the launch, `params_hash`, the creator, the metadata hash, the rules hash and the window; the identity derivation carries a cluster byte (`attestations_verify_only_for_their_launch_and_metadata`). |
| Obtain two signatures at one leaf, then forge (PQ v2 review finding 1: a valid signature that could not land left its leaf clear, and the corrected retry was handed the same leaf) | [X] Every valid signature at an unused leaf lands and burns it, whatever it signed (A2). [M] The leaf probe is keyed by the message, so different messages start at different leaves (review finding 3), and the SDK's durable leaf ledger refuses a second message at a revealed leaf. [R] R24: two devices with two unshared ledgers, or a custom ledger without an atomic `add`. |
| Steer a second device onto the leaf of a withheld signature (audit round 4, ID-F1, medium-high; two independent proofs of concept) | [R] As audited: the probe's start was public and the client stepped past every leaf its RPC reported as used. A dishonest RPC that kept one device's signature without landing it reported fake used bits to a second device with the same identity (a synced passkey, or the same device after its site data was cleared), whose probe then walked onto exactly that leaf: two messages at one leaf, so forgeries. A tampered "other device" export with an unsigned record did the same without a lying RPC (ID-F2b). [M] Fixed in SDK 0.9.0 and the app: the probe's start is `HMAC-SHA256` under a key derived from the identity's secret (`"phalanx/pq/identity/leaf/v2"`), so nobody else can predict or aim at a leaf; on-chain bits never move the choice; the client refuses to sign if the chosen leaf is set on-chain or if the bitmap shows any used leaf its ledger has no record of, until the other device's records are imported; unsigned imported records only mark a leaf taken. Best practice: one device per quantum key unless you import its records. [R] R24 (unsteerable). |
| Lie about the clock to a signing client (audit round 4, ID-F5) | [R] As audited the SDK took signed times from the RPC's clock alone: a clock a year ahead turned a one-hour freeze approval into one usable for a year on the real chain (`poc_identity_freeze_clock`), and windows could be shifted the same way. [M] Fixed in SDK 0.9.0 and the app: refuse to sign a time when the RPC's clock and the device's (and a second RPC's, when available) differ by more than 120 s; show absolute dates; a freeze's `not_after` at most 24 h ahead, a window at most the program's 7-day lead. [R] R43: a wrong device clock with an RPC that agrees. |
| Race the pending-signature guard (audit round 4, ID-F4) | [R] As audited, SDK 0.5's `PendingAttestationError` guard was check-then-act: two concurrent calls (a double click, two tabs, a retry loop) both passed it and both signed, and the RPC holding an unlanded signature could switch it off by reporting that leaf as used or a clock past its window. [M] Fixed in SDK 0.9.0 and the app: a per-launch lock around the check, the reservation and the signature; a signature stays pending unless its leaf landed with a known outcome; at most 3 unlanded signatures per launch (and per launch and kind for authorizations) without an explicit confirmation (ID-F2); the reviewed plan's message compared before reserving (ID-F6). |
| Turn an attesting signature into a burn by naming another record account, repeatedly (on-chain metadata review, medium) | [X] Every account except the payer is fixed by the signed data; another `token_meta`, or another `rules` record than the signed rules hash names, fails with `ConstraintSeeds` and burns nothing (A5, `a_relayer_cannot_turn_an_attesting_signature_into_a_burn`, `attestation_v3_binds_the_published_rules`). |
| Hold back a pending attestation | [R] R22: whoever sees it first can delay it, and if it is held until less than 60 s of its window remain it only burns its leaf. The creator re-broadcasts the same bytes; it never re-signs for a delay. [M] F1 (merged): before it, one landed at `now == not_after` was recorded with no time left and the launch could never go live (audit round 3, 5.1.12); now it needs at least 60 s of its window left to be recorded, and an attestation whose window passed unused is replaced by the next one. |
| Keep `launch` out of an attested window (write-lock contention on `Launch`, congestion, an outage) | [M] F1 (merged): the creator signs a new window at its next leaf, which replaces the expired attestation while the launch is `Configured` (before F1 the launch could never go live, 5.1.12). [R] R28: each window can be contended again, at the attacker's cost. |
| Land an older, unlanded signature once a window has passed (round 3 fix review, info) | [R] R32: before F1 an attested launch made every other signature of its identity inert; now, once the stored window has passed unused, any valid signature of the identity whose own window still has a minute left attests, including one the creator sent earlier that never landed (a dropped transaction an RPC or relayer kept). Whoever holds it can land it ahead of the creator's new signature and so pick, among windows and records the creator signed, one that opens up to 7 days later; the new signature only burns its leaf until that window ends. [M] Every outcome was signed by the identity, and the slot frees again after that window. Clients land, or let burn, every signature they reveal. [M] SDK 0.5.0 (unreleased) enforces this for the signatures in its leaf ledger: `buildAttestLaunchTx` throws `PendingAttestationError`, before reserving or signing anything, instead of signing a new message for a launch while an earlier recorded signature of it has not landed and its window has not ended; `landOrBurnPending` re-sends those signatures (never a new one), with `burnOnly` only the ones that would just burn their leaf, which is safe while the current attestation holds the slot; `getLaunchFlowStatus` lists them and warns. A signature held only in another device's ledger is invisible until imported (`importLeafRecords`). |
| Squat the identity or attestation PDA | [X] Only a valid signature creates them; an address pre-funded by anyone is taken over (`a5_prefunded_identity_and_attestation_addresses_block_nothing`). |
| Burn another identity's leaves | [X] Only a valid signature by that identity burns one of its leaves; a signature by another identity burns that identity's own leaf. |
| Exhaust an identity | [R] Each burn-only landing spends one of 1,024 leaves of the identity that signed; only its holder (or whoever relays signatures it revealed) can spend them. Autopilot authorizations (`pq_authorize`) use the same 1,024 leaves and the same bitmap. The SDK caps automatic re-signs after burn-only landings and warns at 1,000 used leaves. |
| Attest a launch whose parameters someone else chose (a squatted address, a launch copied before confirmation) | [X] The `Launch` PDA names its creator (5.4). [M] The SDK attests only a finalized launch this wallet created with the reviewed `params_hash`. |
| Sign a window in milliseconds, a swapped window or one already over | [M] Such a signature only burns its leaf (`review_an_attestation_of_a_window_that_cannot_open_only_burns_its_leaf`); the SDK validates windows in `bigint` seconds before signing. |
| Steal the identity's secret | [R] R25: malware on the creator's device, a phished passkey or a compromised sync provider can sign as the identity. Launches already live are unaffected; pending ones could be attested with other metadata or windows. |

### 5.15 Creator launch rules

A creator's launch rules (PXR1) add refusals to the Launch Guard during the guard (HOOKS.md §2, SPEC §1.9). Guarantees CR1 to CR5. A launch with rules is tagged "programmable" next to its QSL-1 level: a disclosed feature, not a safer level.

| Attack | Mitigation / residual |
|---|---|
| Honeypot through rules: refuse sells, claims or the batch, or keep refusing after the guard | [X] Rules run last, after every built-in allow and denial, and can only refuse (CR1). A sell, a claim, the batch buy, a zero-amount or exempt transfer and anything at or after `guard_end_ts` never reach them, and every rule's window ends inside the guard, at most 72 h (CR2). Sells and the guard's end are decided from the policy's head before the rest is decoded (review-4 fix R3). Tests: `a_rule_that_refuses_everything_spares_sells_claims_and_the_batch`, `random_rule_sets_only_refuse_what_reaches_them` (300,000 cases), `rules_never_touch_sells_claims_the_batch_or_after_the_guard` (mainnet DBC). |
| Hold holders' exits: a rule aimed at the PQ vault's releases and the shield's unwraps (review 4, R4-1) | [X] Since review-4 fix R1, a transfer whose current top-level instruction is the Noised vault or shield skips the rules; the built-ins still apply (CR3; `rules_never_freeze_pq_vault_or_shield_releases`, `creator_rules_never_hold_a_vault_release`, `creator_rules_never_hold_a_wrap_or_an_unwrap`). |
| Insider-only buying: refuse every public buy except into listed wallets | [R] R33: such a rule set verifies and can run for the whole guard, a private sale of up to 72 h; every holder can still sell (`insider_only_buying_verifies_and_sells_still_pass`). Disclosed, not prevented: the plain-English explanation of the rules and the verifier's and badge's warnings (buys restricted to a key list, rules on wallet-to-wallet transfers, rules active for more than an hour, vacuous rules). |
| Vacuous or misleading rules that look protective | [R] R34: rules such as DENY when FALSE verify and never refuse, yet `RULES_BOUND` is set; the bit means "rules are bound", not "protected" (`vacuous_rules_verify_and_never_refuse`). |
| Get past rules through the vault or the shield | [R] R35: wrap (rules skipped), move the zTokens (the zToken mint has no hook), unwrap (rules skipped) gets past rules on wallet-to-wallet transfers or owner lists, never past the built-ins: under the lock, wrap and unwrap are refused. A call into the vault or the shield through another program's CPI has that program as the top-level instruction, so the rules apply to it. |
| Swap the rules: attest or launch with another record (a relayer, or anyone) | [X] The record lives at `["rules", launch, rules_hash]`, content-addressed, and the identity signs `rules_hash` in message v3. `attest_launch` and `launch` take only the record that hash names (another address fails `ConstraintSeeds` and burns nothing), and `attest_launch` attests only if that record is valid. `launch` hashes the record's bytes again against the attested hash (review-4 fix R6a), `create_policy` verifies them again, and `launch` re-reads the written policy (`written.rules == rules`, else `UnsafePolicy`). The certificate records the hash and sets `RULES_BOUND` (CR4; `attestation_v3_binds_the_published_rules`, `launch_installs_the_attested_rules_and_certifies_them`). |
| Stale or junk rules records under a launch; a pre-funded record address | [X] Records are never modified or closed, and only the one whose hash the attestation names is ever used. An address pre-funded by anyone is taken over (`publish_rules`). The rules that run are in the policy, and anyone can re-check them against the certificate's hash (CR5). |
| Denial of service through large or expensive rules | [M] At most 1,228 bytes (16 rules, 8 keys, 64 condition nodes), verified once by `publish_rules` and again by `create_policy`; conditions partition the nodes, so a transfer evaluates each node at most once. At the largest rule set the hook costs 5,831 CU more per buy and 4,876 per wallet transfer, nothing more per sell (`compute_at_the_largest_rule_set`); clients raise compute limits by about 6k. |
| Malformed stored rules | [M] `create_policy` never stores rules that do not verify for the policy's guard (`InvalidRules`, 6012). Bytes that do not even parse refuse every transfer that reaches the rules (fail closed), never a sell or anything after the guard (`malformed_stored_rules_fail_closed`). |
| Use rules against control changes | [R] Rules see token transfers only, like the built-ins: control changing without a transfer stays invisible (R30). |

### 5.16 Autopilot

The autopilot (`noised_autopilot`, SPEC §9) runs a launch's treasury by a PXG1 graph. It is never in the transfer path. Guarantees AP1 to AP7.

| Attack | Mitigation / residual |
|---|---|
| Drain the treasury | [X] Value leaves only as burns of treasury tokens, to the bound pool's wSOL vault in a buyback whose tokens are all burned in the same instruction, to the active graph's recipients' canonical wSOL ATAs, and as the caller's bounded tip (AP1). The treasury is a PDA; no account is closed, delegated or re-owned, and only DAMM v2, Token-2022 and SPL Token are called, at fixed ids (AP2). No admin, no pause, no configuration account (AP7). Tests: host `treasury_outflows_only_to_allowed_sinks`, LiteSVM `treasury_value_leaves_only_to_allowed_sinks`. |
| Sell the launch token out of the treasury | [X] The only swap takes treasury wSOL in and gives treasury tokens out (AP2). |
| Touch the launch, its LP, its hook policy or its mint's authorities | [X] No autopilot instruction writes them (AP7). It reads the launchpad's `Launch` only at version 2, `Certificate` at versions 3 and 4 and `Authorization` at version 1, and refuses later versions until it is updated (review-4 fix R6b; `a_later_launch_version_is_refused_until_the_autopilot_knows_it`). |
| Bind another mint, or a launch that is not certified | [X] `bind_mint` needs the launchpad's certificate `["certificate", mint]` for this launch and mint, version 3 or 4, with `(checks & 4095) == 4095`. |
| Squat `init_autopilot` | [X] It takes no arguments: anyone may call it, once per launch, and it seeds the reference price with the launch's migration price from its DBC config. |
| Make buybacks trade on another pool (one the attacker seeded) | [X] `bind_pool` binds once, after DBC's migration: a DAMM v2 config whose pool creator authority is DBC's pool authority, and that config's existing pool for (mint, wSOL). Buybacks trade only there (`f2_any_dbc_migration_config_binds_and_no_other_pool_does`, `damm_configs_are_dbcs_migration_configs`). [R] R41: this relies on DAMM v2 letting only a config's pool creator authority create pools under it, and on DBC signing as that authority only in migration. |
| Make a buyback overpay (price manipulation) | [M] A buyback needs observations spanning at least 60 s in the current and previous 900-s bucket, refuses a spot price above `bound * (1 + premium)` with `bound = min(reference, lowest observed price)` and a premium of at most 20%, and sets a minimum out priced from the bound. The reference starts at the migration price, rises toward the window's lowest price when every observation is above it, by at most about 5% per hour, and falls at once to the window's highest price when every observation is below it (AP5; `a_sandwich_gains_at_most_the_premium`, `f1b_an_atomic_walk_moves_nothing`, `f1b_a_two_step_walk_is_stopped_by_one_honest_observation`, `f1a_a_fall_anyone_observed_bounds_every_buyback`). [R] R36: a keeper who sandwiches a buyback gains at most about premium × amount against the bound; one party making every observation of a window can walk the reference up at most 5% per hour (paying the pool's fees both ways) or, after a fall nobody observed, price a buyback at the window's lowest price. One honest observation per window closes both; `update_price` is free and every run is an observation. |
| Be a buyback's only observer after an unobserved fall (audit round 4, A4-1) | [R] R36, shown concretely: a keeper-attacker makes one pumped observation (buy, `update_price`, sell back, atomically; 0.043 SOL in the proof of concept) a minute before a daily node is due, so every observation in the window is its own and the bound is the stale reference, not a market that fell to 0.72 of it; the SDK keeper, which observed only when no observation was counting, went straight to `run` and lost the race. With one honest observation of the fall, the buyback could not run at all. [M] Keepers make their own observation at least 60 s before every buyback, even when one is counting (SDK 0.9.0); an independent observer every 10 minutes or less puts an honest observation in every 15-minute bucket (OPERATIONS.md, Keepers). |
| Throttle a buyback with minimal keeper runs (audit round 4, A4-2, low-medium) | [X] Since the fix, a keeper's buyback below the node's run cap is partial: it spends budget and earns `TIP × amount / p1` at most, but sets no `last_run` and does not count as a run, so the rest runs the same day; the parts never move more than `p1` together (`partial_spent`), and each leaves at least 1% of the pool's SOL for the rest (`RemainderTooSmall`). Before it, a griefer's 1% run used up a daily node's run for a full tip: three days bought back 0.30 of the promised 0.75 SOL (`a4_2_a_partial_buyback_does_not_use_up_the_daily_run`, `a4_2_partial_runs_never_move_more_than_the_run`). |
| Keep a re-appointed operator beyond revocation (audit round 4, A4-3, low) | [X] Since the fix, revocation payload v3 names the activation it revokes (`active_auth`): an operator appointed again, even by the same graph, can always be revoked with a new authorization, and an older revocation cannot reach it. Before it, the new revocation had the same payload as the used one, so it only burned its leaf and the operator kept running its gated nodes until a different graph applied, 72 h later (`a4_3_a_reappointed_operator_is_revoked_again`). |
| Farm tips with curve tokens before graduation (audit round 4, A4-4, low) | [X] Since the fix, a burn earns no tip before graduation, and after it the tokens are valued at the lower of the reference and the lowest observed price, this run's observation included. Before it, burns were valued at the migration price (25 times the curve's opening price in the proof of concept), so 50 burns of cheap curve tokens took the day's 5,000,000-lamport tip cap for a net 2.58 million lamports (`a4_4_burns_earn_no_tips_before_graduation`, `a4_4_burns_after_graduation_are_valued_at_the_lowest_observed_price`). |
| Make keepers pay recipients' account rent (audit round 4, M4, medium) | [X] Since the fix, a distribution never needs a recipient's wSOL account: one without an initialized wSOL account of its own is skipped and its share stays in the treasury; a run that pays nobody fails (`NoRecipientAccount`); keepers never create recipient accounts. Before it, recipients that closed their accounts after each payout made the keeper pay a token account's rent per recipient per run for a tip of at most 100,000 lamports (`m4_recipients_without_accounts_are_skipped_and_cost_keepers_nothing`). [R] R42. |
| Name a recipient that cannot use wSOL (audit round 4, C1) | [R] A PQ vault of another mint named as a graph recipient can never move the wSOL paid to its ATA; once anyone creates that ATA its share is lost. The identity signed the graph. [M] Fixed in SDK 0.9.0 and the app: graph recipients are checked like fee recipients (a vault of another mint refused, other program-owned recipients warned about). |
| Hold buybacks back | [R] R37: dumping the pool in an observation every 15 minutes holds buybacks back, and after a genuine rally they pause until the reference catches up. Liveness only: the treasury keeps its SOL. |
| Forge a graph change | [X] Only an `Authorization` written by the launchpad's `pq_authorize`, after verifying an LMS signature by the launch's own identity, activates, freezes or revokes; only kinds 1 to 3 are written (review-4 fix Ra), each at most once per (launch, kind, payload) (AP3; `leaves_are_shared_and_only_the_launchs_identity_authorizes`, `authorizations_are_written_exactly_once_per_kind_and_payload`, `other_kinds_only_burn_their_leaf`, `launchpad_authorizations_drive_activate_revoke_and_freeze`). No Ed25519 key, the creator's wallet included, can change a graph. |
| Replace a graph quickly (a rug by replacement) | [X] A replacement of an active or queued graph takes effect at least 72 h after its authorization landed; `run` refuses while a due update is not applied, and a frozen graph never changes (AP3; `graph_changes_need_the_identity_and_the_timelock`, `a_queued_first_graph_is_replaced_only_after_the_timelock`). |
| Replay or reuse an old authorization: give a revoked operator power back, or cancel a newer queued graph with an older freeze | [X] Authorizations are ordered by (landing slot, address) and each is used at most once. An activation must be newer than the latest activation and revocation used, a freeze newer than the latest freeze and activation, a revocation newer than the latest revocation (`f3_a_newer_activation_does_not_void_a_revocation`, `f3_authorizations_only_void_what_they_could_undo`). |
| Withhold an authorization, then land or use it later | [R] R38: a landed authorization can be used by anyone while it is valid: an activation within its signed `activate_at` and the timelock, a freeze only until its signed `not_after` (review-4 fix R5, `FreezeExpired`), a revocation at any time, since it only removes power. An unlanded one can be held back by whoever relays it, and the order is the landing slot, not the signing time. Clients land their own authorizations through several RPCs. |
| Abuse by the operator (for example an AI agent), or a stolen operator key | [M] The operator runs only its gated nodes and chooses only their timing and an amount from 1 to the node's limit; it cannot widen any bound (AP4). The identity can revoke it (kind 3), even on a frozen graph, and nothing signed before the revocation gives it power back (`operator_gated_nodes_and_revocation`). [R] R40: within those bounds until revoked. |
| Run a node early, too often or over its budget | [X] A node runs only when its trigger, its condition (on the reference price, never spot), its runs per epoch and its budget allow, and moves at most `min(p1, budget left, treasury)` (AP6; `runs_refuse_when_not_due_condition_budget_or_run_limit`, `ref_price_conditions_read_the_reference_not_spot`). |
| Drain the treasury through tips | [M] A tip is at most 100,000 lamports (a partial buyback's share of it), 10% of the value the run moved, what is left of 5,000,000 lamports per UTC day, and the treasury's wSOL after the action; a burn before graduation is worth nothing, and after it is valued at the lower of the reference and the lowest observed price; a distribution is worth what it paid (`tips_are_capped_per_day_and_come_only_from_the_treasury`, `f5_dust_burns_earn_no_tips`, audit round 4's `a4_4_…` tests). |
| Nobody runs the automation | [R] R39: nothing runs unless someone calls it. Keepers are paid tips; a node whose `p1` is below 0.001 SOL pays less than the full tip, so keepers may ignore it. An operator that stops running stops its gated nodes. |
| Race for a due node, its tip or a due update | [R] R21: whoever lands first runs the node and takes its tip, or applies the update; block producers decide. The node or the update does the same whoever lands it. |
