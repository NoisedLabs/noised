# Security guarantees

Every property Noised claims is listed here, with the code that enforces it, whether anyone can change it, and its caveat. Section references are to [SPEC.md](SPEC.md). Facts with sources are in [research/FACTS.md](research/FACTS.md), cited as "FACTS #n" or by the title of a follow-up section.

**Read the column "Can it change?" with this in mind.** Until a Noised program's upgrade authority is set to None, its holder can replace that program's code. Every row enforced by a `noised_*` program is then only as strong as that key. The answers below describe the state **after finalization**. Check it with `solana program show <program id>`: the line `Authority:` must read `none`.

This revision describes the final programs: post-quantum v2 with the audit-round-2 fixes, the on-chain metadata change (2026-10-09, safety policy 3; [AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md) §8 to §12), and programmable launches with the review-4 fixes (2026-10-11). Round 1 and its follow-up 1b (2026-10-08) shaped rows T5, L1 to L5, L7, V1 to V6 and section 6; round 2 changed L1, L2, L4, V2 and the trust table; PQ v2 added section 8 and the payout-owner rows; the on-chain metadata change added T3's current form and section 9. Audit round 3 (2026-10-10, [AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md) §14) corrected A7's caveat, added L8, and changed A4 and L8 through two program fixes, F1 and F2, which are merged. Programmable launches (P1c: creator launch rules in the Launch Guard, `publish_rules`, attestation message v3, certificate v4, `pq_authorize` and the Autopilot program) added sections 10 and 11 and changed G4, G5, L6, L7, A1, A2, A5, A6 and the trust table. Review 4 ([AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md) §15) fixed R1 (holders' exits through the vault or shield skip the creator rules), R3 (sells and the guard's end are decided from the policy's head), Ra (authorizations only of kinds 1 to 3), Rb (what only the rules use is read only when rules run), R5 (a freeze expires at its signed `not_after`), R6a (`launch` hashes the rules record again) and R6b (the autopilot reads only known launchpad account versions); all are merged. Audit round 4 ([AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md) §16, 2026-10-11) found no exploitable issue in the launch path, the vault, the Shield or `noised-lmots`, and changed AP1, AP3, AP4, AP5 and AP6 through merged autopilot fixes (partial keeper buybacks, revocations that name their activation, no tips for burns before graduation, distributions that skip recipients without an account); its client fixes, among them A2's leaf choice, are done (SDK 0.9.0, `@noisedlabs/mcp` 0.2.0 and the app). Every test named below passed on 2026-10-11 on fresh SBPF v3 builds of all five programs with those fixes: the host tests (323 in the root workspace, 81 in `crates/noised-rules`), and the LiteSVM suites `tests/integration` (113 tests), `programs/noised-launchpad/tests/litesvm` (84), `programs/noised-shield/tests/litesvm` (19) and `programs/noised-autopilot/tests/litesvm` (36), which run our builds against the mainnet program dumps. Nothing is deployed on mainnet; four programs run on devnet (deployed fresh, upgradeable; [DEVNET.md](DEVNET.md)), and mainnet will be deployed fresh too.

## 2. Launch fairness

| # | Guarantee | Enforced by | Can it change? | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Nobody, the creator included, can buy from the pool before the batch. The only exception is the batch's single purchase, and the batch is always the pool's first swap or it refunds. | `noised_hook` refuses every transfer out of the DBC base vault before `snipe_shield_end_ts`, whatever its amount and wherever it goes, unless the transaction's current top-level instruction is the launchpad's `execute_batch`, read from the instructions sysvar (`lib.rs::inside_execute_batch`). The shield is scheduled to end at launch + commit window + 600 s; `execute_batch` ends it at the moment of the batch through `end_snipe_shield`, which can only move the end earlier and only the launchpad's `execute_batch` can call, for its own launch. `execute_batch` makes exactly one swap. `rules::evaluate` checks the shield before the zero-amount pass and before exemptions, so a buy that receives nothing (DBC 0.2.1 executes one, and it still moves the price and the volatility fee) and a buy into an account owned by the batch-authority or launch-authority PDA are refused too. A transfer out of the base vault is never treated as a sell. Second layer: `execute_batch` requires the pool's `has_swap == 0` (`PoolAlreadyTraded`), so a buy that ever got past the hook makes the batch refund in full instead of buying at a moved price or fee. Host tests: `only_the_batch_buys_during_the_shield` (200,000 cases, zero amounts included), `zero_amount_buys_wait_for_the_shield`, `a_transfer_out_of_the_base_vault_is_never_a_sell`, `exemptions_never_open_the_snipe_shield`, `snipe_shield_blocks_only_buys_during_countdown`, `the_shield_end_only_moves_earlier`, `ending_the_shield_only_opens_buys_from_the_batch_on`; `hook_recognizes_execute_batch` pins the discriminator. LiteSVM against mainnet DBC: `l2_exempt_owner_accounts_cannot_buy_through_the_snipe_shield`, `l2b_zero_output_buys_cannot_cross_the_snipe_shield`, `l2c_batch_must_be_the_first_swap`, `l2d_dbc_refuses_a_buy_whose_output_is_the_base_vault`, `r2_the_batch_ends_the_snipe_shield_in_its_own_slot`, `r2_the_batch_never_moves_the_shield_end_later`, `r2_only_the_launchpad_ends_the_shield_and_only_its_own`; under mainnet Token-2022: `zero_amount_buys_wait_for_the_snipe_shield`, `only_a_buy_inside_execute_batch_passes_the_snipe_shield`, `end_snipe_shield_runs_only_inside_execute_batch`. | No. The policy is written once, only by the launchpad; the only later change is the shield end moving earlier at the batch. | The hook sees a buy only as the Token-2022 transfer DBC makes for it. DBC 0.2.1 makes one for every buy, even one that pays out nothing, and refuses a buy whose output account is the base vault itself (error 2040), a self-transfer Token-2022 would never pass to the hook. A DBC upgrade that changed either would let such a buy run unseen; the first-swap check then turns the batch into a full refund (THREAT_MODEL R16). `execute_batch` must be a top-level instruction: a CPI'd call has its buy refused. A DBC upgrade, or an operator revoke if one is ever added, could remove the hook early (T5); even then the batch never buys after another swap. |
| L2 | The batch buys once, at one price, and shares out pro rata. Committing early, even inside the launch transaction, gives no advantage, and the creator cannot shrink the batch to dust. | `commit` never refuses a commit for the total cap, only above `batch_wallet_cap`. `execute_batch`: one DBC `swap2_with_transfer_hook` of `min(total_committed, batch_total_cap)`, requiring `quote_spent <=` that amount. `claim` pays `floor(tokens_out * commit / total)` tokens plus `floor((total - spent) * commit / total)` wSOL (`math.rs::batch_share`), so an oversubscribed batch is filled pro rata. Cap floors (round 2): a non-zero `batch_total_cap` is at least a tenth of the threshold, and a non-zero `batch_wallet_cap` at least the resolved total cap / 200 (`policy.rs::validate_batch_caps`, `InvalidBatchCap`). LiteSVM against mainnet DBC: `fair_open_batch_end_to_end`, `l1_creator_filling_the_cap_in_the_launch_transaction_is_scaled_pro_rata`, `l1_creator_overcommitting_in_the_launch_transaction_gets_exactly_its_pro_rata_share`, `l1_batch_exactly_at_the_cap_spends_everything`, `l1_undersubscribed_batch_spends_the_whole_total`, `r2_batch_cap_floors_and_minute_windows_hold_at_create_launch`. | No. | Rounding dust (under one base unit per participant per asset) stays in the escrows forever. A participant with more capital gets a proportionally larger share: a creator whose commit inside the launch transaction is 80% of everything committed gets 80% of the batch, at the same price as everyone else, and the excess back (`l1_creator_overcommitting_…`). `batch_wallet_cap` is per wallet, so it only limits naive concentration. A batch too small to buy anything fails with `EmptySwap`, then expires and refunds in full (`l1_a_batch_too_small_to_buy_anything_refunds_in_full`). The SDK's verifier reports the caps against the threshold (SPEC §4.1). Exclusion by write-lock contention: see L4 and "Residuals no rule removes". |
| L3 | Batch payouts never exceed what the batch holds, each commit is paid once and only to the payout owners its first commit fixed, and nobody but those owners can block a claim. | `Commit.claimed`; running totals `tokens_claimed` and `quote_refunded` in `Launch`; error `PayoutExceedsEscrow`. `commit` fixes `token_owner` and `refund_owner` at the first commit (the committer or its PQ vaults of the right mint, `InvalidPayoutOwner`), and `claim` pays only their canonical ATAs, validated with `AtaCreator::ensure`: canonical address, created if missing, owner field not compared. LiteSVM: `l3_claim_pays_a_reassigned_wsol_ata_and_creates_a_missing_base_ata`, `l3_expired_refund_pays_a_reassigned_or_closed_wsol_ata`, `l3_prefunded_ata_addresses_block_nothing_and_memos_block_only_their_owner`, `a7_payouts_go_to_the_owners_fixed_at_the_first_commit`, `a7_an_expired_batch_refunds_into_a_wsol_vault`. | No. | **A claim pays the canonical wSOL ATA wherever its owner field points.** Anyone can crank a claim, and it does not compare the owner field. A committer that is its own refund owner and reassigned its SPL Token wSOL ATA to another owner, or was phished into reassigning it (an owner-change drainer's `SetAuthority`), gets its refund in that account, which the new owner controls. The base tokens still reach the committer: its base-token ATA is a Token-2022 account, whose owner cannot be reassigned. A PQ vault never reassigns its token account. The project chose this so that nobody but the owner can block a claim; before audit round 1 a reassigned ATA stranded both tokens and refund forever. Tokens and the refund are paid in one instruction, so a committer that makes its own account unable to receive (for example by requiring memos on its Token-2022 ATA) blocks its own claim, refund included, until it undoes that. |
| L4 | If the batch does not execute within its 10-minute window, every commit is refunded in full. That includes a batch that cannot execute because the pool traded before it, or because it buys nothing. | `claim` expired path (SPEC §1.4). `execute_batch` runs only as the pool's first swap (`PoolAlreadyTraded`) and only if it receives tokens (`EmptySwap`). LiteSVM: `expired_batch_refunds_in_full`, `l2c_batch_must_be_the_first_swap`, `l1_a_batch_too_small_to_buy_anything_refunds_in_full`, `r2_without_the_batch_the_shield_ends_on_schedule`. | No. | **Anyone can force this outcome by paying for it.** Every `execute_batch` write-locks the `Launch` account and the pool, and a block admits at most 24M CU of transactions writing one account. A party that fills that budget ahead of the keepers for the whole window keeps the batch out (audit round 2, finding C8: about 0.012 SOL for the old 30-second window against keepers that bid nothing). The 600-second window makes that cost linear in time, about 20 times higher (an estimate), and keepers' priority fees raise it further; it does not remove it. Block producers and ordering gateways can do it without paying. Public trading then opens without a batch buy. |
| L5 | No creator allocation, vesting, first-swap privilege or head start. | `inspect_config`: `locked_vesting` all zero (flag 256), `enable_first_swap_with_min_fee == 0`, creator fee share 0. The pool is created by CPI, so DBC's first-swap exemption cannot apply. The creator cannot buy before the batch (L1), and a commit bundled with `launch` is pro-rated like any other (L2). LiteSVM: `launch_is_certified_and_the_mint_is_safe`, `l1_creator_filling_the_cap_in_the_launch_transaction_is_scaled_pro_rata`. | No. | The creator can still commit to the batch, with as much capital and as many wallets as it likes, and trade like anyone else. |
| L6 | Every launch rule, built-in or the creator's (section 10), ends at `guard_end_ts`, at most 72 h after launch. | Bounds in both programs (`MAX_GUARD_SECS`). Creator rules verify only if every window lies inside the guard (`to_rel <= min(guard_secs, 259,200)`; `RuleSet::verify` in `publish_rules` and again in `create_policy`). `rules::built_ins` allows everything once `now >= guard_end_ts`, before the creator rules run, and `execute` decides it from the policy's head before decoding the rest of the policy (review 4, R3; CR2). Tests: the 200,000-case property test `everything_is_allowed_after_the_guard`; LiteSVM `everything_is_allowed_once_the_guard_ends`, `sells_and_the_guard_end_never_decode_the_whole_policy`, `rules_never_touch_sells_claims_the_batch_or_after_the_guard`. | No. | Creator rules can refuse buys and wallet-to-wallet transfers for up to the whole guard (CR1). Max-wallet and max-buy are per token account and per transfer. Many wallets get around them. Max-wallet also applies to accounts many people pay into: during the guard, a holder of about max-wallet tokens can fill the Shield's escrow for the mint (one per mint), after which every wrap of that token fails until the guard ends, or top up someone's canonical token account to max-wallet, after which that account's buys and vault `release` payouts into it fail until then (audit round 3, info; griefing only, at most 72 h, nothing lost). |
| L7 | A sell into the pool is never refused by the hook. | `execute` decides it from the policy's fixed head (`state::PolicyHead`: discriminator, length of at least 130 bytes, mint at offset 10, base vault at 74, guard end at 122) before decoding the rest of the policy (review 4, R3): destination = the policy's DBC base vault, from any source other than the base vault itself, means allow (a transfer out of the base vault is a buy, never a sell). It is decided before any token-account data is read and before the policy's later fields, its creator rules included, are decoded, so nothing in them can block a sell. `rules::built_ins` applies the same rule first, and creator rules never see a sell (CR2). Tests: the 200,000-case property test `sells_are_never_blocked`; host `a_version_1_or_any_longer_policy_has_the_same_head`, `the_head_is_read_at_the_policys_offsets`, `a_rule_that_refuses_everything_spares_sells_claims_and_the_batch`; LiteSVM `sells_into_the_base_vault_are_never_blocked` under the mainnet Token-2022 binary and `sells_and_the_guard_end_never_decode_the_whole_policy`. | No. | A sell can still fail for reasons outside the hook: slippage limits, missing accounts in a client, a DBC or Token-2022 upgrade, or a completed curve (DBC rejects swaps between completion and migration). |
| L8 | With the optional wallet-to-wallet lock on, tokens of a launch cannot move between holders' token accounts until `guard_end_ts`: they can only be bought from the pool, sold into it, or moved to and from the launch's own exempt accounts (batch claims). Whether or not the lock is on, during the guard a transfer that is not a sell lands only in a token account whose owner can never change, so no account a position reaches can be handed to another owner. | The hook refuses every transfer that is neither a buy nor a sell while the lock is on (`TransfersLocked`, `rules::evaluate`; host `p2p_lock_blocks_wallet_transfers_but_not_buys`, LiteSVM `wallet_to_wallet_transfers_follow_the_p2p_lock`). Changing a token account's owner moves no tokens, so the hook would never see it; since audit round 3's F2 the hook therefore refuses, while the guard is on, any transfer that is not a sell (and moves tokens) into a token account without Token-2022's `ImmutableOwner` extension (`MutableOwner`, 6010), unless an exempt launchpad PDA owns it; the check runs before the exempt-source pass, the lock and the caps. Host: `decision_table_covers_every_branch`, `the_immutable_owner_rule_only_refuses_mutable_destinations`, `immutable_owner_is_read_as_token_2022_reads_it`; integration: `receiving_accounts_need_an_immutable_owner_during_the_guard`; LiteSVM against mainnet DBC: `f2_buys_and_transfers_need_an_immutable_owner_while_the_guard_is_on`, `f2_launch_payouts_land_only_where_the_owner_cannot_change`. | No. | **The lock stops token transfers, not changes of control.** These need no transfer and no owner change, so no hook sees them: a wallet several people control (a multisig, a smart wallet, a shared key); a token account owned by a PDA or smart wallet whose own program hands control to someone else; a PQ vault whose holder rotates its key to another party's key (`withdraw` with `max_amount = 0`), after which only that party can withdraw what the vault holds; a delegate approval, which shares control and lets the delegate sell during the guard. Associated token accounts always have `ImmutableOwner`, so ordinary wallets are unaffected; a buy, a `release_to`, an unwrap or a transfer into a hand-made account without it waits for the guard to end, and so does a route that passes the tokens through such an account (some aggregators). Claims from the batch are paid out of an exempt escrow into canonical ATAs, and PQ-vault payouts are wallet-to-wallet transfers that wait for the guard to end under the lock. |

## 5. Governance and code

| # | Guarantee | Enforced by | Can it change? | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | No admin, pause, privileged withdrawal or settings update exists. | Program code of all five programs: no such instructions (for the autopilot, AP7). No launchpad PDA and no autopilot account is ever closed. | Only by an upgrade before finalization. | |

## 6. PQ vault (v4)

| # | Guarantee | Enforced by | Can it change? | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Residuals no rule removes

These hold whatever the programs do, and are listed so nobody mistakes them for guarantees.

- **The first transaction in a window wins.** Inside an attested launch window anyone may send `launch`, and inside the commit window everyone commits on equal terms. Likewise whoever lands first runs a due autopilot node and takes its tip, or applies a due graph update (`apply_pending`). Who lands first is decided by block producers and the priority fees transactions pay.
- **Rules can make buying insider-only for up to the guard.** Creator rules may restrict buys to listed wallets (a private sale) for up to 72 h; every holder can still sell. This is disclosed by the explanation and the verifier's and badge's warnings, not prevented (CR1; THREAT_MODEL R33).
- **Withheld authorizations.** Once an `Authorization` has landed, anyone may use it at any later time it is still valid: an activation only while its signed `activate_at` is still far enough ahead for the timelock, a freeze only until its signed `not_after` (since review 4), a revocation at any time (it only removes power). A signed authorization that has not landed can be held back by whoever relays it, and authorizations are ordered by landing slot, not signing time. Clients land their own through several RPCs (AP3; THREAT_MODEL R38).
- **A relayer can delay an attestation, an authorization, a commit or a withdrawal, but not change it.** Everything that matters is signed; holding a transaction back is always possible for whoever sees it first.
- **One device per quantum key, unless you import its records** (best practice). A one-time leaf is safe only if the client remembers every signature it revealed. Two devices that do not share their records can each sign at the same leaf (about 1 in 1,024 per pair of messages when one device's signature never landed). Since audit round 4 a dishonest RPC can no longer steer them into it (SDK 0.9.0 and the app; A2), but nothing on-chain can know what another device signed.
- **A buyback's price bound needs an honest observation.** If every observation in a buyback's window comes from one party, the bound can be stale (AP5). Keepers observe before every buyback, and an independent observer every 10 minutes or less closes the gap.
- **Write-lock contention.** Agave (4.3) admits at most 24,000,000 CU per block of transactions that write one account (`MAX_WRITABLE_ACCOUNT_UNITS`), charges each transaction its requested compute-unit limit when it packs a block, and orders transactions by `fee reward × 1,000,000 / (1 + cost)` (FACTS, PQ v2 facts). Every commit writes the `Launch` account and the batch escrow, every `execute_batch` writes the `Launch` account and the pool, and every `launch` writes the `Launch` account (the only one of its writable accounts that does not derive from the fresh mint). A party that fills those budgets with higher-priority transactions keeps others out while it pays. Round 2 estimated about 0.0035 SOL per 10 seconds against commits with no priority fee (finding C7) and about 0.012 SOL for a 30-second execution window (finding C8). The minimum 60-second commit window and the 600-second execution window make the cost grow linearly with time; measured compute limits and priority fees set by the SDK and by keepers raise it further. It is not removed: the batch then expires and refunds in full (L4), commits are excluded (L2), or an attested launch window passes unused (A4: the creator then signs a new window; before audit round 3's F1, that launch could never go live).
- **Control can change without a transfer.** The Launch Guard, creator rules included, sees token transfers only. A wallet several people control, a PDA or smart-wallet owner, a PQ vault's key rotated to another party, or a delegate approval moves control of a position during the guard without one (L8).
- **Trusted parties outside Noised** (next section): Solana consensus, Token-2022, the ATA program, the Meteora DBC and DAMM v2 upgrade authorities and operators.
- **Wallets' classical keys.** Every Ed25519 wallet, the creator's included, holds its tokens and SOL under a key a quantum computer could recover. Only what sits in PQ vaults is post-quantum custody.

## Superintelligence-proof, interpreted

No system can be proven safe against an arbitrarily capable adversary, and Noised makes no such claim. We take the goal to mean **leave a capable attacker nothing to work with**:

| Principle | How Noised applies it | Where it stops |
|---|---|---|
| No secrets to steal | After `launch`, every privileged role is a PDA, an address with no private key; so is an autopilot's treasury. No Noised key can mint, freeze, rename, take liquidity or redirect fees. | Fee recipients that are ordinary wallets and user wallets are ordinary keys, and so is an autopilot's operator (bounded, revocable); the creator's identity phrase or passkey is a secret for the launches it has not attested yet and for the graph changes of its launches' autopilots. |
| No admins to phish or coerce | No admin instructions, no pause, no settings updates. `Protocol` is written once. | Until finalization, the upgrade authority is exactly such a target. Meteora's multisig and operators are too. |
| Immutable code | Our programs are finalized after audit, with reproducible, verified builds. | Meteora, Token-2022 and the ATA program stay upgradeable, and feature gates can change the runtime. |
| Rules anyone can check | The certificate records the checks performed at launch and the hash of the creator rules the hook enforces. `verifyLaunch` re-checks the live accounts and re-verifies the launch's post-quantum signature. Every rule is a public, deterministic program path. | Checking needs an honest view of the chain: use more than one RPC. |

## Verify it yourself

You do not need the Noised website to check a launch.

```bash
# 1. Mint: authorities and extensions
spl-token display <MINT> --url mainnet-beta
#    expect: no mint authority, no freeze authority, extensions MetadataPointer + TokenMetadata + TransferHook,
#    metadata update authority none; transfer hook program = cHHLLsEH4eHmqZBT4X5XqzutmkFw3daWSySPtV4csia
#    before graduation (authority FhVo3mqL8PW5pH5U2CN4XE33DokiyZnUwuGpH2hmHLuM), none after.

# 2. Certificate, hook policy and attestation addresses
solana find-program-derived-address CfbRmsFpevhiXkbYK48orBDugdKznnezDiVekjAAbNgc string:certificate pubkey:<MINT>
solana find-program-derived-address cHHLLsEH4eHmqZBT4X5XqzutmkFw3daWSySPtV4csia string:policy pubkey:<MINT>
solana find-program-derived-address CfbRmsFpevhiXkbYK48orBDugdKznnezDiVekjAAbNgc string:attestation pubkey:<LAUNCH>
#    a mint with no certificate account at that address is NOT a Noised launch.
#    The certificate's `checks` (bytes 187..191) must satisfy (checks & 4095) == 4095: it is 4,095 without creator
#    rules and 8,191 with them (bit 4096, RULES_BOUND, is informational). Never test checks == 4095.
#    Its `metadata` (bytes 455..487) names the TokenMeta record and its `rules_hash` (bytes 487..519, version 4)
#    the creator rules, all zero for none; the attestation holds the full LMS signature.
solana find-program-derived-address CfbRmsFpevhiXkbYK48orBDugdKznnezDiVekjAAbNgc string:rules pubkey:<LAUNCH> hex:<RULES_HASH>
#    the rules record: its bytes must equal the rules the hook policy holds (u32 length at 275, bytes from 279).

# 3. Autopilot, if the launch has one
solana find-program-derived-address D5fuqCXNP7BkU3CyNrpZUFStgdZ6XwRkpURiK9v5esbx string:autopilot pubkey:<LAUNCH>
solana find-program-derived-address D5fuqCXNP7BkU3CyNrpZUFStgdZ6XwRkpURiK9v5esbx string:treasury pubkey:<LAUNCH>
#    the Autopilot names its active graph, whose record is ["graph", launch, graph_hash] under the same program;
#    each graph change used an Authorization ["auth", launch, [kind], payload_hash] of the launchpad that holds
#    the full LMS signature. The running graph's own one is named in the Autopilot account: active_auth (bytes
#    1076..1108) must be ["auth", launch, [1], SHA256("PHX-GRAPH-ACT-V1" || autopilot || active_graph ||
#    i64le active_activate_at (bytes 1108..1116))].

# 4. Who can still change the code
solana program show CfbRmsFpevhiXkbYK48orBDugdKznnezDiVekjAAbNgc --url mainnet-beta   # Authority: none (after finalization)
solana program show cHHLLsEH4eHmqZBT4X5XqzutmkFw3daWSySPtV4csia --url mainnet-beta
solana program show D5fuqCXNP7BkU3CyNrpZUFStgdZ6XwRkpURiK9v5esbx --url mainnet-beta   # autopilot, if the launch uses one
solana program show dbcij3LWUppWqq96dh6gJWwBifmcGfLSB5D4DuSMaqN --url mainnet-beta   # Meteora: upgradeable, note "Last Deployed In Slot"
```

For the full check, run the SDK: `verifyLaunch(connection, mint)`. It decodes the certificate and re-checks every flag against live state. It also checks the DBC config fields, the hook policy, its creator rules against the certificate's `rules_hash` (`report.rules`, with a plain-English explanation and warnings) and the batch caps, re-verifies the attestation's LMS signature over the message rebuilt from on-chain data, reports the current phase, and after migration confirms the DAMM v2 position lock, the hook revocation and the pool's fee against its canonical config. Finally, it lists the third parties above with the current upgrade slots of DBC and DAMM v2.

The QSL-1 standard ([standard/QSL-1.md](standard/QSL-1.md), draft) writes these checks down as a verification algorithm with conformance levels, so any wallet, explorer or indexer can implement them without the SDK. The SDK's checker for it is `checkQsl1(connection, mint)` (SDK 0.5.0, unreleased): it confirms the RPC serves mainnet, reads the accounts the standard names at `finalized` commitment (never a URL), re-checks every condition from them, never from the certificate's flags, and returns the level, every check with a plain-English sentence, why the next level is not reached, and who you still trust. `qslBadge(result)` turns that into the badge and the wording the standard allows. Until the programs are finalized, no token can reach a QSL-1 level, and on devnet none ever does: the checker reports at most "candidate".

Programmable is not a level. The QSL-1 levels stay L1 No-rug and L2 Quantum-attested. A launch with creator rules or an autopilot is shown with its level and the descriptive tag "programmable — read its rules" (for example "L2 · programmable — read its rules"), never as safer: rules can only refuse transfers (CR1), but they can make buying insider-only for the guard, and a graph spends a treasury on the creator's terms. Since SDK 0.8.0 (unreleased) the SDK's checker and badge follow this (review 4, R2), with the rules explained in plain English and warnings on buys restricted to a key list, rules on wallet-to-wallet transfers, rules active for more than an hour and vacuous rules.
