# Noised — engineering specification

Noised is **the quantum security layer for Solana**: on-chain programs and an SDK, on top of Token-2022 and Meteora, that put hash-based post-quantum signatures where they decide whether a token launches and who can move what a Noised vault holds. Solana itself, ordinary wallets and the pools stay classical ([POST_QUANTUM.md](POST_QUANTUM.md) §8). It has four products and one standard:

| Product | What it is | Here |
|---|---|---|
| **Launch** | A launchpad on Meteora where **nobody can rug a launch, not even its creator**, and where **every launch must be signed by its creator's post-quantum identity before it can go live**. Optionally programmable: creator launch rules in the Launch Guard and an Autopilot for the launch's treasury. | §1, the launchpad and the Launch Guard hook; §1.9 rules; §9 Autopilot |
| **Vault** | Token custody that only a post-quantum LM-OTS signature can move. | §3 |
| **Shield** | Opt-in private balances: a 1:1 wrapper into a Token-2022 confidential-balance token. | §2 |
| **Verify** | An independent check of any Noised token against live chain state: the SDK's `verifyLaunch` (§4.1), and since SDK 0.5 (unreleased) `checkQsl1`, which reports the mint's QSL-1 level by the standard's verification algorithm. | §4 |
| **QSL-1** | The token standard these programs implement: a named, versioned profile on Token-2022 that anyone can check from one account per mint ([standard/QSL-1.md](standard/QSL-1.md), draft). | [docs/standard/](standard/) |

**Programmable launches** (P1c, merged 2026-10-11): a creator may publish launch rules (PXR1) that the Launch Guard enforces on top of its built-in checks while the guard is on (§1.9), and may run the launch's treasury through an Autopilot graph (PXG1, §9). The launch's post-quantum identity signs both (attestation v3, `pq_authorize`). Programmable is a disclosed feature, not a higher safety level: the QSL-1 levels stay L1 No-rug and L2 Quantum-attested, and a programmable launch carries the descriptive tag "programmable — read its rules" next to its level ([standard/QSL-1.md](standard/QSL-1.md)).

This document is the implementation contract. It is the source of truth for program interfaces, account layouts, the safety policy and the invariants that audits check. Background facts (with sources) are in `docs/research/FACTS.md`.

Words used precisely below: **must** = enforced on-chain; **should** = client/SDK behaviour.

Protocol state described here: the five programs as merged after the post-quantum v2 work, the on-chain metadata change (2026-10-09), audit round 3's program fixes F1 and F2 (merged 2026-10-10; they changed no layout and added hook error 6010), and the programmable launches of P1c with the review-4 fixes R1, R3, Ra, Rb, R5, R6a and R6b (merged 2026-10-11): safety policy version 3 (unchanged), `Launch` version 2, `Attestation` version 3, `Certificate` version 4, `TokenMeta` version 1, `LaunchRules` version 1, `Authorization` version 1, `HookPolicy` version 2, `Autopilot` version 1, vault version 4 with key-index binding. Nothing is deployed on mainnet. On devnet, four of the programs (launchpad, hook, autopilot, vault) were deployed fresh on 2026-10-12 from the final builds of this state ([DEVNET.md](DEVNET.md)); mainnet will be deployed fresh too (no in-place upgrade over a version-1 hook policy or a version-2 attestation). Client API: `@noisedlabs/sdk` 0.3 (§4.1), with the additions of SDK 0.4.0 to 0.8.0 (all unreleased) marked as such.

## 0. Summary of the design

| Layer | What it does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Launchpad program | Creates a Meteora DBC transfer-hook config with fixed safe values. Stores the token's metadata on-chain (`publish_metadata`). Verifies, on-chain, a hash-based LMS signature by the creator's post-quantum identity over the launch's configuration, metadata record and launch window (`attest_launch`), and launches the pool only with that attestation and only inside that window, permissionlessly. Runs a pro-rata **fair-open batch**, routes all fees by immutable rules, and writes an on-chain **launch certificate**. Holds every DBC role (creator, partner, LP owner) through PDAs, so no private key controls a launch after it starts. Stores optional creator launch rules (`publish_rules`), which the attestation signs and `launch` installs, and lands the launch identity's other post-quantum authorizations (`pq_authorize`) for the Autopilot. | `programs/noised-launchpad` |
| Launch Guard hook | Token-2022 transfer hook that is active only while the bonding curve runs (Meteora DBC revokes it at curve completion). Enforces the snipe shield (until the batch buys, the only buy that passes is the batch buy), max-buy, max-wallet, an optional wallet-to-wallet lock and, while the guard is on, receiving accounts whose owner can never change, all with hard expiry. Sells are never blocked. A policy may also hold the creator's launch rules (PXR1), checked after every built-in decision: they can only refuse more transfers during the guard, never a sell, a claim, the batch buy or a holder's exit through the Noised vault or shield (§1.9). | `programs/noised-hook` |
| PQ vault | Token vaults that only a post-quantum LM-OTS signature can open (RFC 8554, `LMOTS_SHA256_N32_W4`; the parameter set is also listed in NIST SP 800-208, see [POST_QUANTUM.md](POST_QUANTUM.md) §3 for what that does and does not mean). A signed withdrawal reserves a payout and rotates the key in place; delivery is a separate step. Withdrawals use v1 (4 KiB) transactions. | `programs/noised-vault`, `crates/noised-lmots` (RFC 8554 KAT-verified), `sdk/src/pq/lmots.ts` |
| PQ creator identity | An LMS key (`LMS_SHA256_M32_H10` over `LMOTS_SHA256_N32_W4`, 1,024 one-time leaves) derived from a dedicated phrase or a passkey PRF secret, never from the wallet. Its used-leaf bitmap is on-chain, shared by attestations and authorizations. | `crates/noised-lmots` (`lms`), launchpad `Identity`, `Attestation` and `Authorization` accounts, `sdk/src/pq/{lms,identity,leafLedger,attest,authorize}.ts` |
| Autopilot | Post-launch automation of a launch's treasury: PXG1 graphs (buy back and burn, burn, pay declared recipients) that anyone can run when due, within bounds the chain enforces. Graph changes need the launch identity's LMS authorization and a 72-hour timelock; no admin, no pause; it cannot touch the launch, its LP, its hook policy or its mint's authorities (§9). | `programs/noised-autopilot` |
| Rules crate | `no_std` formats and evaluators shared by the hook, the launchpad and the autopilot (and mirrored by the SDK): PXR1 launch rules, PXG1 graphs and their condition VM. Its README is normative for both byte formats. | `crates/noised-rules` |
| Shield | Opt-in privacy: wrap any token 1:1 into a "zToken" (Token-2022 with Confidential Balances) to hide balances and amounts; unwrap to trade. | `programs/noised-shield` |
| SDK | TypeScript client: launch builders and the planned launch flow, independent on-chain verifier, PQ identities and vaults with durable one-signature ledgers, shield client, programmable launches (rules, graphs, autopilot builders, keepers). | `sdk/` |
| App | Static Vite + React frontend (IPFS-deployable, strict CSP). | `app/` |

> **Change note (post-quantum scheme).** The vault originally used WOTS+C (n = 24, NIST category 3). It now uses LM-OTS `LMOTS_SHA256_N32_W4` because v1 transactions (SIMD-0385, 4,096 bytes) are live on mainnet since 2026-09-15, which makes the standardized parameter set practical: ~70k CU to verify instead of ~300k, published RFC test vectors, and a per-signature randomizer. The superseded `crates/noised-wots` has been removed from the repository.

> **Change note (audit round 1, 2026-10-08).** Vault v4 (§3). The snipe shield now admits only the buy made inside the launchpad's top-level `execute_batch`, read from the instructions sysvar; exempt owners no longer open it (§1.3, HOOKS.md). The batch's total cap no longer refuses commits: an oversubscribed batch is filled pro rata (§1.4). `claim` validates the payout ATAs like `distribute` does, without comparing their owner field (§1.4).

> **Change note (audit round 1b, 2026-10-08).** Zero-amount buys no longer pass the hook during the shield: DBC 0.2.1 executes a buy whose output rounds to zero, and such a buy still moves the price and the volatility fee. Every transfer out of the DBC base vault is a buy, whatever its amount and wherever it goes (§1.3, invariants 3 and 12). `execute_batch` runs only as the pool's first swap, or the batch refunds (`PoolAlreadyTraded`, §1.4, invariant 13). The LM-OTS randomizer is `C = HMAC-SHA256(key = SEED, data = "phalanx/pq/lmots/C" || I || u32be(q) || msg)` (§3.10). Details: [AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md), round 1b.

> **Change note (PQ v2 and audit round 2, 2026-10-09; safety policy 2).** Every launch must be attested by its creator's post-quantum identity before it can go live (`attest_launch`, `Identity`, `Attestation`, certificate bit `PQ_ATTESTED`). The attestation signs a launch window, and `launch` is permissionless inside it and refused outside it. The `Launch` PDA is `["launch", creator, launch_seed]`. Commits fix their payout owners (the committer or its PQ vaults) at the first commit; what one vault receives is held to one wallet's cap (`PayoutVault`). The treasury must be a wSOL PQ vault. Round-2 fixes: `MIN_OPEN_DELAY` 10 → 60 s, `EXECUTION_GRACE_SECS` 30 → 600 s with the shield ended by the batch itself (`noised_hook::end_snipe_shield`), batch-cap floors, and the vault's key-index binding (`InvalidKeyIndex`). Details: [AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md) §8-§10.

> **Change note (on-chain metadata, 2026-10-09; safety policy 3).** A token's name, symbol, uri mode and metadata document (description, icon) are an on-chain record (`TokenMeta`, `publish_metadata`) at the address its content hashes to. The attestation signs that hash (message v2, 189 bytes); `launch()` takes no arguments, derives the mint's metadata from the record and checks it after the pool CPI (certificate bit `METADATA_ONCHAIN`, `Certificate.metadata`). By default the mint's uri is empty. Details: [AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md) §11.

> **Change note (audit round 3, 2026-10-10; merged).** F1: while a launch is `Configured`, an attestation whose window passed without a `launch` is replaced by the next valid attestation of the launch's identity, and an attestation is recorded only with at least 60 s of its window left when it lands (§1.4 `attest_launch`). Before, a missed window, or a held attestation landed in its window's last second, left the launch unable ever to go live. F2: while the guard is on, the hook refuses a transfer that is not a sell into a token account without Token-2022's `ImmutableOwner` extension (`MutableOwner`, hook error 6010), unless an exempt launchpad PDA owns the account, because an account's owner can otherwise be reassigned with no transfer, which the wallet-to-wallet lock and max-wallet never see (§1.3). F3 (clients only, SDK 0.4.0) and F4 (documentation) change no program. Details, tests and mutation results: [AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md) §14.

> **Change note (P1c programmable launches and review 4, 2026-10-11).** `publish_rules` writes a creator's PXR1 rule set at the address its content hashes to; the attestation signs that hash (message v3, 221 bytes; `Attestation` v3, 2,708 bytes); `launch` (23 accounts) re-hashes the record and installs exactly its bytes in the hook policy (`HookPolicy` v2), where they run after every built-in decision and can only refuse (§1.9); `Certificate` v4 (519 bytes) records `rules_hash` and the informational bit `RULES_BOUND` (4096), so every client tests `(checks & 4095) == 4095`. `pq_authorize` lands authorizations signed by the launch identity (kinds 1 to 3), which the new `noised_autopilot` program consumes (§9). Review-4 fixes: R1 holders' exits through the Noised vault or shield skip the creator rules; R3 sells and everything after the guard are decided from the policy's fixed head; Rb the supply and the instructions sysvar are read only for transfers the rules decide; Ra kinds other than 1 to 3 only burn their leaf; R6a `launch` re-hashes the rules record; R6b the autopilot reads launchpad accounts only at known versions; R5 a freeze authorization names its `not_after` (payload v2, `FreezeExpired`). Details: [AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md) §15.

## 1. Launchpad program (`noised_launchpad`)

### 1.1 Roles and PDAs

All seeds are byte strings followed by the listed keys.

| PDA | Seeds | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `Protocol` | `["protocol"]` | Global, immutable after init: treasury (a wSOL PQ vault) and platform fee share. |
| `Launch` | `["launch", creator, launch_seed[32]]` | Per-launch state (version 2, 585 bytes). `launch_seed` is 32 random bytes chosen by the client; the creator in the seeds means nobody can take another creator's address. |
| Launch authority | `["authority", launch]` | Signs as DBC **pool creator**, DBC **partner/fee_claimer**, DBC **leftover_receiver**, and owns the DAMM v2 LP position NFT after migration. Owns the fee vaults (wSOL ATA, base-token ATA). |
| Batch authority | `["batch", launch]` | Owns the fair-open batch escrows (wSOL ATA and base-token ATA). Pays for the batch swap. |
| DBC config | `["dbc-config", launch]` | The DBC `ConfigWithTransferHook` account, created by CPI with this PDA as the `config` signer. |
| Hook authority | `["hook-authority"]` | The only signer `noised_hook::create_policy` and `noised_hook::end_snipe_shield` accept. |
| `Certificate` | `["certificate", base_mint]` | Immutable launch certificate (version 4, 519 bytes). |
| `Commit` | `["commit", launch, owner]` | One participant's fair-open batch commitment and its payout owners (version 2, 147 bytes). |
| `PayoutVault` | `["payout-vault", launch, vault]` | What every commit naming the PQ vault `vault` as its token owner committed, together, held to `batch_wallet_cap` (version 1, 82 bytes; `committed: u64` at 74..82). |
| `Identity` | `["identity", SHA256(0x21 ‖ lms_pk)]` | A creator's post-quantum identity: its LMS public key (56 bytes) and the bitmap of its 1,024 one-time leaves (each accepted once, ever). 196 bytes. Created by the first of its signatures that lands (`attest_launch` or `pq_authorize`, which share its bitmap). |
| `Attestation` | `["attestation", launch]` | The launch's post-quantum attestation (`attest_launch`): written by the first valid signature of the launch's identity that `launch` can use (version 3, 2,708 bytes). Never closed. While the launch is `Configured` and the stored window has passed, the next attesting signature of the same identity replaces it whole (audit round 3, F1). A live launch's attestation never changes. |
| `TokenMeta` | `["metadata", launch, metadata_hash]` | A token's metadata record (`publish_metadata`), at the address its content hashes to. Anyone may publish records under a launch; only the one the attestation names (`Attestation.metadata_hash`, then `Certificate.metadata`) is the launch's. Readers resolve it through those, never by searching for records of a launch. |
| `LaunchRules` | `["rules", launch, rules_hash]` | A launch's creator rules (`publish_rules`, PXR1), at the address its content hashes to (`rules_hash = SHA256("PHX-RULES-V1" ‖ bytes)`). Version 1, `78 + len` bytes (at most 1,306). Like `TokenMeta`, anyone may publish records under a launch; only the one the attestation names (`Attestation.rules_hash`, then `Certificate.rules_hash`) is the launch's. |
| `Authorization` | `["auth", launch, [kind], payload_hash]` | A post-quantum authorization by the launch's identity (`pq_authorize`), written once (version 1, 2,669 bytes). The launchpad gives it no meaning; the autopilot reads kinds 1 to 3 by layout (§9). |

No PDA in this program is ever closed. There is no admin, no pause, no upgrade path in the program logic, and no instruction can change a `Launch`'s fee recipients, its hook policy or its DBC config after creation. The one later change to a hook policy is `execute_batch` ending its snipe shield at the batch (`noised_hook::end_snipe_shield`), which can only move the end earlier.

### 1.2 Safety policy (enforced in `create_launch`)

The creator chooses only the fields in `LaunchParams`. Every other DBC `ConfigParameters` field is fixed by the program.

**Fixed values (creators cannot change these):**

| DBC field | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| quote mint | wSOL | No freeze authority, no badge, no transfer fee. |
| `token_type` | 1 (Token-2022) | Required for transfer-hook configs. |
| `token_update_authority` | 1 (Immutable) | Metadata and pointers frozen; never 3/4 (those hand out **mint authority** on hook configs). |
| `migration_option` | 1 (DAMM v2) | Only supported target. |
| `collect_fee_mode` | 0 (quote only) | All fees in wSOL. Base tokens leave the DBC vault only through swaps, so the hook never sees fee transfers. |
| `activation_type` | 1 (timestamp) | Hook timing is in seconds. |
| `partner_permanent_locked_liquidity_percentage` | 100 | 100% of migrated LP permanently locked, held by the launch authority. |
| `partner_liquidity_percentage`, `creator_liquidity_percentage`, `creator_permanent_locked_liquidity_percentage` | 0 | No withdrawable LP for anyone. |
| `partner_liquidity_vesting_info`, `creator_liquidity_vesting_info` | all zero | No vesting LP. |
| `migration_fee` | `{fee_percentage: 0, creator_fee_percentage: 0}` | No creator or partner migration fee. DBC itself still keeps its fixed 0.2% protocol liquidity migration fee, and quote above the threshold (surplus) is split 80% to the launch authority (routed like fees) and 20% to Meteora (FACTS #28). |
| `creator_trading_fee_percentage` | 0 | All non-protocol fees go to the partner (= launch authority), then are routed by the fee policy. |
| `token_supply` | `None` (dynamic supply) | Unsold base tokens are burned at migration. |
| `locked_vesting` | all zero | No creator allocation. Creators buy like everyone else (in the batch). |
| `pool_creation_fee` | 0 | |
| `enable_first_swap_with_min_fee` | false | No creator first-swap fee exemption. |
| `migrated_pool_fee` / base-fee mode / market-cap params / `compounding_fee_bps` | zero/default as required by DBC for fixed migration fee options | Use the DBC validation rules (read `ConfigParameters::validate` in the DBC source) to pick the exact defaults. |
| `leftover_receiver`, `fee_claimer` | launch authority | |
| transfer hook program | `noised_hook` | |

**Creator-chosen (`LaunchParams`) and bounds:**

```rust
pub struct LaunchParams {
    pub token_decimal: u8,              // 6..=9
    pub sqrt_start_price: u128,         // validated by DBC
    pub curve: Vec<CurvePoint>,         // 1..=8 points {sqrt_price: u128, liquidity: u128}; validated by DBC
    pub migration_quote_threshold: u64, // MIN_THRESHOLD (1 SOL) ..= MAX_THRESHOLD (100_000 SOL), lamports
    pub base_fee: BaseFeeParams,        // {cliff_fee_numerator, first_factor, second_factor, third_factor, base_fee_mode}
                                        // mode 0 (linear) or 1 (exponential) scheduler only; the scheduler's
                                        // total duration (number_of_period * period_frequency) must be
                                        // <= open_delay_secs so the fair-open batch pays the floor base fee.
    pub dynamic_fee: Option<DynamicFeeParams>, // passed through; DBC validates
    pub migration_fee_option: u8,       // 0..=5 (fixed DAMM v2 fee tiers 0.25%..6%)
    pub open_delay_secs: u32,           // MIN_OPEN_DELAY (60) ..= MAX_OPEN_DELAY (1800): fair-open commit window
    pub guard_secs: u32,                // >= open_delay_secs + EXECUTION_GRACE_SECS (600), <= 72h
    pub max_wallet_bps: u16,            // 0 (off) or 10..=10_000, of the initial supply, during the guard
    pub max_buy_bps: u16,               // 0 (off) or 1..=10_000
    pub p2p_locked: bool,               // pause wallet-to-wallet transfers during the guard
    pub batch_wallet_cap: u64,          // max lamports one wallet (or one payout vault) may commit; 0 = no cap,
                                        // else >= resolved batch_total_cap / 200
    pub batch_total_cap: u64,           // max lamports the batch SWAPS: 0 (= threshold) or threshold/10 ..= threshold;
                                        // commits above it are accepted and refunded pro rata
    pub recipients: Vec<FeeRecipient>,  // 1..=4 {wallet: Pubkey, bps: u16}; bps sum == 10_000; no default keys;
                                        // no duplicates; not the launch's own PDAs
    pub pq_identity: [u8; 32],          // SHA256(0x21 || LMS public key) of the identity that must attest; not all zero
}
```

`pq_identity` is the last field, so `params_hash` (SHA-256 of the borsh-serialized `LaunchParams`) covers it. Field order is part of `params_hash` and never changes. Creator launch rules are not a `LaunchParams` field and `params_hash` does not cover them: the attestation binds them (§1.9).

Constants (`constants.rs`): `EXECUTION_GRACE_SECS = 600`, `MIN_OPEN_DELAY = 60`, `MAX_OPEN_DELAY = 1800`, `MAX_GUARD_SECS = 72*3600`, `MIN_THRESHOLD = 1_000_000_000`, `MAX_THRESHOLD = 100_000 * 1_000_000_000`, `MAX_PLATFORM_FEE_BPS = 5_000`, `MIN_BATCH_TOTAL_CAP_DIVISOR = 10`, `MIN_BATCH_WALLET_CAP_DIVISOR = 200`, `MAX_CURVE_POINTS = 8`, `MAX_RECIPIENTS = 4`, `MIN_MAX_WALLET_BPS = 10`, `MAX_TRADING_FEE_BPS = 1_000`, `MIN_LAUNCH_WINDOW_SECS = 60`, `MAX_LAUNCH_WINDOW_SECS = 3_600`, `MAX_LAUNCH_LEAD_SECS = 7 * 86_400`, `SAFETY_POLICY_VERSION = 3`. Metadata limits (UTF-8 bytes, not characters): `MAX_NAME_LEN = 32`, `MAX_SYMBOL_LEN = 10`, `MAX_URI_LEN = 200`, `MAX_DOC_LEN = 3_072`, `META_FLAG_INLINE_URI = 1`, `META_FLAG_EXTERNAL_URI = 2`, `INLINE_URI_PREFIX = "data:application/json;base64,"`, `EXTERNAL_URI_PREFIX = "https://"`, `MAX_INLINE_URI_LEN = 4_125`.

Why the windows and floors (audit round 2, findings C7, C8 and C0): every commit write-locks the same `Launch` account and batch escrow, and Agave lets one writable account use at most 24,000,000 CU per block. A party that fills that budget with high-priority transactions keeps every other commit out (or every `execute_batch` out) while it pays. A minute-long commit window and a ten-minute execution window make that exclusion cost linearly more (about 0.02 SOL for 60 s against commits that pay no priority fee, per the round-2 estimate); they do not remove it. The batch ends the shield itself, so the long execution window never delays trading (§1.4 `execute_batch`). The cap floors stop a creator from shrinking the fair open to dust while every other check passes (THREAT_MODEL §5.2).

Transaction size: `create_launch` carries the whole curve and the recipient list in one legacy transaction, which the creator's wallet signs. DBC accepts 16 points, but 16 took 1,315 bytes before PQ v2, over the 1,232-byte legacy limit, so the policy caps the curve at **8 points**. Measured in the launchpad's LiteSVM suite (PQ v2, 2026-10-09; `a3_the_largest_create_launch_fits_a_legacy_transaction`): a typical `create_launch` is 867 bytes and about 57k CU; the largest (8 points, 4 recipients, a dynamic fee, `pq_identity`, compute-unit limit and price) is 1,107 bytes, or 1,203 bytes when another account pays the fee. Every launch the program accepts fits a transaction every wallet can sign.

Hook timing written into the policy: `snipe_shield_secs = open_delay_secs + EXECUTION_GRACE_SECS`, `guard_secs` as given. The hook enforces its own hard bounds again (`MAX_SNIPE_SHIELD_SECS = 3600`, which covers the largest 1,800 + 600, `MAX_GUARD_SECS = 72h`, max-wallet >= supply/1000, max-buy >= supply/10_000). Once the batch has bought, `execute_batch` ends the shield early (`end_snipe_shield`: `snipe_shield_end_ts = min(snipe_shield_end_ts, now)`); the certificate keeps the scheduled end.

### 1.3 Launch timeline

```
create_launch     creator's wallet, legacy tx      Launch = Configured; params name pq_identity
publish_metadata  anyone (throwaway payer), v1     TokenMeta ["metadata", launch, H] written whole
publish_rules     anyone (throwaway payer), v1     optional: LaunchRules ["rules", launch, R] written whole
                  (the client waits for confirmation, finalized preferred, before attesting)
attest_launch     anyone relays, v1                LMS signature by pq_identity over message v3 (H, R or zero, window)
                                                   -> Attestation ["attestation", launch]; the leaf is burned
launch()          ANYONE, only inside the signed window [not_before, not_after]
T0                pool + mint from the TokenMeta record + hook meta list + hook policy (with the attested
                  rules record's bytes) + certificate (atomic)
|-- commit window: [T0, T0 + open_delay)        (60 s..30 min) anyone commits wSOL to the batch
|-- execution window: [T0 + open_delay, T0 + open_delay + 600 s)
|                       anyone calls execute_batch (as a top-level instruction): ONE DBC buy for the whole batch,
|                       only as the pool's first swap (else the batch expires and refunds in full);
|                       the same instruction then ends the snipe shield (end_snipe_shield)
|                       (until the batch, or T0 + open_delay + 600 s, the hook refuses every buy that is not that one)
|-- public trading from the batch's execution (or from T0 + open_delay + 600 s if no batch executed)
|-- guard rules (max-wallet, max-buy, p2p lock, creator rules) until T0 + guard_secs
|-- curve completes -> DBC revokes the hook (program + authority = None)
`-- anyone calls DBC migration_damm_v2 -> DAMM v2 pool, 100% LP permanently locked
```

Inside the attested window the first `launch` transaction to land wins: anyone can open the launch, and nobody can open it at another time. Keepers and the creator's own client should send `launch` at `not_before` (OPERATIONS.md, Keepers). If nobody opens the launch by `not_after`, that window is over for good: `launch` never runs outside it. The creator then signs a new window at its identity's next leaf; while the launch is still `Configured`, that attestation replaces the expired one, and the launch opens in the new window (audit round 3, F1). The metadata and rules records can be reused, or new ones published and signed. No commit, and so no user funds, exist before `launch`.

Nobody, including the creator, can buy before the batch executes. Until `snipe_shield_end_ts` (= `execute_end_ts` as scheduled, or the moment of the batch), the hook refuses every transfer out of the DBC base vault, whatever its amount and wherever it goes, unless the transaction's currently executing top-level instruction is the launchpad's `execute_batch`, which makes exactly one swap: the batch buy. The hook reads this from the instructions sysvar (HOOKS.md §2). The shield is checked before the zero-amount pass and before exemptions:
- a buy whose output rounds to zero is refused like any other. DBC 0.2.1 executes such a buy, and it still moves the price and the volatility accumulator; on a curve whose first segment holds dust liquidity, a 2-lamport buy before round 1b made the batch pay the floor fee plus the maximum dynamic fee (AUDIT.md, round 1b);
- a buy into an account owned by the batch-authority or launch-authority PDA is refused like any other;
- a transfer out of the base vault is never treated as a sell, even when its destination is the base vault itself.

Zero-amount transfers that are not buys still always pass, and sells always pass (invariant 3). As a fail-safe, `execute_batch` runs only as the pool's first swap (§1.4, invariant 13): if anything ever traded the pool before the batch, the batch can only expire and refund every commit in full.

Hook decision order (**must**; `noised_hook` `execute`, then `rules::built_ins` and `rules::creator_rules`; HOOKS.md §2 gives the reasons). A **buy** is a transfer whose source is the policy's base vault, whatever its destination; a **sell** is a transfer into the base vault from any other account. Steps 1 to 3 are decided from the policy's fixed head (`PolicyHead`, §1.9) before the rest of the policy is decoded (review 4, R3), so nothing in the rest of a policy can block a sell or anything after the guard.
1. No Noised policy for the mint: allow. A policy of another mint: refuse (`PolicyMismatch`).
2. Not a buy, and either a sell or a zero amount: allow, decided from account keys and the amount alone.
3. `now >= guard_end_ts`: allow.
4. A buy before `snipe_shield_end_ts` whose transaction's current top-level instruction is not the launchpad's `execute_batch`: refuse (`SnipeShield`), whatever the amount and whoever receives the tokens.
5. A zero amount (from here on only a buy can be zero): allow.
6. An exempt destination owner (the batch authority or the launch authority): allow.
7. The destination token account lacks Token-2022's `ImmutableOwner` extension: refuse (`MutableOwner`, 6010; audit round 3, F2).
8. An exempt source owner: allow.
9. Then the wallet-to-wallet lock (never for buys), max-buy and max-wallet.
10. The creator's launch rules, if the policy has any (§1.9): the first active rule that refuses wins (`RuleDenied`, 6011). Skipped when the transaction's current top-level instruction is the Noised vault or shield (holders' exits; review 4, R1). A transfer that reaches this step is a buy or a wallet-to-wallet transfer, non-zero, between non-exempt owners, into an account with `ImmutableOwner`, before the guard ends.

Step 7 exists because an owner change moves no tokens, so no hook runs: Token-2022 refuses `SetAuthority(AccountOwner)` only on an account with `ImmutableOwner`, and without the rule tokens bought into a hand-made account could change hands during a `p2p_locked` guard, and one owner could hold many accounts each at `max_wallet` (audit round 3, proof of concept against the mainnet binaries). The hook reads the extension from the destination account's own data, which Token-2022 already passes it (`has_immutable_owner`: the account-type byte at offset 165, then the extension entries as Token-2022 reads them; anything malformed reads as "no extension"), so the meta list and the policy layout are unchanged. Its position: after the zero-amount pass (nothing moves) and the exempt-destination pass (only the launchpad could sign an owner change for an account its PDA owns, and it never does; the batch buy reaches the batch escrow this way); before the exempt-source pass, so batch claims also land only in accounts with an immutable owner (they pay canonical Token-2022 ATAs); and before the lock and the caps, which reassignment would get around. No other account is excepted. Token-2022 ATAs always carry `ImmutableOwner`, so buys, claims and vault `release` payouts into ATAs, and wraps into the Shield escrow (an ATA), are unaffected; the DBC base vault has no `ImmutableOwner` but receives only sells, which step 2 allows; sells are never affected. What does need an immutable-owner destination during the guard: a buy, a vault `release_to`, a Shield unwrap or a wallet transfer into any other account, including a route that passes the tokens through another program's non-ATA account.

Everyone in the batch pays the same average price: tokens and unspent wSOL are shared pro rata by lamports committed. Splitting across wallets, committing early, or bundling a commit with the launch transaction therefore gives no advantage. When commits exceed `batch_total_cap`, the batch swaps at most the cap and every committer gets the same fraction of its commit refunded. `batch_wallet_cap` is per wallet and anyone can split across wallets, so it only limits naive concentration; its floor (`>= batch_total_cap / 200`) stops it from keeping the batch near-empty.

### 1.4 Instructions

#### `initialize_protocol(platform_fee_bps: u16)`
- Accounts: `authority` (signer, payer), `protocol` (init), `treasury`, `program` (this program), `program_data`, system program.
- The signer must be the program's upgrade authority (`program.programdata_address() == program_data` and `program_data.upgrade_authority_address == Some(authority)`, else `NotUpgradeAuthority`), so it cannot be front-run.
- `platform_fee_bps <= MAX_PLATFORM_FEE_BPS`, and `treasury` must be an existing Noised PQ vault of wSOL (`pq::is_vault_of`: owned by `noised_vault`, the `Vault` discriminator, version 4, mint = wSOL), else `InvalidProtocolParams`. The platform share then lands in ATA(treasury, wSOL), the vault's own token account, which only the vault's hash-based signature can move.
- Can run only once (PDA `init`). No update instruction exists.

#### `create_launch(launch_seed: [u8; 32], params: LaunchParams)`
- Accounts: `creator` (signer, payer), `protocol`, `launch` (init, `["launch", creator, launch_seed]`), `launch_authority`, `dbc_config` (PDA, created by DBC CPI), `quote_mint` (= wSOL, address-checked), `hook_program` (= noised_hook, address + executable), DBC program + event authority, system program.
- Validates `params` against §1.2 (`policy::validate`, including the batch-cap floors, `InvalidBatchCap`, and a non-zero `pq_identity`, `InvalidPqIdentity`) and snapshots `protocol.treasury` and `protocol.platform_fee_bps` into `Launch`.
- CPI `dynamic_bonding_curve::create_config_with_transfer_hook` with `config` = DBC-config PDA (signed with its seeds), `fee_claimer` = `leftover_receiver` = launch authority, `quote_mint` = wSOL, `transfer_hook_program` = noised_hook, `payer` = creator.
- Writes `Launch` (version 2) with `status = Configured`, `params_hash`, `pq_identity`; emits `LaunchCreated {launch, creator, dbc_config, params_hash, platform_fee_bps, pq_identity}`.
- The fee recipients cannot be checked on-chain for strandability without overflowing the legacy transaction; the SDK refuses vault-program accounts that are not wSOL vaults, token accounts, mints, programs and unopened off-curve addresses as recipients (`buildCreateLaunchTx`). A recipient that is a PQ vault of another mint would strand its whole share for good: `distribute` pays ATA(recipient, wSOL), which such a vault can never move (audit round 3, an info finding; its fix is F3). The identity attests the parameters, so this check belongs before signing; F3 adds it to the SDK's parameter validation (with a connection) and to the app's wizard, and the SDK warns about other program-owned recipients. A recipient may be the launch's autopilot treasury, `["treasury", launch]` under the autopilot program, derivable before the launch exists: `distribute` then pays its wSOL ATA, which the autopilot runs (§9); SDK 0.7 accepts it with a warning.

#### `publish_metadata(name: String, symbol: String, flags: u8, uri: String, doc: String)`
- Permissionless and idempotent; in practice sent by the client's throwaway fee payer in a v1 transaction. Accounts: `payer` (signer, writable; pays the rent), `launch` (`status == Configured`, else `WrongLaunchState`), `token_meta` (`init_if_needed` at `["metadata", launch, metadata_hash_v2(name, symbol, flags, uri, doc)]`), system program. Data: discriminator `b3bfa861c719e592`, then the borsh arguments.
- `metadata_hash_v2 = SHA256("PHX-META-V2" ‖ u32le(len name) ‖ name ‖ u32le(len symbol) ‖ symbol ‖ u8 flags ‖ u32le(len uri) ‖ uri ‖ u32le(len doc) ‖ doc)`: the tag, then the record's content in borsh order. Lengths are UTF-8 bytes.
- Rules (`policy::validate_metadata`), checked in this order:
  1. `flags` is 0 (no uri), `INLINE_URI` (1) or `EXTERNAL_URI` (2), else `InvalidMetadataFlags`;
  2. `doc` is at most 3,072 bytes (`MAX_DOC_LEN`) and `uri` at most 200 (`MAX_URI_LEN`, the DAS indexer's `varchar(200)`), else `MetadataTooLong`;
  3. name 1..=32 and symbol 1..=10 bytes, else `InvalidMetadata`;
  4. with `EXTERNAL_URI`, `uri` is 9..=200 bytes, starts with `https://` and every byte is printable ASCII (0x21..=0x7E), else `InvalidMetadata`; without it, `uri` is empty, else `InvalidMetadataFlags`;
  5. `INLINE_URI` needs a non-empty `doc`, else `InvalidMetadataFlags`.
- The program does not parse `doc` (UTF-8; the SDK writes compact JSON with a 32x32 PNG icon inline). The record is created whole, its space exactly its content; it is never modified or closed. The address is the content, so an existing record already holds these bytes: publishing it again writes nothing and emits nothing. An address pre-funded by anyone is taken over. Emits `MetadataPublished {launch, token_meta, metadata_hash, flags, uri_len: u16, doc_len: u16}` when it creates the record.
- `TokenMeta` layout (Anchor account, discriminator `sha256("account:TokenMeta")[..8]` = `8257ae23152c5c13`): `version: u8` (1) at 8, `bump: u8` at 9 (the canonical bump), `flags: u8` at 10, `launch: Pubkey` at 11, `metadata_hash: [u8; 32]` at 43, then `name`, `symbol`, `uri`, `doc` as borsh strings from 75. Size `91 + name + symbol + uri + doc` bytes (93 to 3,405). Rent at mainnet's 5,080 lamports per byte: `(128 + size) × 5,080`, about 0.0070 SOL for a typical 1,244-byte record with a 32x32 icon and 0.0179 SOL at the maximum.
- Measured (LiteSVM, 2026-10-09): a v1 transaction of 309 to 3,621 bytes and 12,668 to 23,799 CU; a 100,000-CU and 2 MiB loaded-data config is enough.

#### `publish_rules(bytes: Vec<u8>)`
- Optional (a launch without creator rules attests the zero hash). Permissionless and idempotent, like `publish_metadata`; in practice sent by the throwaway key that then sends `attest_launch`. Data: discriminator `d5f586baa8198fe9`, then `bytes` as borsh bytes. Accounts: `payer` (signer, writable; pays the rent), `launch` (`status == Configured`, else `WrongLaunchState`), `rules` (writable, `init_if_needed` at `["rules", launch, rules_hash(bytes)]`), system program.
- `rules_hash = SHA256("PHX-RULES-V1" ‖ bytes)`. The bytes must verify as PXR1 for the launch's `guard_secs` (`noised_rules::RuleSet::verify`, else `InvalidRules`, 6048; format in §1.9 and `crates/noised-rules/README.md`).
- The record is created whole, never modified or closed; an existing record already holds these bytes, so publishing again writes and emits nothing; an address pre-funded by anyone is taken over. Emits `RulesPublished {launch, rules, rules_hash, len: u16}` when it creates the record.
- `LaunchRules` layout (discriminator `ee49471de730278d`): `version: u8` (1) at 8, `bump` at 9 (canonical), `launch` 10..42, `rules_hash` 42..74, `u32` length at 74, the bytes from 78. Size `78 + len` (at most 1,306).
- Measured (review-4 build): a typical 286-byte set 11,117 CU, the largest 1,228-byte set about 16k to 17.6k CU; a v1 transaction of 580 to 1,522 bytes (SDK); a legacy transaction fits up to 898 bytes of rules.

#### `attest_launch(lms_pk: [u8; 56], lms_sig: Vec<u8>, metadata_hash: [u8; 32], rules_hash: [u8; 32], not_before: i64, not_after: i64)`
- Permissionless: the LMS signature authorizes, not the fee payer (a client sends it from a throwaway key in a v1 transaction, 3,005 bytes whatever the record, 3,037 with a rules account), and anyone may relay it. Data: discriminator `b518c935f67efd4f`, then the borsh arguments (`lms_sig` as borsh bytes, 2,508 long; `rules_hash` follows `metadata_hash`). 7 accounts: `payer` (signer, writable; pays the first identity and attestation rents), `launch` (any status), `identity` (`init_if_needed`, `["identity", SHA256(0x21 ‖ lms_pk)]`: the signing key's own; the IDL carries no PDA for it), `attestation` (`init_if_needed`, `["attestation", launch]`), `token_meta` (read-only, content unchecked; it must be `["metadata", launch, metadata_hash]` with the canonical bump, else `ConstraintSeeds` 2006 and nothing is written or burned; the IDL carries no PDA for it either), system program, then `rules` (optional, last; read-only, content unchecked): the `LaunchRules` address `["rules", launch, rules_hash]` the signed hash names, or the launchpad program id (Anchor's `None`) for the zero hash; any other account fails `ConstraintSeeds` and burns nothing.
- The signed message v3 (221 bytes): `"PHX-ATTEST-V3" ‖ program_id ‖ launch ‖ launch.params_hash ‖ launch.creator ‖ metadata_hash ‖ rules_hash ‖ i64le(not_before) ‖ i64le(not_after)`, with `rules_hash` all zero for no rules. (Message v2, 189 bytes, is retired; the first PQ v2 build's v1 was also 221 bytes but has another tag.) The signature is RFC 8554 `LMS_SHA256_M32_H10` over `LMOTS_SHA256_N32_W4` (2,508 bytes); the leaf `q` comes from the signature. An invalid signature fails `InvalidPqSignature`, a leaf already used `PqLeafUsed`.
- Leaves are one-time (two signatures from one leaf allow forgeries), so every valid signature at an unused leaf lands and burns its leaf, whatever it signed and whatever the launch's status. It **attests** only if all of these hold:
  1. *the attestation slot is free* (audit round 3, F1): `attestation.launch == Pubkey::default()` (never written) **or** `launch.status == Configured && now > attestation.not_after` (the stored window passed unused; `launch` runs only inside it, so it can never be used again);
  2. the signing identity is `launch.pq_identity`;
  3. the window is usable (`pq::launch_window_is_valid`): it lasts 60..=3,600 s, opens at most 7 days after landing, and leaves at least 60 s when the attestation lands, `max(now, not_before) + MIN_LAUNCH_WINDOW_SECS <= not_after` (F1; so a held or late landing never fixes a window with only seconds left);
  4. `token_meta` is the record the hash names: owned by the launchpad, the `TokenMeta` discriminator, version 1, that launch and hash, storing the canonical bump;
  5. when `rules_hash` is not zero, `rules` is the record it names (`pq::is_rules_record`): owned by the launchpad, the `LaunchRules` discriminator, version 1, that launch and hash, storing the canonical bump.

  It then writes every field of the `Attestation` (version, launch, identity, leaf, metadata hash, rules hash, message hash, slot, `not_before`, `not_after`, and the 2,508-byte signature, which always fills the account exactly; the bump is rewritten with the same value), so a replacement keeps nothing of the attestation it replaces, and emits `LaunchAttested {launch, pq_identity, leaf, metadata_hash, rules_hash, slot, not_before, not_after}`. Otherwise it emits `PqLeafConsumed {pq_identity, leaf, launch}` and leaves the attestation as it is (an empty one: version 0, `launch` default, bump set). An attestation whose window has not ended (before it, inside it, or in its last second) and any attestation of a `Live` launch are never replaced; the certificate keeps the attestation that opened the launch. `launch` needs `now <= not_after` and a replacement `now > not_after`, so within one slot both never apply.
- Why (audit round 3, R3-1): before F1 the attestation was written once, so a window that passed without a `launch` (keepers kept out by write-lock contention on the `Launch` account, congestion or an outage), or an attestation a relayer held back and landed at `now == not_after`, left the launch `Configured` forever: every later signature only burned a leaf. Now that is a retry: the creator signs a new window at its next leaf (SDK 0.4.0: `getLaunchFlowStatus` reports "Window passed: sign a new opening time", `replanLaunchWindow`). Contention can still delay a launch, window after window, at the attacker's cost (§8); it can no longer end it.
- **Readers.** `LaunchAttested` can be emitted more than once per launch. While the launch is `Configured`, the `Attestation` account (not an older event) is authoritative and may still be replaced, so clients, indexers and verifiers must not cache it; once `Live`, it never changes and its leaf and slot are the certificate's.
- **Older signatures can take a freed slot** (round 3 fix review, info). Once the stored window has passed unused, any valid signature by the identity attests if its own window still has a minute left, including one the creator sent earlier that never landed. Whoever holds such bytes (a dropped transaction kept by an RPC or relayer) can land it ahead of the creator's new signature and so choose, among windows and records the creator signed, one that opens up to 7 days later; the new signature then only burns its leaf until that window ends. Every outcome is one the identity signed; the cost is delay. Clients should land, or let burn, every signature they reveal. SDK 0.5 (unreleased) enforces it for the signatures in its leaf ledger: `buildAttestLaunchTx` throws `PendingAttestationError` instead of signing a new message for a launch while an earlier recorded signature of it has not landed and its window has not ended, and `landOrBurnPending` re-sends those signatures (never a new one), or with `burnOnly` only the ones that would just burn their leaf, which is safe while the current attestation holds the slot (§4.1). A signature that only another device's ledger holds is invisible to it until imported.
- Every account but the payer is fixed by the signed data (the launch by the message, the identity by the key, the attestation by the launch, each record by the launch and its hash, no rules account for the zero hash), and any other account fails the transaction. Whoever relays a signature can land it or delay it, but cannot change what it attests. Its levers are timing alone: a signature held back until less than 60 s of its window remain only burns its leaf, and the creator signs again; a held signature landed after a window passed is the previous point. On a `ConstraintSeeds` failure the client fixes the account and resends the same signature; it never re-signs for it.
- `Attestation` layout (version 3, 2,708 bytes): `version` at 8, `bump` at 9, `launch` 10..42, `pq_identity` 42..74, `leaf: u16` 74..76, `metadata_hash` 76..108, `rules_hash` 108..140, `message_hash` (SHA-256 of the message) 140..172, `slot` 172..180, `not_before` 180..188, `not_after` 188..196, signature length 196..200, signature 200..2,708.
- The client publishes the records (metadata, and rules if any) and waits for their confirmation (finalized preferred) before it sends the attestation. A signature that lands before its records exist only burns its leaf; the client signs again at its next leaf, and should stop after two or three burn-only landings for one launch and show why. After landing, a client checks the `Attestation` (version 3, `message_hash` = SHA-256 of its own message) rather than trusting the landing.
- Leaf choice (a client rule; the program never checks how a leaf was chosen). Up to SDK 0.8 (pinned by `lms.json`): `start = u16be(SHA256("phalanx/pq/identity/leaf" ‖ I ‖ message)[0..2]) mod 1024` with `I = lms_pk[8..24]`, then the first leaf, stepping +1 with wrap-around, that is clear in the on-chain bitmap and absent from the client's durable leaf records. Audit round 4 (ID-F1, AUDIT.md §16) showed that this let a dishonest RPC steer a second device onto the leaf of a signature it withheld, by reporting fake used bits. Since SDK 0.9.0: `start = u16be(HMAC-SHA256(probe key, "phalanx/pq/identity/leaf/v2" ‖ message)[0..2]) mod 1024`, where the probe key is derived from the identity's secret seed, so only the identity's holder can compute it; the leaf is the first from there, stepping +1 with wrap-around, that is absent from the ledger and the in-process guard, and on-chain bits never move it; the client refuses to sign if that leaf is set on-chain, or if the bitmap shows any used leaf its ledger has no record of (import the other device's records first). Either way the same message gives the same leaf and the same bytes, so a retry is idempotent. Before every signature that contains a time (a window here, `activate_at` or `not_after` in §1.4 `pq_authorize`), SDK 0.9.0 refuses when the cluster's clock and the device's differ by more than 120 s.
- Measured (2026-10-10, with F1): the integration vector transaction costs 99,113 CU (17 more than before F1) and loads 784,820 bytes; all 1,024 leaves of one identity take 79,778 to 114,482 CU (mean 95,445); the worst digest (990 chain steps) projects to about 170k CU; a replacement took 101,201 and 103,115 CU in two runs. With message v3 (review-4 build, 2026-10-11): 91k to 115k CU. Clients use a 300,000-CU and 2 MiB loaded-data config. The first attestation's payer spends rent(196) + rent(2,708) + one 5,000-lamport fee = 16,057,800 lamports at mainnet rent (2,676-byte v2: 15,895,240); a replacement costs only the fee (the account keeps its size and lamports).

#### `launch()`
- No arguments (data: discriminator `99f15de116454a3d`). Permissionless inside the attested window: `attestation.not_before <= now <= attestation.not_after`, else `NotLaunchWindow`. Signers: `payer` (anyone; pays the rent of the pool, mint, hook accounts and certificate) and `base_mint` (a fresh keypair).
- 23 accounts: `payer`, `launch`, `launch_authority`, `dbc_config`, `base_mint`, `pool`, `base_vault`, `quote_vault`, `quote_mint`, `dbc_pool_authority`, `hook_authority`, `extra_account_meta_list`, `policy`, `certificate`, `hook_program`, `dbc_program`, `dbc_event_authority`, `token_2022_program`, `token_program`, `system_program`, `attestation` (`["attestation", launch]` with its stored bump), `token_meta` (`["metadata", launch, attestation.metadata_hash]` with its stored bump; read-only), `rules` (optional, last; read-only): the `LaunchRules` record `["rules", launch, attestation.rules_hash]` with its stored bump, present exactly when the attestation's rules hash is not zero, else the program id (`AttestationMismatch` otherwise). A missing record fails `AccountNotInitialized` (3012), another record `ConstraintSeeds` (2006).
- Requires `attestation.launch == launch`, `attestation.pq_identity == launch.pq_identity`, a `TokenMeta` of version 1 with that launch and hash, and, with rules, `SHA256("PHX-RULES-V1" ‖ rules.bytes) == attestation.rules_hash` (review 4, R6a: re-hashed here, so the binding does not rest on `publish_rules` being the only writer of a record) (`AttestationMismatch`). An empty, burn-only attestation is refused.
- The mint's name and symbol are the record's. Its uri is derived from the record: `""` without a uri mode; `"data:application/json;base64," + base64(doc)` with `INLINE_URI` (RFC 4648, standard alphabet, padded; at most 4,125 bytes); the record's `uri` with `EXTERNAL_URI`.
- CPI `initialize_virtual_pool_with_token2022_transfer_hook` (creator = launch authority PDA signer, payer = the payer), with `InitializePoolParameters { name, symbol, uri }`.
- CPI `noised_hook::initialize_extra_account_meta_list` (payer = the payer). The list holds exactly two entries: the policy PDA `["policy", mint]` and the instructions sysvar.
- CPI `noised_hook::create_policy` signed by the hook-authority PDA with: `pool`, `snipe_shield_secs`, `guard_secs`, `max_wallet = initial_supply * max_wallet_bps / 10_000`, `max_buy = initial_supply * max_buy_bps / 10_000`, `p2p_locked`, `exempt = [batch_authority, launch_authority, default, default]`, `rules` = the rules record's bytes (empty without rules; the hook verifies them again, `InvalidRules`). `initial_supply` = the mint supply right after pool init. The written policy is re-read and must hold exactly these values, `written.rules == rules` included (`UnsafePolicy`).
- **Post-condition checks (must)** on the freshly created mint: owner = Token-2022; mint authority None; freeze authority None; extension set is exactly {MetadataPointer, TokenMetadata, TransferHook}; TransferHook program = noised_hook; MetadataPointer authority None and address = mint; TokenMetadata update authority None; the TokenMetadata holds exactly the record's name and symbol and the derived uri, and no additional metadata (compared in place with the bytes sent to DBC; `MetadataMismatch`, unreachable with an honest DBC). Abort if any fails.
- Writes `Certificate` (§1.5, naming the record and the attested `rules_hash`; `checks` = 4,095, plus `RULES_BOUND` when the re-read policy holds non-empty rules); sets `launch.base_mint`, `launch.pool`, `launch.launch_ts`, `commit_end_ts = launch_ts + open_delay`, `execute_end_ts = commit_end_ts + EXECUTION_GRACE_SECS`, `guard_end_ts`, `status = Live`. Emits `Launched {launch, base_mint, pool, certificate, initial_supply, launch_ts, commit_end_ts, execute_end_ts, guard_end_ts, pq_identity, pq_leaf}`.
- Size (review-4 build, SDK encoding): a legacy transaction with both compute-budget instructions of 988 bytes without rules and 1,020 with a rules account, whatever the records, so keepers and wallets need no metadata or rules bytes. Compute on that build: about 143k to 210k CU, about 150k to 158k with the largest rule set. Compute model (measured 2026-10-09, before rules): CU is a base per record plus 1,500 CU per extra bump-search step of the mint's PDAs: base 142,672 with no uri, 188,107 at the largest inline uri, 143,996 with a 200-byte external uri; measured launches took 148,672 to 221,107 CU, and a launch exceeds 258,607 CU with probability under 10^-6 (47 extra bump-search steps at the largest inline uri). Clients use a 300,000-CU limit. The mint is `386 + name + symbol + uri` bytes (at most 4,553). `[launch, commit]` as a v1 bundle with a priority fee was 1,149 bytes (2026-10-09, before the rules account).

#### `commit(amount: u64)`
- Accounts: `owner` (signer, payer), `launch` (`status == Live`, `has_one = batch_authority`), `commit` (`init_if_needed`, `["commit", launch, owner]`), `batch_authority`, `quote_mint` (= wSOL), `owner_quote_account` (wSOL token account of `owner`), `batch_quote_escrow` (ATA of the batch authority, `init_if_needed`), token program, ATA program, system program, then `token_owner`, `refund_owner` and the optional `payout_vault`.
- Window: `launch_ts <= now < commit_end_ts`, else `NotCommitWindow`. `amount > 0` (`ZeroAmount`). Not after the batch executed (`AlreadyExecuted`; audit round 4, L-i2: defense in depth, unreachable today because the batch executes only after the commit window).
- **Payout owners.** The first commit fixes `token_owner` (who receives the tokens) and `refund_owner` (who receives the unspent or refunded wSOL). Each must be the committer itself or a Noised PQ vault of that mint: the base mint for `token_owner`, wSOL for `refund_owner` (`pq::is_payout_owner`), else `InvalidPayoutOwner`. Later commits must pass the same two accounts (`InvalidPayoutOwner`). Nobody, the committer included, can redirect a share afterwards.
- **Caps.** The committer's total must stay `<= batch_wallet_cap` when the cap is non-zero (`CapExceeded`). When `token_owner` is a vault, `payout_vault` must be the `["payout-vault", launch, token_owner]` account (`init_if_needed`); it sums every commit naming that vault and is held to the same cap (`CapExceeded`), because claims are exempt from the hook and shares paid to one account would otherwise add up past `max_wallet` and around `p2p_locked`. When `token_owner` is the committer, `payout_vault` must be omitted (the program ID in its place, Anchor's `None`); any other combination fails `InvalidPayoutOwner`. The total cap never refuses a commit (§1.3).
- **A vault's quota is shared (audit round 3, documented residual).** Anyone may name any PQ vault of the base mint as the token owner of its own commit, so a third party can use up a vault's quota before the vault's holder commits, and the holder's commit naming that vault then fails `CapExceeded`. The tokens the third party's commit buys are paid to that vault (a donation); its unspent wSOL is refunded to the `refund_owner` the third party fixed at its own first commit (itself, or a wSOL PQ vault it names), because `token_owner` and `refund_owner` are fixed separately and `claim` pays each leg only to its own owner. So the cost is the part of its commit the batch spent. A client avoids it by opening a fresh vault and committing to it in the same transaction (SDK 0.3's `preparePqVault` returns the `open_vault` transaction on its own, so a client has to put both instructions in one transaction itself), so no ordinary user sees the vault's address first; a leader that reorders transactions could still copy the public key, open the vault first and fill its quota, and the victim then retries with a new key. Binding the record to its first committer, or keying the cap on (launch, committer, vault), was rejected: the first lets a front-runner lock a vault out for one lamport, the second brings back the aggregation that `PayoutVault` exists to stop.
- Transfers `amount` wSOL (`transfer_checked`, SPL Token) from the owner's wSOL account to the batch wSOL escrow. Updates `Commit` (owner, amount, claimed, token_owner, refund_owner) and `launch.total_committed`; emits `Committed {launch, owner, amount, owner_total, total_committed, token_owner, refund_owner}`.
- Measured (PQ v2): a commit naming two vaults is 718 bytes and 47.2k CU.
- The PQ v2 draft's owner-signed `claim_to` was replaced by these payout owners (spec review amendment A7): a permissionless `claim` could pre-empt it into the committer's Ed25519 wallet, it kept an Ed25519 power to redirect funds until claim time, and arbitrary destinations of hook-exempt claims bypassed `p2p_locked` and `max_wallet`.

#### `execute_batch()`
- Permissionless, once. Window: `commit_end_ts <= now < execute_end_ts` (600 s), else `NotExecuteWindow`. Requires `total_committed > 0` (`EmptyBatch`) and not executed (`AlreadyExecuted`).
- **The batch must be the pool's first swap (must):** requires the DBC pool's `has_swap == 0` (`checks::dbc_pool_has_swapped`; DBC sets the flag in every swap, including one that buys nothing), else `PoolAlreadyTraded`. The hook refuses every other buy until the shield ends and nobody holds tokens to sell before the batch, so this fails only if something got past the shield. The batch then can never execute: it expires and `claim` refunds every commit in full. It never buys at a price or a volatility fee that an earlier swap moved.
- Must be sent as a **top-level instruction**: the hook recognizes the batch buy only when the current top-level instruction is `execute_batch` (HOOKS.md §2), so a CPI'd `execute_batch` fails with `SnipeShield`. Other instructions may share the transaction.
- `amount_in = min(total_committed, batch_total_cap)`. CPI `swap2_with_transfer_hook` with `payer` = batch authority (signed), input = batch wSOL escrow, output = batch base escrow (ATA of batch authority for the base mint, Token-2022, `init_if_needed`), `swap_mode` = PartialFill (1), `amount_0 = amount_in`, `amount_1 = 0` (minimum out; safe because no one else can buy before the batch), referral none. Remaining accounts: exactly the 4 hook accounts `[extra_account_meta_list, policy, instructions sysvar, noised_hook program]`, passed with `TransferHookAccountsInfo { slices: [TransferHookBase(4)] }`.
- Records `quote_spent` and `tokens_out` from balance differences of the two escrows; requires `quote_spent <= amount_in` (`PayoutExceedsEscrow`) and `tokens_out > 0` (`EmptySwap`). Sets `executed = true`. If the curve completed during this swap that is fine (DBC revokes the hook in the same swap).
- **Then it ends the snipe shield:** CPI `noised_hook::end_snipe_shield`, signed by the hook-authority PDA, with this launch's policy (`["policy", base_mint]` under the hook, writable) and the instructions sysvar. The hook sets `snipe_shield_end_ts = min(snipe_shield_end_ts, now)`, refuses unless the top-level instruction is the launchpad's `execute_batch` (`NotInBatch`), never moves the end later, and does not read the mint (so it works after DBC revoked the hook). Extra accounts: `hook_authority`, `policy`, `hook_program`. Emits `BatchExecuted` (launchpad) and `SnipeShieldEnded {mint, pool, scheduled_end_ts, snipe_shield_end_ts}` (hook).
- If nobody executes in the window, or `execute_batch` cannot run (`PoolAlreadyTraded`, `EmptySwap`), the batch is **expired** at `execute_end_ts`: every commit is refunded in full by `claim`.
- Measured (PQ v2): 946 bytes, 114k-174k CU, of which the `end_snipe_shield` CPI is 13.65k CU.

#### `claim(owner: Pubkey)`
- Permissionless (anyone may crank a claim; funds go only to the commit's payout owners). Once per `Commit` (`AlreadyClaimed`). Accounts: `payer` (anyone; pays for missing ATAs), `launch`, `commit` (`["commit", launch, owner]`), `token_owner` (= `commit.token_owner`), `refund_owner` (= `commit.refund_owner`), `batch_authority`, `base_mint`, `quote_mint`, `batch_quote_escrow`, `batch_base_escrow` (needed when tokens are paid), `refund_owner_ata`, `token_owner_ata` (each needed when that leg pays), token program, Token-2022, ATA program, system program; then the 4 hook accounts while the hook is active.
- If executed: `tokens = floor(tokens_out * commit / total_committed)` base tokens to the canonical base-token ATA of `token_owner` (Token-2022) and `refund = floor((total_committed - quote_spent) * commit / total_committed)` wSOL to the canonical wSOL ATA of `refund_owner`. An oversubscribed batch is therefore filled pro rata: the refund includes each commit's share of `total_committed - batch_total_cap`.
- The two ATAs are validated by `AtaCreator::ensure`: canonical ATA address of that owner for that mint and token program, created if missing (payer = caller), owned by the token program and holding that mint. The token-account **owner field is not compared**, as in `distribute`: only that owner can reassign its own ATA (a vault never does), so nobody else can block a claim. The consequence: a claim pays a reassigned canonical wSOL ATA wherever its owner field now points, so a committer that is its own refund owner and reassigned that ATA, or was tricked into reassigning it, gets its refund there (SECURITY_GUARANTEES.md L3). A base-token ATA is a Token-2022 ATA, whose owner cannot be reassigned.
- Transfers out of the batch escrow are exempt from the hook's rules (source owner = batch authority); while the hook is active the base transfer still needs the 4 hook accounts as remaining accounts.
- If expired (now >= execute_end_ts and not executed): full `commit` wSOL refund to the refund owner. Before either: `BatchPending`.
- Running totals `tokens_claimed <= tokens_out` and `quote_refunded <= refundable` bound every claim (`PayoutExceedsEscrow`). Rounding dust stays in the escrows forever (bounded by the number of participants, in base units).
- Emits `BatchClaimed {launch, owner, tokens, refund, expired, token_owner, refund_owner}`. Measured (PQ v2): a third-party claim into two vaults is 973 bytes and 80.5k CU.

#### `claim_fees()` and `claim_surplus()`
- Permissionless. `claim_fees`: CPI DBC `claim_trading_fee2` (fee_claimer = launch authority signer, `max_amount_a = 0`, `max_amount_b = u64::MAX`) into the launch authority's wSOL ATA. Before migration only (DBC reverts otherwise; the error propagates).
- `claim_surplus`: after the curve completes, CPI `partner_withdraw_surplus` and `creator_withdraw_surplus` (both roles are the launch authority) into its wSOL ATA. Emits `FeesClaimed {launch, quote_amount, surplus}`.

#### `claim_lp_fees()`
- Permissionless, after migration. CPI DAMM v2 `claim_position_fee` for one position whose NFT account is owned by the launch authority and whose `pool`'s mints are (base_mint, wSOL) (`checks::verify_lp_position`, `InvalidPosition`). Receives base tokens into the launch authority's base ATA and wSOL into its wSOL ATA. Clients send one position per transaction (the SDK's `buildClaimLpFeesTx` returns one transaction per position with fees). Emits `LpFeesClaimed`.

#### `distribute()`
- Permissionless. Splits the launch authority's whole wSOL balance: `platform = floor(balance * platform_fee_bps / 10_000)` to the treasury's canonical wSOL ATA (the treasury is a wSOL PQ vault, so this is the vault's token account), then the rest to `recipients` by bps (floor; the last recipient gets the remainder of the creator share so nothing stays behind except when a transfer would be zero). Remaining accounts are `[wallet_i, wsol_ata_i]` per stored recipient; each ATA is checked and created if missing by `AtaCreator::ensure` (canonical address, owner field not compared; payer = caller). A recipient may be a wSOL PQ vault: its canonical wSOL ATA is the vault's token account.
- Burns the launch authority's whole base-token balance (`burn_checked`, signed by the launch authority) — LP fees paid in the launched token are destroyed, never sold. Emits `Distributed`.

#### `pq_authorize(lms_pk: [u8; 56], lms_sig: Vec<u8>, kind: u8, payload_hash: [u8; 32])`
- Permissionless: the LMS signature authorizes, not the fee payer (a throwaway key in a v1 transaction of about 2,924 bytes). Data: discriminator `89bf3ed6b9288ff7`, then the borsh arguments. Accounts: `payer` (signer, writable), `launch` (any status), `identity` (writable, `init_if_needed`, `["identity", SHA256(0x21 ‖ lms_pk)]`: the **same** account and leaf bitmap as `attest_launch`), `authorization` (writable, `init_if_needed`, `["auth", launch, [kind], payload_hash]`), system program.
- The signed message (140 bytes): `"PHX-AUTH-V1" ‖ program_id ‖ launch ‖ launch.creator ‖ u8 kind ‖ payload_hash`. An invalid signature fails `InvalidPqSignature`, a used leaf `PqLeafUsed`; every valid signature at an unused leaf lands and burns its leaf.
- It **writes** the `Authorization` only if (a) nothing was written at that address yet, (b) the signer is `launch.pq_identity`, and (c) `kind` is 1, 2 or 3 (`AUTHORIZATION_KINDS`; review 4, Ra); otherwise it is burn-only and emits `PqLeafConsumed`. When it writes, it emits `Authorized {launch, authorization, pq_identity, kind, payload_hash, leaf, unix_time, slot}`. Every account but the payer is fixed by the signed data, so a relayer can land or withhold a signature but not change what it does.
- The launchpad gives `payload_hash` no meaning; the autopilot reads the account by layout and checks what it stands for. Kinds: 1 `GRAPH_ACTIVATE`, 2 `GRAPH_FREEZE`, 3 `OPERATOR_REVOKE`; payloads (integers little-endian; `autopilot` is `["autopilot", launch]` under the autopilot program):
  - activate: `SHA256("PHX-GRAPH-ACT-V1" ‖ autopilot ‖ graph_hash ‖ i64 activate_at)`;
  - freeze: `SHA256("PHX-GRAPH-FRZ-V2" ‖ autopilot ‖ graph_hash ‖ i64 not_after)` (version 2 since review 4, R5);
  - revoke: `SHA256("PHX-OP-REVOKE-V3" ‖ autopilot ‖ graph_hash of the active graph ‖ operator ‖ active_auth)`, where `active_auth` is the `Autopilot` account's record of the authorization that activated the running graph (version 3 since audit round 4, A4-3: a revocation names the activation it revokes, §9.3).
- `Authorization` layout (discriminator `0344a8f3c3a8204b`, version 1, 2,669 bytes): `version` at 8, `bump` at 9, `launch` 10..42, `pq_identity` 42..74, `kind` at 74, `payload_hash` 75..107, `message_hash` (SHA-256 of the 140-byte message) 107..139, `unix_time` 139..147 and `slot` 147..155 (both from `Clock`, never from arguments), `leaf: u16` 155..157, signature length 157..161 (2,508), signature 161..2,669. The full signature is stored so anyone can re-verify it from account state. Never modified or closed.
- Measured (review-4 build): 93k to 107k CU.

### 1.5 Certificate (`["certificate", base_mint]`)

Written once in `launch`, never modified, never closed:

```
version: u8,                    // 4
bump: u8,
launch: Pubkey, base_mint: Pubkey, pool: Pubkey, dbc_config: Pubkey,
creator: Pubkey,                // informational only: has no powers
launched_at: i64,
token_decimal: u8, initial_supply: u64,
checks: u32,                    // bitflags at offset 187..191; the twelve below are all set on every
                                // certificate (ALL = 4095); test (checks & 4095) == 4095:
                                //  1 mint authority None, 2 freeze authority None,
                                //  4 metadata update authority None, 8 metadata pointer authority None,
                                //  16 extension set exact, 32 hook = noised_hook,
                                //  64 LP 100% permanently locked by config, 128 no migration fee,
                                //  256 no creator allocation, 512 quote = wSOL,
                                //  1024 PQ_ATTESTED (the launch's identity signed it, verified on-chain),
                                //  2048 METADATA_ONCHAIN (the mint's TokenMetadata, re-read after the
                                //       pool CPI, is exactly the attested record's name, symbol and uri)
                                // plus, informational and never required:
                                //  4096 RULES_BOUND (the launch has creator rules, and the hook policy,
                                //       re-read after it was written, holds exactly the attested
                                //       rules record's bytes)
migration_quote_threshold: u64, migration_fee_option: u8,
snipe_shield_end_ts: i64,       // the SCHEDULED end (= execute_end_ts); the live policy's end moves to
                                // the moment of the batch when it executes earlier
execute_end_ts: i64, guard_end_ts: i64,
max_wallet: u64, max_buy: u64, p2p_locked: bool,
platform_fee_bps: u16, recipients: [FeeRecipient; 4] (unused = default, bps 0),
params_hash: [u8; 32],          // sha256 of the borsh-serialized LaunchParams
policy_version: u16,            // = SAFETY_POLICY_VERSION (3), at offset 411..413
pq_identity: [u8; 32],          // the identity that attested the launch (413..445)
pq_leaf: u16,                   // its leaf that signed (445..447)
attested_slot: u64,             // slot of the attestation (447..455; ["attestation", launch] holds the signature)
metadata: Pubkey,               // the TokenMeta record the identity attested (455..487)
rules_hash: [u8; 32],           // the creator rules the identity attested, which the hook policy
                                // holds (487..519); all zero for none
```

Size 519 bytes with the discriminator (version 3 was the same without `rules_hash`, 487 bytes). `checks` is 4,095 without rules and 8,191 with them, so every client and verifier **must** test `(checks & 4095) == 4095`, never `checks == 4095`; `RULES_BOUND` means "rules are bound", not "protected" (§1.9, §8). `SAFETY_POLICY_VERSION` stays 3. Flag 128 ("no migration fee") means the config sets no creator or partner migration fee; Meteora's fixed 0.2% protocol migration fee still applies (§1.2). A verifier resolves the token's metadata through `metadata` (or, before launch, `Attestation.metadata_hash`), recomputes `metadata_hash_v2` from the record, resolves the rules through `rules_hash` (`["rules", launch, rules_hash]`, re-hashed, compared with the hook policy's bytes) and re-verifies the LMS signature over the 221-byte message v3.

The batch caps are not in the certificate; they are fixed fields of the `Launch` account (`launch`) and committed to by `params_hash`. The SDK must **not** trust the certificate alone: `verifyLaunch` re-checks the live mint, DBC config, hook policy and (after migration) the DAMM v2 position lock state.

### 1.8 CPI depth budget

Max instruction stack depth on mainnet is 5. `launchpad -> DBC swap -> Token-2022 -> hook` = 4. `launchpad -> DBC init -> Token-2022/System` = 3. `launchpad -> hook end_snipe_shield` = 2, `launchpad -> hook create_policy` = 2. `publish_rules` / `pq_authorize` -> System = 2. Autopilot: `run` (buyback) `-> DAMM v2 swap -> SPL Token / Token-2022` = 3 (the launch's hook is revoked by then, so no hook level); burns and distributions `autopilot -> Token-2022 / SPL Token` = 2; `init_autopilot` / `bind_mint` `-> ATA program -> token program / System` = 3. Never add a layer in between. A CPI'd `execute_batch` cannot succeed (§1.4), so the spare fifth level is never used on the batch path.

### 1.9 Creator launch rules and the hook policy

A creator may add launch rules to the Launch Guard: extra refusals while the guard is on (at most 72 h), in the PXR1 format of `crates/noised-rules`, whose README is normative for the bytes, the condition VM and the verification order. They are optional (no rules = the zero rules hash) and are not part of `LaunchParams`. The chain binds them end to end: `publish_rules` verifies them for the launch's `guard_secs` and writes the `LaunchRules` record at the address their hash names; the attestation signs that hash (message v3); `launch` re-hashes the record (R6a) and passes its bytes to `noised_hook::create_policy`, which verifies them again for the policy's guard (`RuleSet::verify`, else `InvalidRules` 6012) and stores them; `launch` re-reads the policy (`written.rules == rules`); the certificate records `rules_hash` and sets `RULES_BOUND` (§1.5). Stored rules that do not parse refuse (fail closed, code 255 `CODE_MALFORMED`); `create_policy` never stores such bytes.

**PXR1 (version 1)**, little-endian, exact length, at most 1,228 bytes: `"PXR1" | version u8 = 1 | n_keys u8 (0..=8) | n_rules u8 (1..=16) | n_nodes u8 (0..=64) | reserved [0; 4] | keys [32] × n_keys | rules (20 bytes each) | condition nodes (10 bytes each)`. A rule is `from_rel u32 | to_rel u32 | cond_off u8 | cond_len u8 | effect u8 | code u8 | arg u64`, active while `from_rel <= now_rel < to_rel`, with `now_rel = now - launch_ts` (seconds since the launch opened) and `to_rel <= min(guard_secs, 259,200)`. Effects: 1 `DENY` (refuse if the condition holds), 2 `CAP_AMOUNT` (if it holds, refuse `amount > cap`), 3 `CAP_DEST_BALANCE` (if it holds, refuse a destination balance after the transfer `> cap`); a cap is an amount or, with bit 63 set, `bps` (1..=10,000) of the **current** supply. `code` (0..=63) is shown to users. Condition leaves: `IS_BUY`, `IS_P2P`, `TIME_LT`, `TIME_GE`, `AMOUNT_GT`, `DEST_BAL_AFTER_GT`, `DEST_OWNER_IN` and `SRC_OWNER_IN` (a mask over the key list), `TOP_PROGRAM_IN` (the program of the transaction's current top-level instruction, a key mask), `TX_IX_COUNT_GT` (number of top-level instructions); combined in postfix with `AND`, `OR`, `NOT`, `TRUE`, `FALSE` on a boolean stack of at most 16 values, verified once when written. `rules_hash = SHA256("PHX-RULES-V1" ‖ bytes)`.

**What rules can do:** only refuse more transfers before `guard_end_ts`: time-windowed denials, per-transfer caps, destination-balance caps, allow or deny by wallet key lists (up to 8 keys), by top-level program, by instruction count (anti-bot). **What they cannot do:** allow anything a built-in refuses; touch a sell, a batch claim, the batch buy, a zero-amount transfer, a transfer into or out of an exempt launchpad account, or anything at or after `guard_end_ts`; change after the identity signed them; outlast the guard; apply to holders' exits.

**Evaluation** (step 10 of §1.3; `execute` → `rules::built_ins` → `rules::creator_rules`). The rules run last, after every built-in allow (sell, guard end, the batch, zero amount, exempt destination, exempt source = claims) and every built-in denial (`SnipeShield`, `MutableOwner`, `TransfersLocked`, `MaxBuy`, `MaxWallet`, each keeping its own error). What reaches them is a buy or a wallet-to-wallet transfer (`is_p2p = !is_buy`), non-zero, between non-exempt owners, into an account with `ImmutableOwner`, before the guard ends. The first active rule that refuses wins: `RuleDenied` (6011), logged as `Launch rule {i} (code {c}) refused the transfer`.

**Holders' exits (review 4, R1).** When the program of the transaction's current top-level instruction is the Noised PQ vault (`7BQ6…`) or the Noised shield (`BUMH…`), the creator-rules step is skipped: vault `release` / `release_to` payouts and shield wraps and unwraps are decided by the built-ins alone, which still apply exactly as before (p2p lock, max-buy, max-wallet, `MutableOwner`). A call into the vault or shield made by another program through CPI has that program as the top-level instruction, so the rules still apply to it. Residual (disclosed): routing through the vault or shield bypasses anti-bot **rules**, never built-ins: a wrap (rules skipped), a move of zTokens (no hook on the zToken mint) and an unwrap (rules skipped) get past a rule that restricts wallet-to-wallet transfers or owner lists; with the built-in p2p lock on, the wrap and the unwrap are still refused.

**Lazy reads (review 4, Rb).** The mint's supply and the instructions sysvar (top-level program, instruction count) are read only for a transfer that reaches the rules step under a policy that has rules; sells, claims, the batch, exempt owners and rule-less policies never pay for them.

**Policy head (review 4, R3).** `execute` first reads the policy's fixed head (`state::PolicyHead`): the `HookPolicy` discriminator and a length of at least 130 bytes, `mint` at 10..42, `base_vault` at 74..106, `guard_end_ts` at 122..130. From these alone it decides steps 1 to 3 of §1.3 (no policy: allow; another mint: `PolicyMismatch`; a sell or a non-buy zero amount: allow; `now >= guard_end_ts`: allow), and only then decodes the whole policy. So no layout or version problem in the rest of a policy can block a sell or anything after the guard; a version-1-sized policy reads the same head (tested).

Rules are disclosed, not judged: a rule set may restrict buys to listed wallets for the whole guard, vacuous rules still set `RULES_BOUND`, and rules see token transfers only (§8).

## 2. Shield program (`noised_shield`) — opt-in confidential balances

Confidential Balances (Token-2022 `ConfidentialTransferMint`, ZK ElGamal proof program re-enabled on mainnet 2026-06-04) hide **balances and transfer amounts**, not who transacts with whom. AMMs cannot trade confidential mints, so privacy is a separate, reversible layer next to the tradeable token.

### PDAs
| PDA | Seeds |
|---|---|
| `Shield` | `["shield", base_mint]` |
| Shield authority | `["shield-authority", base_mint]` (mint authority of the zToken; owner of the escrow) |
| zToken mint | `["z-mint", base_mint]` (Token-2022) |
| escrow | ATA(shield authority, base_mint, base token program) |

### zToken mint
Token-2022 with: `ConfidentialTransferMint { authority: None, auto_approve_new_accounts: true, auditor_elgamal_pubkey: None }`, `MetadataPointer` (authority None, address = itself), `TokenMetadata` (name `"Shielded " + name` truncated to 32, symbol `"z" + symbol` truncated to 10, uri copied when the base mint has Token-2022 metadata with a uri of at most 200 bytes and no invisible character, else empty (a launch's inline `data:` uri over 200 bytes, any document over 126 bytes, gives an empty uri); update authority None after init), decimals = base decimals, freeze authority None, mint authority = shield authority PDA (only ever used by `wrap`).

### Instructions
- `create_shield()` — permissionless, once per base mint. Base mint must be SPL Token or Token-2022 whose extensions are within {MetadataPointer, TokenMetadata, TransferHook, TransferFeeConfig}; reject PermanentDelegate, Pausable, DefaultAccountState, NonTransferable, ConfidentialTransferMint, ConfidentialMintBurn, MintCloseAuthority, ScaledUiAmount, InterestBearingConfig, PermissionedBurn, and any unknown extension.
- `wrap(amount)` — user transfers base tokens to the escrow (`transfer_checked`, forward transfer-hook extras from remaining accounts). Mint zTokens equal to the escrow balance **difference** (handles transfer fees).
- `unwrap(amount)` — `burn_checked` zTokens from the user (user signs), then transfer `amount` base tokens from the escrow to the user.
- Invariant after every instruction: `escrow balance >= zToken supply` (`token.rs::require_backed`; abort otherwise). Strict equality cannot be enforced: anyone can donate to the escrow or burn zTokens directly, and equality would let a 1-unit donation brick every wrap and unwrap.
- Confidential operations (configure account, deposit, apply pending, confidential transfer, withdraw) are plain Token-2022 instructions done client-side.
- On a Noised launch mint during its guard, a `wrap` or `unwrap` sent as the shield's own top-level instruction is decided by the Launch Guard's built-in checks alone: creator launch rules never apply to it (§1.9, review 4, R1).

Honest caveats to document: hides amounts not parties; ElGamal/Ristretto is not post-quantum (recorded history could be decrypted later); the Token-2022 program is upgradeable by a Squads multisig and the ZK program has a feature-gate kill switch that was used in 2025.

## 3. PQ vault (`noised_vault`) — contract for clients

> **Change note — v4 (audit round 1):** in-place key rotation with a sequence number; withdraw only reserves a payout; delivery by release/release_to; sweep and successor vaults removed.
>
> Why: v3 paid the recipient inside `withdraw`, so the recipient could make a revealed signature fail forever (reassigning its SPL Token ATA's owner, requiring memos or refusing non-confidential credits on a Token-2022 account). The key holder was then left with abandoning the vault or reusing a one-time key. v3 also could not execute a withdrawal to the vault or its successor PDA (duplicate mutable accounts), paid a fixed transfer fee on the whole remainder at every withdrawal, and accepted mints with a close authority, which can be closed at zero supply and re-created with a permanent delegate.

> **Change note — key-index binding (audit round 2, finding C5; part of PQ v2):** `InvalidNextKey` compared the next key only with the current one, so a message could reinstall a spent key, whose next signature would be its second. Each key is now bound to its position by its LM-OTS index: `open_vault` takes only `q = 0`, and `withdraw` only a next key with `q = sequence + 1` (`InvalidKeyIndex`, 6006). `q` is hashed into every LM-OTS hash and into the key id, so no key can ever control a vault twice. Layouts and the message are unchanged; a vault supports up to `u32::MAX` withdrawals.

### 3.1 Scheme and keys

- Scheme: LM-OTS (RFC 8554 section 4), `LMOTS_SHA256_N32_W4` (typecode 3, n = 32, w = 4, p = 67). Rust: `crates/noised-lmots`; TypeScript: `sdk/src/pq/lmots.ts`; both reproduce RFC 8554 Appendix F Test Case 2 byte for byte. One key signs one message, ever. About 128-bit security against a quantum attacker, which gets only Grover's square-root speed-up against SHA-256 (POST_QUANTUM.md §4).
- Public key `VaultKey { id: [u8;16] (LM-OTS I), q: u32, k: [u8;32] (K) }`; `key_id = SHA256(0x20 || u32be(3) || I || u32be(q) || K)`.

### 3.2 Accounts

| Account | Address | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| `Vault` | PDA `["vault", mint, genesis_key_id]` | `version: u8` (= 4), `bump: u8`, `mint: Pubkey`, `genesis_key_id: [u8;32]` (key id of the first key; the PDA seed), `sequence: u64` (withdrawals executed so far; signed into every message), `key_id: [u8;32]`, `key_ident: [u8;16]`, `key_q: u32` (always equal to `sequence`), `key_k: [u8;32]` (the current key), `reserved: u64` (tokens held for payouts not yet released). 174 bytes with the discriminator. Never closed. |
| Vault token account | ATA(vault, mint, mint's token program) | The **deposit address**. Created by `open_vault` (`init_if_needed`), never moves, never closed. |
| `Payout` | PDA `["payout", vault, u64le(sequence)]` | `version: u8` (= 1), `bump: u8`, `vault: Pubkey`, `sequence: u64`, `destination_owner: Pubkey`, `amount: u64`, `rent_payer: Pubkey`. 122 bytes. Created by `withdraw`, closed by `release` / `release_to` (rent to `rent_payer`). |

The vault address commits to the key that opened it, so nobody can open a vault at someone else's address with a different key. It never changes: every withdrawal replaces the key **in place** and increments `sequence`. The token account and the unreserved balance stay where they are.

The launchpad recognizes a vault by these bytes alone (`pq::is_vault_of`: owner `noised_vault`, discriminator `sha256("account:Vault")[..8]`, version byte 4 at offset 8, mint at 10..42; pinned against the vault crate in tests): the treasury and a commit's payout owners may be vaults.

### 3.3 Instructions

#### `open_vault(key_id_arg: [u8; 32], key: VaultKey)`
- Accounts (in order): `payer` (signer, writable), `mint`, `vault` (init, seeds `["vault", mint, key_id_arg]`), `vault_token_account` (ATA of the vault, `init_if_needed`: anyone may pre-create it without blocking the open), `token_program`, `associated_token_program`, `system_program`.
- Requires `key.q == 0` (`InvalidKeyIndex`), `key_id(key) == key_id_arg` (`KeyIdMismatch`) and an eligible mint (§3.5, `UnsupportedMint`). Writes `version = 4`, `sequence = 0`, `genesis_key_id = key_id = key_id_arg`, the key, `reserved = 0`. Emits `VaultOpened`.

#### `withdraw(args: WithdrawArgs)`

```rust
pub struct WithdrawArgs {
    pub signature: Vec<u8>,          // LM-OTS C || y[0..67], 2,176 bytes
    pub destination_owner: Pubkey,   // the recipient WALLET, never a token account
    pub max_amount: u64,             // upper bound; min(max_amount, balance - reserved) is reserved
    pub next_key_id: [u8; 32],       // key id of the key that controls the vault afterwards
    pub next_key: VaultKey,          // must hash to next_key_id; its q must be sequence + 1
}
```

- Accounts (in order): `payer` (signer, writable; pays the payout's rent, refunded at delivery; no authority over the vault), `mint`, `vault` (writable; seeds and `has_one = mint`), `vault_token_account` (**read-only**; ATA(vault, mint)), `payout` (writable, `init` at `["payout", vault, u64le(vault.sequence)]`), `token_program`, `system_program`.
- Checks: `key_id(next_key) == next_key_id` (`KeyIdMismatch`); `next_key_id != vault.key_id` (`InvalidNextKey`); `next_key.q == vault.sequence + 1` (`InvalidKeyIndex`; also refuses at `sequence = u32::MAX`); LM-OTS verification of `signature` over the §3.4 message with the vault's current key and current `sequence` (`InvalidSignature`).
- Effects: `amount = 0` if `destination_owner == vault`, else `min(max_amount, balance - reserved)`; `reserved += amount`; the `Payout` records the signed `sequence`, `destination_owner`, `amount` and `rent_payer = payer`; `sequence += 1`; the key becomes `next_key`. Emits `VaultWithdrawn`. **No token moves, and no account the recipient or any other third party controls is read or written.**

#### `release()`
- Permissionless. Pays a payout to the canonical ATA of its `destination_owner` (§3.7).
- Accounts (in order): `payer` (signer, writable; pays for the ATA if it is missing), `mint`, `vault` (writable), `vault_token_account` (writable), `payout` (writable; `has_one = vault, rent_payer, destination_owner`; closed to `rent_payer`), `rent_payer` (writable), `destination_owner`, `destination` (writable, unchecked by Anchor; checked in the handler, ignored when the payout is zero), `memo_program` (= SPL Memo `MemoSq4gqABAXKb96qnH8TysNcWxMyWCqXgDLGmfcHr`), `token_program`, `associated_token_program`, `system_program`; then the mint's transfer-hook extra accounts, if it has an active hook (for a Noised launch token: meta list, policy, instructions sysvar, hook program).

#### `release_to()`
- Signed by the payout's `destination_owner`. Pays the payout to **any** token account of the mint that the recipient names.
- Accounts (in order): `destination_owner` (signer), `mint`, `vault` (writable), `vault_token_account` (writable), `payout` (writable; same constraints as `release`), `rent_payer` (writable), `destination` (writable; a token account of `mint` under `token_program`, any owner), `memo_program`, `token_program`; then the transfer-hook extra accounts.

Both delivery instructions: `reserved -= amount`; if `amount > 0`, a memo CPI when the destination requires one, then `transfer_checked` of `amount` signed by the vault PDA; emit `PayoutReleased`; close the payout to `rent_payer`.

Errors: `KeyIdMismatch` (6000), `InvalidSignature` (6001), `InvalidNextKey` (6002), `UnsupportedMint` (6003), `InvalidDestination` (6004), `MathOverflow` (6005), `InvalidKeyIndex` (6006, round 2). Events: `VaultOpened {vault, mint, key_id}`, `VaultWithdrawn {vault, mint, sequence, destination_owner, amount, payout, next_key_id}`, `PayoutReleased {vault, payout, sequence, destination_owner, destination, amount}`.

### 3.4 Withdraw message (185 bytes, `programs/noised-vault/src/message.rs`)

| Offset | Bytes | Field |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 8 | `"PHX-PQV4"` (domain tag and version) |
| 8 | 1 | `0x01` (withdraw) |
| 9 | 32 | program ID |
| 41 | 32 | vault address |
| 73 | 32 | mint |
| 105 | 8 | `sequence`, u64 little-endian (the vault's value before this withdrawal) |
| 113 | 32 | `destination_owner` (recipient wallet) |
| 145 | 8 | `max_amount`, u64 little-endian |
| 153 | 32 | `next_key_id` |

The message contains no blockhash or timestamp, so a dropped transaction can be re-broadcast with the same signature. The `sequence` makes a signature valid at exactly one position of the vault's history.

### 3.5 Mint eligibility (`open_vault`, `token.rs::require_supported_mint`)

SPL Token mints are accepted. A Token-2022 mint is refused (`UnsupportedMint`) if it has any extension that leaves an Ed25519 path to the deposit:

| Refused | Why |
|---|---|
| NonTransferable | tokens could never leave |
| PermanentDelegate | an Ed25519 key can move tokens out of any account |
| MintCloseAuthority **with an authority** | at zero supply the mint can be closed and re-created at the same address with a permanent delegate, which would then drain the vault. An absent or `None` close authority can never be set later, so checking once at `open_vault` is complete. |
| TransferFeeConfig with a fee-config **or** withdraw-withheld authority | a stolen authority could raise the fee to 100% and collect the withheld tokens on the next payout |
| any extension type the interface crate does not know | fails to parse |

A transfer fee whose two authorities are both `None` is accepted: it applies only to the amount a payout moves, like any transfer of that mint. Freeze authority, Pausable, DefaultAccountState and transfer hooks are accepted: they can delay a payout but never block `withdraw`, and never take funds. Clients warn about them (§4.1 `checkVaultMint`).

### 3.6 Why a valid signature always executes

A one-time signature, once revealed, must stay executable, or its owner would be tempted to sign a second message with the same key. `withdraw` therefore reads nothing a third party controls:
- `payer` is the submitter's own signer.
- `mint` is fixed by the vault and, being eligible, cannot be closed.
- `vault` is owned by this program.
- `vault_token_account` is read-only and only its balance is read. Only the vault PDA could close it or reassign its owner, and the program never does. A frozen account still reads.
- `payout` is a fresh PDA of this program for this sequence. Only this program can create an account there, and each sequence is used once.

`withdraw` makes no token transfer and never touches the recipient's accounts. Once a correct signature for the vault's current sequence exists, anyone can execute it at any later time. Only four things can stop it, and the SDK rules out all four before signing: a second signature by the same key for the same sequence executed first, which is forbidden (§3.10); a message whose `next_key_id` equals the current key id (`InvalidNextKey`); a next key whose index is not `sequence + 1` (`InvalidKeyIndex`); and a submitter that does not supply the next public key matching the signed `next_key_id`. Clients store that key with the signature.

### 3.7 Delivery rules

- `release` pays only the **canonical ATA** of `destination_owner` for the mint and its token program, and creates it when missing (the caller pays its rent). It requires that account to be owned by the token program and to hold this mint. It deliberately does **not** compare the token account's owner field, exactly like the launchpad's `AtaCreator::ensure`: only the ATA program can create an account at that address, and only the wallet itself can reassign it, so a reassigned ATA is where the wallet chose its funds to go.
- If the destination is a Token-2022 account that requires incoming transfer memos, `release` and `release_to` CPI SPL Memo immediately before the transfer. Token-2022 accepts a memo only as the previous sibling instruction, so a memo added by the submitter at top level would not count.
- `release_to` is for recipients whose canonical ATA cannot receive: a confidential-transfer account that refuses non-confidential credits, an account frozen by the mint's freeze authority, or any account the recipient prefers. The recipient wallet signs it. An off-curve recipient, such as a program PDA, can use it only if its program signs by CPI.
- A payout can be delayed by: the mint's freeze authority (vault or destination account frozen), a pause, a transfer hook refusing the transfer (for example Launch Guard's wallet-to-wallet lock or max-wallet during a launch's guard window, or, since audit round 3's F2, a `release_to` destination without Token-2022's `ImmutableOwner` extension during that window: `MutableOwner`, until `guard_end_ts`), or a destination that cannot receive. A launch's creator rules never delay a payout sent as the vault's own top-level instruction (§1.9, review 4, R1). The payout stays reserved and can be delivered later. A recipient can delay only its own payout, never the withdrawal and never the rest of the vault.
- Zero payouts, including any withdrawal to the vault itself, transfer nothing and only close the payout. `withdraw` with `max_amount = 0` is a pure key rotation. Its `next_key` may be anyone's key, so it hands control of everything in the vault to that key's holder without moving a token: no transfer hook sees it, a launch's wallet-to-wallet lock included (THREAT_MODEL 5.2.11).

### 3.8 Deposits and what cannot be recovered

- The deposit address is the vault's token account, ATA(vault, mint), not the vault PDA. Anyone can deposit with an ordinary `transfer_checked`. A wallet that sends the vault's own mint to the vault address resolves the same ATA. For wSOL, transfer SOL into the vault's token account and call SPL Token `SyncNative`, which anyone can call.
- **Documented limitation, not fixed on-chain:** native SOL or tokens of another mint sent to the vault PDA itself are unrecoverable. No instruction moves lamports out of a `Vault` account or tokens out of ATA(vault, other mint). SOL sent to a vault address before `open_vault` is absorbed into that account the same way.

### 3.9 Transactions and the v1 wire format

- A `withdraw` carries 2,312 bytes of instruction data (8-byte discriminator, 4-byte length prefix, 2,176-byte signature, 32-byte recipient, 8-byte maximum, 32-byte next key id, 52-byte next key), so it must be sent as a **v1 transaction** (SIMD-0385, 4,096-byte limit, live on mainnet since 2026-09-15). `withdraw` alone, or `withdraw` followed by `release` of the same payout, fits in one v1 transaction. Measured in LiteSVM against the mainnet token programs (`tests/integration` `withdraw_and_release_sizes_and_compute`, ranges over 26 runs on 2026-10-08): `withdraw` alone is 2,693 bytes and about 79k–106k CU (a legacy encoding would be 2,777 bytes); with `release` it is 2,845 bytes and about 121k–172k CU for an SPL Token mint, 2,977 bytes and about 145k–190k CU for Token-2022 with the Launch Guard hook. Loaded account data is at most about 1.95 MB. The fee payer should be a throwaway Ed25519 key funded with a little SOL: it has no authority over the vault. `open_vault`, deposits, `release` and `release_to` fit ordinary legacy transactions, so no user wallet ever has to sign a v1 transaction.
- The launchpad's `publish_metadata` (up to 3,621 bytes), `publish_rules` (580 to 1,522 bytes), `attest_launch` (3,005 bytes, 3,037 with a rules account), `pq_authorize` (about 2,924 bytes) and the keepers' `[launch, commit]` bundle are v1 transactions too, each signed only by a throwaway or keeper key.
- **v1 wire format** (from `solana-message` 4.6 `versions/v1` and `solana-transaction` 4.3 `versioned/mod.rs`; implemented by `sdk/src/tx/v1.ts` and checked byte for byte against vectors from `tests/integration/tests/sdk_vectors.rs` and by `harness.rs::v1_wire_format_matches_the_spec`):
  - `transaction = message || signatures`. The message comes FIRST; `signatures` is a fixed array of `num_required_signatures` 64-byte Ed25519 signatures over the message bytes, with **no** length prefix (unlike legacy/v0).
  - `message = 0x81 (version prefix 0x80|1) || header [num_required_signatures, num_readonly_signed, num_readonly_unsigned] || config_mask (u32 LE) || recent_blockhash [32] || num_instructions (u8, ≤ 64) || num_addresses (u8, ≤ 64) || addresses [32 × n] || config_values || instruction_headers || instruction_payloads`.
  - `config_values`, in this order, only for set mask bits: priority fee u64 LE (bits 0 and 1 both set: the total fee in lamports, not a price per unit), compute-unit limit u32 LE (bit 2), loaded-accounts-data-size limit u32 LE (bit 3), heap size u32 LE (bit 4, multiple of 1024, 32–256 KiB). **Always set bits 2 and 3**: unset means a limit of 0. ComputeBudget instructions are ignored in v1.
  - `instruction_header = program_id_index u8 || num_accounts u8 || data_len u16` (little-endian), one per instruction, all headers before all payloads; `instruction_payload = account indices [u8 × num_accounts] || data`.
  - Address order is that of `CompiledKeys::compile`: the fee payer first, then writable signers, readonly signers, writable non-signers and readonly non-signers, each group in byte order of the keys. No address lookup tables.
  - Config words the SDK uses: `withdraw` 200,000 CU plus 80,000 (SPL Token), 130,000 (Token-2022, no hook or Launch Guard) or 300,000 (another hook) for a `release` leg, with 4 MiB of loaded data (`VAULT_COMPUTE_UNITS`, `VAULT.WITHDRAW_LOADED_ACCOUNTS_BYTES`); `attest_launch` 300,000 CU and 2 MiB (`ATTEST_COMPUTE_UNITS`, `ATTEST_LOADED_ACCOUNTS_BYTES`); `[launch, commit]` 330,000 CU and 8 MiB (`COMPUTE_UNITS.launchCommitBundle`, `LAUNCH_BUNDLE_LOADED_ACCOUNTS_BYTES`); `publish_metadata` 100,000 CU and 2 MiB (SDK 0.4).
  - RPC submission of a v1 transaction over 1,232 bytes must use base64 encoding (FACTS, follow-up on V1 wallets and TPU).

### 3.10 Client key derivation and signing rules (SDK)

> The domain tags below (`phalanx/pq/...`) keep the project's original name (it was renamed Noised): they are protocol constants inside every signature and key derivation, so they never change.

- A **separate** 24-word BIP-39 "PQ recovery phrase" (never derived from the Ed25519 wallet). `master = BIP39 seed (64 bytes)`. The same phrase (or a passkey's PRF secret, §4.1 `pqIdentity`) also derives the creator's LMS identities, on their own domain-separated path ([POST_QUANTUM.md](POST_QUANTUM.md) §3).
- A user can hold several vaults per (cluster, mint), numbered by `account` (u32). The key that signs a vault's withdrawal at `sequence` is derived from `(account, sequence)`; the genesis key is `sequence = 0`. Keys never repeat because `(account, sequence)` never repeats.

  ```text
  path = cluster (1 byte: 0 mainnet, 1 devnet, 2 localnet) || mint (32) || u32le(account) || u32le(sequence)
  SEED = SHA256("phalanx/pq/lmots/seed" || master || path)
  I    = SHA256("phalanx/pq/lmots/id"   || master || path)[..16]
  q    = sequence        (the program requires it, §3.3: keys exist for sequences 0..=2^32 - 1, and at
                          sequence 2^32 - 1 no withdrawal can execute, so clients refuse to sign there)
  ```
- Vault address of `account`: `["vault", mint, key_id(key(account, 0))]`. The current key of a vault is `key(account, vault.sequence)`; before signing, the client checks that its key id equals the vault's `key_id` and refuses to sign otherwise. The next key is `key(account, vault.sequence + 1)`.
- Randomizer: `C = HMAC-SHA256(key = SEED, data = "phalanx/pq/lmots/C" || I || u32be(q) || msg)`, HMAC as in RFC 2104 (the construction FIPS 205 uses for SLH-DSA's `PRF_msg`; Rust: `noised_lmots::PrivateKey::randomizer` and `sign_deterministic`). It is unpredictable without `SEED`, and signing the same message again reproduces the same signature. The signer verifies its own signature before returning it. Known answer, which every signer must reproduce: `SEED = 0x11 × 32`, `I = 0x22 × 16`, `q = 7`, `msg = "phalanx"` gives `C = efff9f338802ed72a95c53d848647c110c67a6a943e9aeecf572bd978e9d79d3` (`crates/noised-lmots/tests/lmots.rs::deterministic_randomizer_vector`). Two signers that derive `C` differently produce two signatures for one withdrawal, which the next rule forbids.
- Vectors: `tests/integration/vectors/vault_withdraw.json` holds the derivation (`derivation`: path, `SEED`, `I`, `q`, `K` and key id for several `(account, sequence)` pairs and all three clusters), the randomizer known answer (`randomizer`), and per withdrawal the derived `C` and signature (`derivedRandomizer`, `derivedSignature`). `tests/integration/tests/sdk_vectors.rs` checks the file against the Rust code (and that every committed withdrawal names the key of the next position). The SDK's vector test (`sdk/test/vault_vectors.test.ts`) reads a byte-identical copy.
- Never produce a second signature with a key: not for a different recipient, amount or next key, and not for the same message with a different randomizer. The SDK enforces this with a durable **vault-key ledger** (`createVaultKeyLedger`): the key is reserved for its message with the store's atomic `add` before any signature exists, and the signature is recorded before it is returned; any other withdrawal for that key is refused (`VaultKeyInUseError`, carrying the recorded withdrawal to re-broadcast). An in-process guard additionally refuses a key the process saw sign another message. Only ever re-broadcast a stored signature. Before signing for a vault's current sequence, look for an already revealed signature in the vault's transaction history, failed transactions included (`findPendingWithdrawal`), and re-broadcast it instead.
- Before signing, check the recipient (`checkRecipient`, §4.1) and have the user confirm the result (`RecipientConfirmation`); check the RPC's genesis hash against the cluster (`assertCluster`). The program cannot tell a wallet from a token account, a mint or a PDA, and the message binds no cluster.

## 7. Invariants (audit checklist)

1. Every certified mint: mint authority None, freeze authority None, metadata and pointer authorities None, extensions exactly {MetadataPointer, TokenMetadata, TransferHook(noised_hook)} until graduation, then TransferHook program and authority None.
2. Every launchpad-created DBC config has exactly the fixed values of §1.2.
3. No sell (a transfer into the DBC base vault from any other account) and no zero-amount transfer that is not a buy is ever refused by the hook; no rule applies after `guard_end_ts`; mints without a Noised policy are never restricted. A transfer out of the base vault is always a buy, never a sell, whatever its destination.
4. Batch: `sum(commits) == total_committed`; `quote_spent <= min(total_committed, batch_total_cap)`; each commit is paid once, only to its payout owners fixed at the first commit; payouts never exceed `tokens_out` and `total_committed - quote_spent`; expired batches refund 100%; every commit naming one vault as token owner, together, stays within `batch_wallet_cap`.
5. Fees: `distribute` pays out at most the balance; the platform share uses the snapshot bps and goes to the treasury, a wSOL PQ vault; recipients and bps never change after `create_launch`.
6. No instruction moves funds owned by launchpad PDAs except: batch buy (DBC), batch claims (to payout owners), fee distribution (to treasury/recipients), base-fee burns.
7. No admin: `Protocol` cannot be modified; no pause; the autopilot has no admin, no pause and no configuration account; programs become immutable after audit (`solana program set-upgrade-authority --final`).
8. Vault (v4): tokens leave a vault's token account only through `release` / `release_to` of a `Payout`. A `Payout` is created only by a valid LM-OTS signature of the vault's current key over the exact (program, vault, mint, sequence, destination_owner, max_amount, next_key_id). It holds `min(max_amount, balance - reserved)` (0 when `destination_owner` is the vault) and is paid only to `destination_owner`'s canonical ATA (`release`) or to a token account that `destination_owner` signs for (`release_to`). Every withdrawal increments `sequence` and replaces the key with the signed next key, whose index is `sequence + 1` (the genesis key's is 0), so no key ever controls a vault twice and no signature executes twice. A genesis key id can never be reopened, and the vault and its token account are never closed or moved.
9. Shield: escrow balance >= zToken supply after every instruction.
10. A valid signature always executes: `withdraw` reads no account a third party controls (§3.6).
11. `reserved <= vault token balance`, before and after every vault instruction.
12. Before `snipe_shield_end_ts` the only buy is the batch buy: the hook refuses every transfer out of the DBC base vault, whatever its amount (zero included) and whoever receives the tokens, unless the transaction's current top-level instruction is the launchpad's `execute_batch`.
13. The batch is always the pool's first swap, or it refunds: `execute_batch` requires the DBC pool's `has_swap == 0` (`PoolAlreadyTraded`), so a batch whose pool was traded first can never execute and every commit is refunded in full after `execute_end_ts`.
14. A launch goes live only with an attestation: `launch` requires `["attestation", launch]` written by `attest_launch` after an LMS signature by `launch.pq_identity` verified on-chain over the 221-byte message v3 naming this program, launch, `params_hash`, creator, metadata hash, rules hash and window; and it runs only inside that window. Every certificate has `PQ_ATTESTED` and names the identity, the leaf and the attestation slot. An attestation is replaced only while the launch is `Configured` and after its window ended, only by another valid attestation of the launch's identity, and every field is overwritten; an attestation is recorded only with at least 60 s of its window left when it lands; once the launch is `Live` the attestation never changes (audit round 3, F1).
15. A leaf of an identity is accepted at most once, ever: the identity's bitmap bit is set by the first valid signature at that leaf, and every valid signature at an unused leaf lands and sets it, whatever it signed.
16. The mint's metadata is the attested record: `TokenMeta` records are written whole at the address of their content hash and never modified or closed; `launch` uses only the record the attestation names and requires the mint's TokenMetadata to hold exactly its name, symbol and derived uri and nothing else (`METADATA_ONCHAIN`); the certificate names the record.
17. A hook policy's snipe-shield end only moves earlier, and only by the launchpad's `execute_batch` for its own launch after the batch bought; nothing else in a policy ever changes.
18. Before `guard_end_ts`, no transfer that is not a sell (and not of zero amount) lands in a token account whose owner can be reassigned (no `ImmutableOwner` extension), except an account owned by an exempt launchpad PDA (audit round 3, F2); sells and transfers after the guard are unaffected (invariant 3 still holds). This covers reassignment only: control of an account's owner (a shared wallet, a PDA or smart wallet, a PQ vault's key, a delegate) can still change without any transfer (THREAT_MODEL 5.2.11).
19. Creator launch rules only refuse, and only before `guard_end_ts`: they run after every built-in allow and denial and never see a sell, a batch claim, the batch buy, a zero-amount transfer, a transfer into or out of an exempt launchpad account, anything at or after `guard_end_ts`, or a holder's exit whose current top-level instruction is the Noised vault or shield (review 4, R1); sells and everything after the guard are decided from the policy head before the policy is decoded (R3).
20. The installed rules are exactly the attested bytes: `launch` uses only the `LaunchRules` record whose hash the attestation signed (message v3), re-hashes its bytes (R6a), `create_policy` verifies them for the guard, and the re-read policy must hold exactly them; the certificate records `rules_hash` and sets `RULES_BOUND` exactly when they are non-empty; the zero hash installs no rules.
21. An `Authorization` is written at most once per `(launch, kind, payload_hash)`, only after an LMS signature by the launch's identity verified on-chain over the 140-byte message, only for kinds 1 to 3 (review 4, Ra), with time and slot from `Clock`; every other valid signature only burns its leaf (in the bitmap attestations use); it is never modified or closed.
22. Autopilot treasury value leaves only through: burns of treasury tokens; the bound pool's wSOL vault in a buyback whose received tokens are all burned in the same instruction; the active graph's recipients' canonical wSOL ATAs; the caller's tip (bounded, §9.5). The treasury never sells the token; no treasury account is closed, delegated or re-owned; the only programs called are DAMM v2, Token-2022 and SPL Token, at fixed ids.
23. The active graph changes only through an `Authorization` of the launch identity, each used at most once and never after a newer one it could undo; a replacement (of an active or a queued graph) takes effect at least 72 h after it became visible and after its authorization landed; a frozen graph never changes; a freeze is used only until the `not_after` it was signed with (review 4, R5); a revoked operator gets no power back from anything signed before its revocation; a revocation names the activation it revokes (`active_auth`, payload v3), so an operator appointed again can always be revoked again (audit round 4, A4-3).
24. A buyback trades only on the pool bound by `bind_pool`, only with observations spanning at least 60 s in the current and previous 900-second bucket, only while the spot price is at most the node's premium above `min(reference, lowest observed price)`, and with a minimum out priced at that bound plus the premium; the reference rises at most about 5% of price per hour and falls at once to the window's highest price (`reference.rs`).

## 8. Trust that remains (must be disclosed in UI and docs)

- Meteora DBC/DAMM v2 programs are upgradeable by a 4-of-7 Squads v3 multisig with no timelock. An upgrade can change account layouts and versions our finalized code reads (DBC 0.2.2, pending as PR #211, adds a config version byte and transfer-fee fields in former padding but keeps every 0.2.1 instruction layout and config offset) and can add operator powers: draft PR #214 (not in 0.2.2) would let a single DBC operator revoke the transfer hook of any hook pool before graduation, which would end the snipe shield and every guard rule early (the batch would then refund: `execute_batch` fails under DBC 0.2.1's `NoTransferHookProgram` check, and in any case cannot run after another swap). The snipe shield also relies on DBC making a Token-2022 transfer, which the hook sees, for every buy, even one that pays out nothing, and on DBC refusing the base vault as a buy's output account; if a release changed that, invariant 13 turns the batch into a full refund.
- DAMM v2 operators (single keys) can disable pools or raise post-migration fees (base fee up to 10%). Every graduated pool also carries the default dynamic fee of its migration config, up to about 20% of the tier on top of it.
- Token-2022 is upgradeable by a 4-of-6 multisig; the ATA program and validator feature gates can change the runtime (e.g. the ZK ElGamal kill switch). Solana consensus (validator and vote keys) is classical.
- Transaction ordering: block engines and the BAM / Harmonic gateways that front about 62% of leader slots see transactions before inclusion and can reorder, delay or drop them (they cannot change signed content). Anyone can buy priority and fill an account's 24M-CU per-block write budget: the commit and execution windows only make that exclusion cost more, linearly. The same contention on the `Launch` account can keep `launch` out of an attested window; the creator then signs a new window (audit round 3, F1), so it delays a launch at the attacker's cost instead of ending it. Inside the attested launch window the first `launch` to land wins; a relayer of an attestation can delay it but not change what it does.
- Wallets' classical keys: every Ed25519 wallet, the creator's included, is exposed to a quantum attacker; holdings outside PQ vaults, the fee recipients that are ordinary wallets and the creator's `create_launch` signature are classical. Wallet support for v1 transactions is uneven (Phantom does not sign them, Ledger cannot); Noised never needs a user wallet to sign one.
- The mint authorities of non-Noised mints held in vaults: freeze, pause and hook authorities can delay payouts but never block `withdraw`; a mint authority can dilute.
- Passkey users trust their authenticator, and with synced passkeys the sync provider; the PRF secret travels under classical channels to roaming authenticators ([POST_QUANTUM.md](POST_QUANTUM.md) §3).
- Creator launch rules are disclosed, not prevented or judged (review 4): a rule set can restrict buys to listed wallets (insider-only buying, a private sale) for up to the whole guard (at most 72 h), while every holder can still sell; vacuous rules (e.g. `DENY` when `FALSE`) verify, never refuse, and still set `RULES_BOUND`, which means "rules are bound", not "protected"; rules see token transfers only, so control that changes without a transfer is invisible to them; and routing through the Noised vault or shield bypasses rules, never built-ins (§1.9). Verifiers and badges warn about buys restricted to a key list, rules targeting wallet-to-wallet transfers, rules active for more than an hour and vacuous rules, and show the rules in plain English.
- Autopilot (§9): the operator is a classical Ed25519 key that chooses only the timing and amount of its gated nodes within the graph's bounds until the launch identity revokes it, and can also simply not run. Automation runs only if someone calls it (keepers, paid by tips; a node whose `p1` is below 0.001 SOL pays less than the full tip, so keepers may ignore it). Buyback prices are bounded, not guaranteed: a keeper that sandwiches a buyback gains at most about premium × amount against the bound; one party making every observation of a window can walk the reference up at most about 5% per hour (paying pool fees both ways) or, after a market fall nobody observed, price a buyback at the window's lowest price; one wrong-side observation every 15 minutes, from anyone who pays the pool's fees for a round trip each time, holds back any step with a price condition, a burn or a payout as much as a buyback (liveness only; §9.6, THREAT_MODEL R37). One honest observation per window closes the first two (every keeper run is one; `update_price` is free), so the bound depends on one: keepers observe before every buyback, and an independent observer every 10 minutes or less is recommended (§9.6; audit round 4, A4-1). Withheld authorizations: once landed, an `Authorization` can be used by anyone at any later time it is still valid (an activation within its signed `activate_at` and the timelock, a freeze only until its signed `not_after`, a revocation at any time), and a signed but unlanded one can be held back by whoever relays it; order is by landing slot, not signing time. `bind_pool` relies on DAMM v2 letting only a config's pool creator authority create a pool under it and on DBC signing as that authority only in migration. The first transaction in a window wins: whoever lands first runs a due node and takes its tip, or applies a due update. Keepers, the operator, recipients that are ordinary wallets, Meteora and Token-2022 stay classical; graph changes are post-quantum (LMS identity) and the treasury has no key (a PDA).
- Our own programs are upgradeable until finalized; after audit the upgrade authority is set to None.

## 9. Autopilot program (`noised_autopilot`)

Post-launch automation of a launch's **treasury**: graphs (PXG1) whose nodes buy back and burn the token, burn treasury tokens or pay declared recipients, run by anyone when due, within bounds the chain enforces. There is no admin, no pause and no configuration account; no instruction touches the launch, its hook policy, its LP or its mint's authorities; value moves only through DAMM v2, Token-2022 and SPL Token, at fixed ids. Its audit invariants are listed in `programs/noised-autopilot/src/lib.rs` (1 to 7; §7 invariants 22 to 24); its residuals are in §8.

### 9.1 Accounts

| `Autopilot` | `["autopilot", launch]` | Version 1, 1,244 bytes (fields below; 1,076 before audit round 4 appended three fields, with no version change: nothing is deployed). Created by `init_autopilot`. |

QSL-1's programmable checks do this.

### 9.2 Graphs (PXG1)

Normative format: `crates/noised-rules/README.md`. At most 2,044 bytes; up to 16 nodes, up to 8 recipients (bps summing to 10,000), an optional operator key; `graph_hash = SHA256("PHX-GRAPH-V1" ‖ bytes)`. A node has a trigger (1 `EVERY` at least 600 s, 2 `AFTER` another node, 3 `ON_GRADUATED`, 4 `WHEN`), an optional condition (treasury SOL or tokens at least, graduated, price at most or at least, time, runs in epoch), an action (1 `BUYBACK_BURN`, 2 `BURN`, 3 `DISTRIBUTE_SOL`; 4 and 5 are reserved and refused), `operator_gated`, `epoch_secs` (at least 3,600), `budget_per_epoch`, `p1` (the per-run cap), `p2` (the buyback premium in bps, at most 2,000) and `max_runs_per_epoch`. A price condition never reads spot alone: it must hold across the reference and every price observed in the window (§9.6, "Price conditions"). A price condition's argument (`PRICE_LTE`, `PRICE_GTE`) is a `u64` compared with sqrt prices (Q64.64), so it can only name a price below one lamport per raw token unit: 0.001 SOL per whole token at 6 decimals, 1 SOL at 9 (audit round 4, L-i3); the SDK refuses a higher one (`PriceOutOfRange`).

### 9.3 Graph changes and authorizations

Every graph change needs an `Authorization` written by the launchpad's `pq_authorize` (§1.4) of the matching kind over the matching payload: launchpad-owned, at `["auth", launch, [kind], payload_hash]` with its stored bump, version 1, exactly 2,669 bytes, for that launch, by the launch's `pq_identity`, with a 2,508-byte signature (else `InvalidAuthorization`). Freshness (`logic::check_fresh`): authorizations are ordered by (landing slot, account address); each is used at most once; an activation must be newer than the latest activation and revocation used; a freeze newer than the latest freeze and activation used; a revocation newer than the latest revocation used (else `StaleAuthorization`). So a revoked operator gets no power back from anything signed before its revocation, and a freeze cannot cancel a newer queued graph. Replacing an active graph, or a queued one, takes effect only after `UPDATE_TIMELOCK_SECS` = 259,200 s (72 h).

Payloads (`launchpad.rs`; integers little-endian): kind 1 `SHA256("PHX-GRAPH-ACT-V1" ‖ autopilot ‖ graph_hash ‖ i64 activate_at)`; kind 2 `SHA256("PHX-GRAPH-FRZ-V2" ‖ autopilot ‖ graph_hash ‖ i64 not_after)` (review 4, R5); kind 3 `SHA256("PHX-OP-REVOKE-V3" ‖ autopilot ‖ active_graph ‖ operator ‖ active_auth)` (audit round 4, A4-3; v2 lacked `active_auth`). A revocation therefore names the activation it revokes: once another activation is applied, even of the same graph with the same operator, an earlier revocation can no longer be used and the identity signs a new one, which always gets its own `Authorization` address. Before v3, an operator revoked and then appointed again by the same graph could never be revoked again (the new signature had the same payload, so it only burned its leaf, and the old authorization was stale).

The **operator** (for example an AI agent) is an Ed25519 key named in the graph: it alone runs operator-gated nodes and chooses only their timing (within the trigger) and amount (1 to the limit). It cannot widen any bound, the launch identity can revoke it (kind 3, also on a frozen graph), and its gated nodes stop if it does not run.

- **`update_price()`**: `autopilot` (w), `damm_pool`. Permissionless and free (no tip), any time after `bind_pool` (`NotGraduated` before), never "too soon". Records an observation and moves the reference (§9.6). Emits `ReferencePriceUpdated` on every call.

### 9.5 `run`

First, no queued update may be due (`PendingUpdateDue`). Then, once graduated, the run reads the bound pool (a buyback's pool and remaining accounts must be valid: `InvalidPool`, `InvalidDammAccounts`; a burn or a distribution that cannot read it skips this), observes its price and moves the reference as `update_price` does, so every run is an observation. Then `logic::plan_run` checks, in order: the node exists (`NoSuchNode`); a gated node is signed by the stored operator (`NoOperator`, `WrongOperator`); the node is due: trigger, runs this epoch (`MaxRunsReached`), budget this epoch (`BudgetSpent`), the condition, its price leaves judged across the **reference and every price observed in the window**, this run's observation included (§9.6; `ConditionFalse`; `NodeNotDue` otherwise); then per action, as below.

- **Amount.** The node's run cap is `cap = min(p1 - partial_spent[node], budget left this epoch)`, and `limit = min(cap, treasury balance)`. The operator gives an amount in `1..=limit` on its gated nodes (`InvalidAmount`); a keeper that names no amount moves `limit` (`NothingToDo` at 0). For a `BUYBACK_BURN` a keeper may instead choose an amount from `min(floor, limit)` (at least 1) up to `limit`, where `floor` = 1% of the pool's wSOL (`AmountTooSmall` below; `InvalidAmount` above; `AmountNotAllowed` for other actions); the amount must equal `cap` or leave at least `floor` of it (`cap - amount >= floor`, else `RemainderTooSmall`), so no part leaves a remainder too small to swap.
- **Partial runs** (audit round 4, A4-2). A keeper-chosen buyback amount below `cap` is a partial run: it spends from the epoch's budget and adds its amount to `partial_spent[node]`, but it sets neither `last_run` nor `last_seq` and does not count toward `max_runs_per_epoch`, so the node stays due and the rest of its run can follow at once. Its tip is at most `TIP_LAMPORTS × amount / p1`, so the parts of one run earn less than one tip together. Because the cap shrinks by `partial_spent`, the parts never move more than the run would, even across epochs. A completed run (any run that is not partial) counts as before and clears `partial_spent[node]`, as does a newly applied graph. Without a chosen amount a run is never partial, even when the treasury holds less than the cap, and the operator's amounts are never partial. Before the fix, a keeper's smallest allowed buyback used up a daily node's run and earned a full tip.
- **`BUYBACK_BURN`**: only after `bind_pool` (`NotGraduated`); needs observations spanning at least 60 s in the current and previous 900-second bucket (`PriceNotObserved`); requires `spot <= bound × (1 + p2)`, with `bound = min(reference, lowest price observed in the window)` (`PremiumExceeded`); swaps exactly the amount of treasury wSOL for the token on the bound pool (DAMM v2 `swap`, exact in) with a minimum out priced from the bound at the premium (`floor(floor(amount × 2^64 / price(bound)) × 10,000 / (10,000 + p2))`, `PriceOutOfRange` at 0), checks that exactly the amount left and at least the minimum arrived (`SwapShortfall`), then burns every token received in the same instruction. Remaining accounts, exactly 5 (`InvalidDammAccounts`): DAMM v2 pool authority, the pool's token A vault, token B vault, DAMM v2 event authority, DAMM v2 program.
- **`BURN`**: burns treasury tokens; no remaining accounts (`UnexpectedAccounts`).
- **`DISTRIBUTE_SOL`**: plans `floor(amount × bps / 10,000)` for each recipient (dust stays) and pays it to the recipient's canonical wSOL ATA (SPL Token). Remaining accounts: the canonical wSOL ATA address of every recipient, writable, in graph order, whether or not an account exists there (`InvalidRecipientAccounts`). A recipient whose address does not hold an initialized SPL Token account of wSOL owned by that recipient (none there, or one whose owner field was reassigned) is skipped and its share stays in the treasury; a share that rounds to 0 is neither paid nor skipped; a run that pays nobody fails `NoRecipientAccount` (audit round 4, M4). Keepers never create recipient accounts. The node's budget is charged the planned total (`NodeRan.spent`), skipped shares included; what was paid is `spent - kept`.
- **Tip.** After the action the run is recorded and the caller's `tip_account` receives, from the treasury's wSOL, `min(100,000 lamports, the run's own maximum, what is left of 5,000,000 lamports this UTC day, the treasury's wSOL after the action, 10% of the value the run moved)`. The run's own maximum is 100,000 lamports, or `100,000 × amount / p1` for a partial buyback. The leg that completes a split run (its amount is the rest of the run, `cap`) is not partial and may earn the full 100,000, so a node run split into partial buybacks earns up to about twice the tip of an unsplit one; it is still at most 10% of the value moved and within the daily cap, and `audit4.rs` asserts exactly this (accepted: review of the framework-free rewrite, autopilot F2). The value is the lamports swapped for a buyback, the lamports actually paid for a distribution (skipped shares excluded), and for a burn the tokens burned valued at `min(reference, lowest price observed in the window, this run's observation included)` after graduation and at nothing before it, so a burn before graduation earns no tip (audit round 4, A4-4: before the fix, burns were valued at the reference, which before graduation is the migration price, and curve tokens burned through the treasury earned more in tips than they cost). Emits `NodeRan`.

### 9.6 Reference price and observations

Observations (`update_price` and every run) keep, per 900-second bucket, only the lowest and the highest pool price, so extra observations cannot hide one; the current and the previous bucket (the last 15 to 30 minutes) are the window, usable once its earliest observation is at least 60 s old. The reference moves only to the window's edge: it rises toward the window's lowest price when every observation is above it, by at most 243 sqrt-bps per hour (about 5% of price per hour) from an allowance capped at one hour that only real rises spend; it falls at once to the window's highest price when every observation is below it. Bounds this gives and leaves: §7 invariant 24, §8.

**Price conditions** (review F1 of the framework-free rewrite, 2026-10-13). A run judges its node's price leaves over the range from `min(reference, lowest price observed in the window)` to `max(reference, highest price observed in the window)`, the window's observations counted however recent and this run's own included (`logic::condition_prices`, `reference::observed`): `PRICE_GTE x` holds only if the low end is at least `x`, `PRICE_LTE x` only if the high end is at most `x`, and under `NOT` the other end decides (`Graph::condition_over`: three-valued, a leaf "holds at every price of the range" or "at some", `NOT` swaps the two). So an observation can make a price condition false, never true. Before, conditions read the reference alone, and since one observation on the other side of the reference vetoes its move, a pump in the run's own transaction (buy, `run`, sell back) kept a `PRICE_GTE` node running after a fall, and a dump a `PRICE_LTE` node after a rise. The cost: after a real move a condition waits until the window holds no observation from before it (at most 30 minutes) and the reference has followed.

**Price-gated nodes can be held back, never set off** (follow-up review of the fix, L-1; accepted). The same rule lets anyone keep a price condition false: one observation on its wrong side, made in one transaction (a trade across the threshold, `update_price`, the trade back), counts for the rest of its bucket and the next, whoever else observes. One round trip per 900-second bucket holds back any price-gated node, a `BURN` or a `DISTRIBUTE_SOL` as much as a `BUYBACK_BURN`, for as long as the attacker pays the pool's fees: about 96 × 2 × fee × the volume that moves the pool past the threshold, per day (about 2.4 SOL a day for a full-range pool with 100 SOL a side, a threshold 5% away in sqrt price, about 10% in price, and a 0.25% fee). Nothing leaves the treasury, one outlier counts for at most 30 minutes, and the creator can replace the graph after the 72 h timelock. Any rule under which an observation can make a condition false has this property. So a threshold close to the market is cheap to hold back, and a payout that must happen on time should not depend on a price condition alone (THREAT_MODEL R37).

**The evaluation errs only toward false** (follow-up review I-1). Each leaf is judged on its own end of the range, so a condition that tests the price twice and needs different leaves at different prices is false over a range that straddles its thresholds, even where every single price passes: `PRICE_GTE a OR PRICE_LTE b` with `b >= a`, or `PRICE_GTE a OR NOT PRICE_GTE a`. It is never true where some price of the range fails, and a wider range never turns false into true (checked exhaustively over every condition of up to five nodes). Bands (`PRICE_GTE a AND PRICE_LTE b`) and their negations are exact, and over a single price it is exactly the one-price evaluation (`crates/noised-rules/README.md`).

**The bound needs an honest observation in the window** (audit round 4, A4-1). Every observation in a window can come from one party. After a fall nobody observed, a keeper-attacker that makes a pumped observation (buy, `update_price`, sell back, in one transaction) a minute before a node is due is the window's only observer, so the bound is the stale reference, not the fallen market. One honest observation of the fall in the window closes this. So a keeper makes its own `update_price` observation at least 60 s before every buyback, even when an observation is already counting (SDK 0.9.0), and whoever runs keepers should run an independent `update_price` at least every 10 minutes, so that each 900-second bucket holds an honest observation (OPERATIONS.md, Keepers).

### 9.7 Reading the launchpad (review 4, R6b)

The autopilot does not link the launchpad: it reads its accounts by layout, checking owner, discriminator, version and length first, and only at known versions: `Launch` version 2 (status at 13, `dbc_config` 78, `base_mint` 174, `pq_identity` 553; length at least 585; else `NotALaunch`), `Certificate` versions 3 and 4 (prefix through `checks` at 187..191; else `InvalidCertificate`), `Authorization` version 1 (exactly 2,669 bytes). Any later version is refused until the autopilot is updated.
